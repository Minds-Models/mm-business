The exit-first verdict

To turn the €7M-post round into a strong outcome — a €100–150M exit, ~10–16× for the seed — you need to reach €8–12,5M ARR by 2031 and be valued as a measurement currency (8–12×), not a mixed SaaS (5×). The multiple matters as much as the revenue: the same €10M ARR is a €50M exit as an enablement vendor and a €120M exit as a currency. That single fact reshapes the plan four ways: optimize for the multiple (pure-data / own-the-aggregate, not the higher-revenue personalization blend); front-load the cross-retailer moat; make revenue recurring fast; and — because the Czech pipeline alone tops out near €4–6M — bake Europe/grocery expansion and a Series A into the next 12 months, not later.

The one number that governs the exit

The same revenue is worth 2,4× more as a currency than as a mixed vendor.

€8M ARR × 5× (mixed SaaS) = €40M. €8M ARR × 12× (measurement currency) = €96M. You do not primarily grow your way to a big exit — you re-rate your way there. Everything below is in service of earning the 8–12× multiple, which means owning the cross-retailer aggregate and being recurring.

04 · How the strategy changes

Five shifts an exit-first plan forces

1
Optimize for the multiple, not revenue

Choose the pure-data / currency path over the higher-revenue personalization+data blend. €8M as a currency (€96M) beats €10,6M as a mixed vendor (€53M). Personalization stays only as deployment funding and a wedge — never the story or the revenue mix at exit.

2
Front-load the cross-retailer aggregate moat

A currency needs breadth no single retailer can replicate. Get the perpetual aggregate-rights clause into 3–4 retailer contracts fast, and stand up a cross-retailer aggregate product early. This is the asset the acquirer actually buys — and the thing that can't be rebuilt.

3
Recurring over one-off, aggressively

The multiple rewards recurring ARR and punishes project revenue. Convert paid pilots to subscriptions on the fastest clock; measure the business in ARR, not in studies delivered. A "reports" company trades at 2–4×; a currency at 8–12×.

4
Go multi-country / grocery — funded by a Series A

The €8–12M target is unreachable from Czech specialty retail alone. Bake in 2–3 European markets and a grocery-scale chain, which needs a €2–4M Series A in ~12–18 months. The €500k is the bridge that proves rights + first recurring so the A is raisable.

5
Start the acquirer relationship at year 1, not year 5

Strategic premiums go to companies the buyer has watched become a threat. Open informational relationships with NIQ/Circana/an RMN player now, be visibly the "who" layer they lack, and make yourself a buy-not-build decision by 2030. Exits are cultivated, not auctioned cold.

Exit rate at the 12-month mark: ~€0,4–0,5M recurring data ARR, 3–4 retailers under perpetual aggregate rights, a live cross-retailer aggregate, and a closed Series A. That is precisely the profile that (a) justifies the A valuation and (b) starts the acquirer clock. Miss the aggregate-rights breadth or the recurring shift, and you're on the €50M-trap curve, not the €150M curve — same effort, half the outcome.

What drops off the plate. Exit-first means saying no to revenue that doesn't build the multiple: bespoke one-off studies beyond what's needed for proof, O2-type low-data-value deployments, and personalization features that don't fund deployment. Every month spent on non-recurring, non-aggregate, non-breadth work is a month off the curve.
