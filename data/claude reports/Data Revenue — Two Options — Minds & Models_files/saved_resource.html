Minds & Models · Data business · Revenue model — rev 2

Two ways to run it: personalization + data, or data only

Same sensor deployment from the finance plan, but now assuming data rights are solved with every retailer and rollout runs as 3 chains in parallel. The question this answers: keep personalization and add data on top, or drop personalization and become a pure data-infrastructure business — and what each does to revenue.

Driver Active Screens (sensors) from finance plan Density 1,75 sensors/loc · 125 loc/retailer Data figures M&M net of ~28% retailer rev-share
The answer in one paragraph

Option 1 (personalization + data) is the bigger near-term top line: ~€10,6M M&M revenue by 2031, because you keep the €6,6M personalization line and stack ~€4M of data net on top. Option 2 (pure data) is smaller in revenue — ~€4M base, ~€7,9M if data monetizes aggressively — because you give up personalization and share ~28% of data revenue with retailers. But Option 2 is the leaner, more scalable, higher-multiple business: at measurement-currency valuations (8–15× vs 4–6× for a mixed SaaS), the aggressive pure-data case is worth more equity value at half the revenue. Option 1 maximizes revenue; Option 2 maximizes what the revenue is worth — and matches your original "in-store data infrastructure" thesis.

01 · What changed since rev 1

Three inputs, rewritten

Rights solved
Data rights signed with all retailers, no friction. The old "monetization ramp" was mostly a rights-lag; now only a short brand-sales ramp remains (30%→75%→95%→100% across 2026–29).
Pipeline-paced rollout
Concurrent rollouts are set by your actual 8-chain pipeline, not a flat number: ~2–3 in 2026 (EuroOil 50 + SuperZoo commitments), rising to ~8 in 2027–28 as Teta, COOP, Planeo & Sportisimo start, then 10+ with Europe. Every instrumented store sits in a live chain from 2027, so data revenue tracks locations directly.
Data drives value
Revenue is judged on the data line, not the personalization run-rate. Option 2 removes personalization entirely and models the business the strategy report describes — pure data relationships.
The sensor ramp ≈ your named pipeline. Instrumenting all 8 named chains at ~150-store monetization density = ~961 locations = ~1 700 sensors, which the finance model crosses during 2028. So the plan's deployment curve is the pipeline converting — no heroic assumptions. Everything past ~2028 (from ~2 800 locations in 2029 to ~4 900 in 2031) is beyond the named Czech pipeline: additional CZ chains, then Europe. At ~125–150-store density the sensor base implies ~29 monetized chains by 2030 — right at the ~25–30 organized-CZ ceiling, so Europe is required from ~2030.

Option 1

Keep personalization, add data on top

The finance-plan personalization line is untouched; data net is stacked on top of it. This is the "and" strategy — two revenue lines off one deployment, highest near-term revenue, lowest strategic risk.

  • Highest revenue, lowest risk. You never bet the company on data alone — personalization funds the sensor rollout and de-risks the raise, while data is pure upside.
  • But it's a two-product company. Two sales motions, two value propositions, harder to position as "the in-store data infrastructure." The data line is a feature bolted onto a personalization business, not the business itself.
Option 2

Pure data — drop personalization entirely

No personalization revenue. The company is only data relationships: syndicated category reads, insight studies, retail-media measurement — sold to brands, agencies and RMN players, with retailers on a data-partnership rev-share. This is the model in the strategy report.

What the pivot changes, from the strategy report

  • Deployment funding flips. Personalization prepayments funded the sensor rollout. Drop them and deployment must be funded by brand co-funding + investment (the report's model: brands finance the sensor network that produces their category read). This is the #1 cash-flow risk — the revenue chart assumes the same sensor curve still gets deployed.
  • Retailer becomes a rev-share partner, not a customer. ~28% of data revenue goes to the retailer (better than NIQ's ~300 CZK/store/mo). That haircut is why Option 2's M&M revenue is lower — but it's what secures the rights and the POS feed.
  • Leaner, more scalable, cleaner story. One product, one motion, one positioning: "the in-store consumer-data infrastructure." Easier to sell to brands/agencies/RMN, easier to expand across countries, and it's the identity that earns a data-currency valuation multiple.
  • Higher-quality revenue. Syndicated data sold to many brands off one dataset scales at near-zero marginal cost and compounds — versus personalization, which is sold per-device.

06 · How to read the choice

What each option is actually betting on

  • Option 1 bets on execution certainty. Two revenue lines, personalization funds the rollout, ~€10,6M by 2031. You almost certainly get there. The ceiling is the valuation multiple — a two-product company doesn't get the data-currency premium.
  • Option 2 bets on category creation. You give up €6,6M of safe personalization revenue and a chunk of data revenue to retailers, to become a clean data-infrastructure company. It only wins if data monetizes aggressively — but if it does, it's worth roughly 2× Option 1's equity value and matches your original market thesis.
  • The hidden dependency is deployment funding. Option 1's personalization cash is what pays for the sensors in the ground. Option 2 must replace that with brand co-funding and investment before the sensor curve — and therefore this entire data-revenue timeline — can happen. Solve that and Option 2 is viable; leave it unsolved and the pure-data curve slides right by 6–12 months.
  • They're not mutually exclusive in time. The strongest play may be Option 1 now, Option 2 later: use personalization to fund deployment and prove the data product, then drop personalization and re-rate as a pure data-currency business once rights, POS and brand demand are locked. The revenue lines above are the same sensors either way.
Caveats that bound all of this. (1) Same sensor curve is assumed for both options; in pure-data it depends on replacement funding actually landing. (2) Per-location ARR rests on one verified comparable (ÚZEI 3,7M CZK) + your PMI/Nestlé points — widen the band before quoting externally. (3) Figures are year-end run-rate, net of ~28% retailer share; recognized revenue lags. (4) The 8 named chains are monetized by ~2028 (~1 700 sensors); everything past that assumes new CZ chains then Europe from ~2030 — the CZ-only ceiling is ~29 chains. (5) Valuation multiples are illustrative, not quotes.
