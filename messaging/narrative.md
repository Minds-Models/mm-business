## Problem statement (pitch deck)

- ✅ Headline: **"E-commerce runs on behavior. / Physical retail got left behind — with receipts."**
  (accent words: *behavior*, *receipts*)
- ✅ Wedge line: **"Physical retail knows what sold — but not who buys."**
  (combo: wedge as headline, behavior line as subline — both work)
- ✅ Pillars (hedged — keeps both narratives open):
  1. **No behavioral data** — 87% of in-store analytics are collected only after payment.
  2. **No measurability** — 70% of marketers struggle to target & measure offline ads.
  3. **No way to respond** — 71% of customers expect personalized interactions — stores can't deliver.
- Hook slide (after problem): ◻ "The behavior e-commerce runs on? It walks into your store
  every day. We're the first to read it." / ◻ short variants: "Retail's richest data walks
  in the door every day. Unread." · "Every customer carries hundreds of data points.
  We're the first to use them."
- Hedging doctrine: **hedge upstream, not by mixing** — the problem is "stores don't know
  their customers / lack behavior data" (upstream of both data and personalization);
  the business-model slide is the ONLY place both monetizations appear side by side.

## Key metaphors & vocabulary

- **"behavior"** = the owned word (e-com runs on it; stores don't have it; we supply it).
- what/who wedge: *what sold* (POS, solved) × *who buys* (missing, ours).
- "Last unmeasured medium" / "measurement currency" — investor vocabulary, use in market
  & business-model slides, not cover.
- Depth claim, safe pattern: "88 attributes per shopper, zero identities" /
  "We understand every shopper — without identifying anyone."
- Privacy disarm line (small print near any depth claim): "No faces. No identities.
  Aggregated patterns only (k≥25)."
- Avoid: "personal touch"/"disconnected" (old stage-4 framing), "collect data about
  individuals", "deep visual analysis of individuals", "tracking".

## Strategy spine (context for any new material)

- Value chain: 1 collect (HIGH energy, the moat) → 2 insights (LOW) → 3 actions (LOW/MED)
  → 4 execution (HIGH, crowded). **Collect at 1, sell at 2–3, partner at 4.**
- Retailers pay in RIGHTS (mandate → study-rights → chain subscription), brands pay CASH,
  brokers = distribution/optionality only.
- Retailer-as-monetizer (dunnhumby model): their audience, their control, their majority;
  we are the intelligence layer. Trading data (margins, sell-through, elasticity) is
  firewalled from brands, forever.
- Rights sequencing rule: NEVER pre-sell a retailer's data without their knowledge —
  mandate letter first, then brand outreach. Pre-mandate brand talk = methodology only.
