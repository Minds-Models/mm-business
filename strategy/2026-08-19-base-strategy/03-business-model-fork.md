## What the fork actually is

Not a fundraising question — a re-run of decision **D1**. Option A *is* the platform SaaS line
(~€237–357k/yr on a 250-store chain ≈ €20–30k/mo against a €12k/mo burn) that was consciously
forgone when brands-only was chosen. Say that out loud in any discussion of it.

| | A — breakeven first | B — raise on the data-source thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Money | ~€150–300k ARR ceiling; camera-analytics SaaS commoditizes (a published competitor floor is $9–49/store/mo; ShopperTrak sold at ~2.3× revenue) | Only version with a VC-scale outcome; €0 supply is the capital-efficient form of "burn the round on data access" |
| Founder hours | Consumes the same ~1,000 sellable hours; builds a per-chain SaaS motion | Builds the syndication motion the €5M depends on |
| Rights position | **Worse by default** — under the EU Data Act the party who *pays* for the analytics is the statutory "User" and can restrict monetisation of the output. Contractible around, but must be explicit | Cleanest — barter makes the rights ask frictionless |

## Two structural facts that should drive the decision

1. **Under brands-only, deployment increases burn.** We carry ~€41/store/month all-in, so 250
   free stores add ~€10k/mo — nearly doubling burn — while producing €0 until a brand seat signs.
   Supply growth is a burn multiplier, not a revenue multiplier. Under any paid model, deployment
   is self-funding.
2. **Neither option clears December.** Breakeven needs ~€144k/yr for base burn alone. Even priced,
   the SuperZoo waves reach only 30–60 stores by December (≈€3–6k/mo). Breakeven arrives Feb–Mar
   at the earliest. **December is a bridge question, not a business-model question** — solve it
   with the €60–100k standby (papered by 30 Sep) so the cash clock does not pick the model.

## The optionality instrument — do this regardless of the fork

**Anchor at a price, discount to zero for rights.** Bid the platform at €99/store/mo with a €0
invoice and the rights rider named as the consideration. Effects: the retailer sees the value of
what they receive; the rights clause has a visible price tag; and **we keep the right to charge at
renewal without renegotiating the relationship.** A pure €0 bid is the single genuinely
irreversible act available in the next eight weeks — price-anchoring at zero with the anchor
account cannot be undone. This removes that irreversibility at no cost.

Second instrument: consider **cost-recovery pricing** (~€20–40/store/mo covering inference and
support). At 250 stores that is €5–10k/mo — it neutralises the burn multiplier, preserves the
"we make no money on your cameras" story, and keeps the Data Act position intact via an explicit
waiver clause. This was offered and declined at the plan checkpoint; the cash question is the
argument for revisiting it.

## The cost of holding both doors open

Two-tracking for six weeks ≈ €17k of burn plus bridge dilution at the €3.5M cap. Worth paying
against a permanent wrong turn. **Not** worth paying: running both sales motions at half intensity
through October — two founders splitting a SaaS motion and a syndication motion guarantees neither
reaches its gate. That failure mode looks like prudence until February.

Free optionality, do this week regardless: ask for the mandate now (revocable — it commits
nobody); write the pre-seed deck to carry both stories (floor *and* upside — pre-seed investors do
not punish a revenue floor); hold the seller hire until after 15 Oct, since it is the first spend
that hard-codes which motion we are building.
