# WS — Fundraise

**Owners:** Ondřej (process) · Josef (pitch)
**Objective:** money in the account before the mid-December wall, raised on a thesis that

---

## 1. The diagnosis — what the passes really mean

Feedback received across ~8 months and two attempts: *"too early"*, *"we can't see the VC case
mathematically"*, *"issue with scale"*, *"come back when the pilots convert to rollouts"*, and the
polite terminal form — *"keep us in the loop."* Underneath are four distinct objections, and they
need four distinct answers:

| What they said | What they mean | What answers it |
|---|---|---|
| "Can't see the VC case" | Your numbers do not reach a fund-returning outcome | **The re-rating argument** (§3) — the multiple, not the revenue, is the lever |
| "Issue with scale" | Per-store hardware + enterprise sales = linear and slow | **The mechanism change** (§4) — the retailer's budget decision has been removed entirely |
| "Come back with pilot conversion" | Pilots that never become rollouts are anti-proof | **The inbound RFI** — a 206-store chain asking us to run on their existing cameras |
| "Keep us in the loop" | Free option, no cost to them, no date | **The milestone contract** (§5) — make them name their gate |

**The meta-objection, which is the one the founders named:** eight months passed, the story
changed, nothing is signed. *Why won't the next eight look the same?* Everything below is built to
answer that one.

## 2. Narrative — "we changed the payer, not the product"

Neither "evolution" nor "revolution". The precise, defensible frame:

- **The thesis never moved:** physical retail does not know who its shoppers are; we read that,
  and match it to receipts.
- **What changed is who pays.** We assumed retailers would buy personalization. The pilots proved
  retailers are structurally bad buyers — ~4% margins, roughly one analyst per two or three
  categories, no budget line for shopper insight. Brands are good buyers: 14–16% margins,
  dedicated data teams, and an existing measurement budget they already spend millions on.
- **So we flipped the customer, not the technology.** The engine, the data asset and the POS
  matching are unchanged and compounding. One side of the market gives us supply for free; the
  other side pays.
- **And that is also why the speed changes** — see §4. The reason the last eight months were slow
  is that we were selling to the side of the market with no budget for us.

Say it in one line: **"We changed the payer, not the product."**

Why this beats both alternatives: pure "evolution" sounds like spin when the business model
visibly moved; pure "revolution" reads as instability to people already worried we flail. This
frame is honest, evidence-led, makes the pilots the *reason* we know rather than time lost, and
keeps the technical asset continuous.

**Never say:** "pivot", "we're changing direction", "the old model didn't work" unprompted.
**Do say:** "the pilots taught us where the money is", "same engine, different buyer",
"we spent eight months buying the answer to a question most companies guess at".

## 3. The spine — reverse-engineered from the exit

This is what answers *"we can't see the VC case"*, and it must come early in the deck.

- A five-year outcome here is a **strategic acquisition**, not an IPO. Buyers: consumer-insight
  and measurement incumbents (the "who" layer they structurally lack — buy or be disrupted),
  retailer data arms, retail-media platforms, and data/PE roll-ups.
- **The multiple is the lever, not the revenue.** The same €8M ARR is a ~€40M exit as a mixed
  vendor and a ~€96M exit as a measurement currency. You do not grow your way to the outcome —
  you **re-rate** your way there.
- Therefore the target: **€8–12.5M ARR by 2031 at a currency multiple** → a €100–150M exit →
  roughly 10–16× for money invested at today's terms. A €50M exit is the trap: a fine business,
  ~5× for the seed, below what a seed investor underwrites.
- **What earns the multiple** (and therefore what this round buys): breadth of perpetual
  aggregate rights across several retailers · recurring revenue rather than studies ·
  multi-country coverage · a live cross-retailer aggregate product.
- Which makes use-of-funds *derived from the exit* rather than asserted — the most legible
  structure an investor can be handed.

**Number reconciliation — RESOLVED (20 Aug).** The exit model and the operating plan were ~9×
apart for 2027 and ran on different economics. One reconciled external curve now exists in
**`12-numbers-external.md`** — that file is canonical for every number that leaves the building.

- **Externally pitch the base case:** €0.8–1.4M contracted ARR at the Feb-2027 checkpoint, on a
  curve reaching €10–14M by 2031 → €80–168M at currency multiples, centred on the €100–150M band.
- **Internally run** the €4.40M architecture (`04-revenue-architecture.md`); it never appears as an
  external forecast.
- **Brands-only improved the exit story:** keeping the whole brand euro instead of ~50% pulls the
  old curve forward by ~12 months and lifts the CZ-only ceiling to ~€7–10M — so European expansion
  becomes upside rather than a precondition. Use this; it directly answers "can't see the VC case".
- The **published exit artifact still shows the old numbers** and sits on an open share link with
  the cap table in it. Re-publish or restrict before diligence.

## 4. The velocity proof — the mechanism, not a promise

Do not claim to be fast. **Show that the thing which made us slow has been structurally removed**,
then let them verify it on dates.

| Was (why 8 months went slowly) | Is now |
|---|---|
| Retailer had to allocate budget → CFO → procurement → capex | **The retailer pays nothing.** No budget line means no CFO, no procurement committee |
| Every store needed our hardware, mounted and maintained | Software on cameras they already own; a chain deploys in remote waves |
| First signature was a commercial contract | First signature is a **60-day revocable mandate** — no procurement can block it. Show the actual one-pager |
| Revenue depended on retailers who have no budget for insight | Revenue comes from **brands**, buying on annual measurement budgets that lock Oct–Nov — a research line item, which does not trigger the IT/security review that adds 8–12 weeks to every camera deal |
| Outbound cold chains | **Inbound**: a 206-store chain sent us an RFI asking for exactly this model |

Then hand them the dated gates from `10-calendar-gates.md` and invite them to check. Velocity
claimed is noise; velocity *verifiable on a date* is the whole argument.

## 5. Breaking the "keep us in the loop" loop

The polite pass is a free option for them and a founder-hour sink for us. Two moves:

**(a) The milestone contract — make them name their gate.** Go back to every warm fund with one
page: *"You said too early. By 15 October we will have: [1] a signed data-rights mandate,
[2] receipt-verified parity across two retail categories, [3] the RFI outcome, [4] first brand
commitment. If we deliver those four, what specifically would you do?"*

- If they name a concrete next step → you now hold a conditional commitment with a date, and every
  update is evidence against a standard they set.
- If they cannot name one → they are not an investor for this round. Deprioritise and stop
  spending hours. **Qualifying out is the point**, not a side effect.

**(b) Change the artifact, not the pitch.** Never re-pitch the same deck to the same fund. Send one
new **evidence object** roughly every three weeks — mandate signed · parity number · RFI outcome ·
first brand LOI. Each is news rather than nagging, and "keep us in the loop" is literally an
invitation to do exactly this. Three evidence drops in six weeks *is* the velocity proof.

**(c) Solve "nobody wants to go first."** Their own words: no fund will lead. So remove "first"
from the question:
- Use an instrument that needs no lead and no price negotiation (convertible/SAFE on a cap).
- **Manufacture the lead from strategics and operator-angels** — ex-incumbent-measurement people,
  retail-media executives, FMCG data leaders, the advisor who triggered this strategy work. A
  strategic angel saying *"this is the layer the incumbents lack"* de-risks the round in a way a
  generalist fund's €200k does not.
- Highest-leverage single conversation available: ask that advisor for **more than money** — to
  anchor and lend his name. He signalled a ticket already.

## 6. Two strategies — pick at the 15 Oct gate

Both assume the standby bridge is papered by 30 Sep regardless (`03-business-model-fork.md`).

### Strategy A — "Bridge & Convert" (small, fast, instrument-led)

- **Raise:** €500–700k on a **convertible/SAFE at a cap**, not a priced equity round.
- **Why the instrument matters:** nobody has to go first, nobody has to negotiate a price, and it
  closes in weeks rather than months — which is what a three-month runway actually permits.
- **Who:** operator-angels and strategics first (they can lead at this size), existing convertible
  holders topping up, then Czech and CEE funds as followers into a round that is already moving.
- **Story:** CEE-local capital efficiency — pilots converted to rights, a real inbound chain deal,
  and a data asset compounding. Modest ask, high proof density.
- **Pros:** matches the runway; matches where the pilots are; does not require a fund to move first;
  keeps the fork open; fastest path to not dying.
- **Cons:** it is the same instrument the funds already passed on; risks confirming the
  "small ambition" read; the exit math says it only works as a bridge to the real round.
- **Success looks like:** €500–700k closed by 15 Nov, ≥2 strategic angels on the cap table, ≥3
  funds holding a written milestone contract for Q1.

### Strategy B — "Re-rate & Raise" (the seed the exit math actually requires)

- **Raise:** €1.5–2.5M seed at €8–12M post, on the measurement-currency thesis and the exit-first
  spine, positioning M&M as a European data company that happens to be based in CZ.
- **Who:** Western EU/UK data & retail-tech specialists (they price multiples correctly and know
  the incumbent-displacement story) + CEE regional funds with commerce-data theses. **These
  audiences carry no old anchor — for them this is a first impression, not a pivot.**
- **Timing reality:** a 4–6 month cycle. It **cannot** close before mid-December, so it is only
  viable behind a bridge. Open conversations in September, formal process from November, close Q1-27
  on rights + recurring + parity.
- **Pros:** matches the exit arithmetic (the €2–4M round is not optional); one process instead of
  two; escapes the local anchor and the "too early" loop entirely; attracts investors who
  underwrite re-rating rather than only ARR curves.
- **Cons:** hardest pitch with zero rights signed today; sluggish European market; higher
  dependence on the Oct–Nov artifacts landing.
- **Success looks like:** ≥3 thesis-fit funds in partner meetings by mid-December, term sheet in Q1.

### The decision rule (same 15 Oct gate as the business-model fork)

| Evidence at 15 Oct | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Mandate signed · parity ≥92% · ≥1 brand commitment · ≥2 funds named a real gate | **B** — go for the re-rating round; bridge covers the gap |
| Artifacts landed but funds still vague | **A** — close the instrument round from angels/strategics, revisit B in Q1 |
| Artifacts missed | **A, minimum viable** — bridge only, cut burn, rebuild evidence before asking anyone for anything |

**Do not run both processes at full intensity.** The same capacity arithmetic that governs sales
governs this: two founders cannot run an angel round and an institutional process simultaneously
and do either well.
