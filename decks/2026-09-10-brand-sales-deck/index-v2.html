The blind spot
Why none of it answers the three decisions

Your data is good. But all three of your sources
only start once somebody has already paid.

Which means the shopper who considered your category and walked away with nothing is missing from every one of them. Not as a gap in data quality. By design.

Sell-out
Exactly what left the store, by product, by week, by chain. The most precise outcome measure you can buy, and the number your own bonus is written on.
Blind spotIt begins at the moment of payment. Everyone who picked your product up and put it back again is missing from it, and always will be.
The loyalty card
Where a chain runs a real card programme, it genuinely links a basket to a household and follows it over time. It is the strongest who-answer on the market today.
Blind spotIt only sees people carrying the card, and only baskets that were paid for. In a forecourt or convenience trip, most shoppers do neither.
The panel
Projectable, comparable year on year, and the only instrument here that can legitimately say “the market” rather than “this store”.
Blind spotIt asks people to remember, weeks later. It was never in the store at the moment the choice was made, and your competitors buy the same one.
We do not claim the who-question is unanswered. Where a chain sells a loyalty-based read, it answers it well, for card-holders and for baskets that were paid for. This is the population that read cannot contain.
