Send the triggering event for CMS
What each option asks of you

Option 1 changes your backend.
Option 2 changes every installed screen.

Option 1 · Cloud to cloud
What changes
  • An HTTPS endpoint that receives the event.
  • A live push from your cloud to one specific player, while the shopper is still there.
  • Resolving edge_node_id to a player, and a segment to creative mapping in your CMS.
What it costs
  • Two networks in the live path. A slow or unreachable push degrades quietly to the fallback.
  • No shared latency budget. Neither side can measure the whole chain alone.
Answer this first
How fast can your cloud reach one specific player? If you already push live, this is mostly plumbing. If it is a poll or a CMS sync, that is where the effort sits.
Option 2 · On device
What changes
  • Your player accepts a local event and resolves it to a creative.
  • Your backend gets the segment to creative mapping down to devices, alongside the media.
  • Both halves handle a segment they have no creative for.
What it costs
  • Your player has to be extensible. A closed appliance will not do.
  • Every installed screen needs a player update, and again on every protocol change. Our release trains stay coupled.
In exchange
Latency stops being your problem. The trigger never leaves the device, so time to display is ours to control, measurable, and something we can commit to.

The event

One session, two events.
Two fields carry the decision, the rest is context.

A session is one shopper's engagement with a screen. It begins when someone gives the screen sustained attention and ends when they leave. It produces exactly two events: a start that carries the segment, and an end.

FieldTypeWhy it is there
segment_id requuidThe decision. The only field you need to pick a creative. Absent on session_end.
edge_node_id requuidWhich of our devices it came from. Your handle for pairing to a player.

event_id reqstringIdempotency, and correlating your logs with ours.
occurred_at reqtimestampLets you drop an event that arrived too late to be worth showing.
segment_namestringHuman readable. What you do with it is up to you.
age_bandstringA coarse band, for example 30s. Never an exact age.
genderstringA coarse estimate, useful as a secondary key if you want one.

Pairing our device to your player
Every event carries edge_node_id, and our API publishes where each node is installed. Enough for you to pair it with the right player. In Option 2 the event is already on the right device.
Session end is a signal, not a command. What the screen returns to is defined and run by LUXX, in both options. An event that arrives too late to show is the same case as a segment you have no creative for: stay on the fallback.

Send the triggering event for CMS
True in both options

What is the same either way.
Including the one detail that is expensive to find in production.

Both options need
  • A camera on every participating screen, facing the shopper. A hardware and mounting change, and an extra step at installation.
  • Two companies' software on one box. CPU, memory and thermal budget, the OS baseline, who owns updates, what the watchdog does.
  • The CMS stays yours. What plays where and when, how media reaches the device, how it is cached.
  • A segment catalogue on our API. We publish each retail customer's segments, you read them and keep your mapping in step.
  • A stable ID and a location for every edge node, so you can pair it with the right player.
Frames are processed on the device and not retained. What leaves is anonymized: no identifiable imagery, no biometric template, no re-identification. The system classifies, it does not recognize.
The detail worth stating plainly
The segment catalogue changes over time
  • Segments are derived per retail customer, from that customer's own visitor data. Not a fixed global taxonomy.
  • When a new segmentation is published, the catalogue is re-issued and segment IDs are not guaranteed to survive it.
  • We expose a catalogue version and a published-at timestamp, so your CMS can flag segments with no creative behind them.
An unknown or unmapped segment ID means "stay on the fallback", never an error. Worth agreeing that explicitly.
Built against the GDPR and the EU AI Act from the start, and we are glad to take your legal team through the detail. In-store notice obligations usually sit with the retailer. Worth agreeing early who supplies the wording.

Where this goes next

Six things to agree,
and one place we would start.

Open points
  • The exact payload, and which optional fields you actually want.
  • Transport and authentication for the handoff.
  • Session-end semantics, and the fallback behavior on your side.
  • Catalogue versioning, and what your CMS does with an unmapped segment.
  • What each side measures for latency, and what each of us will commit to.
  • Pilot scope: how many stores, for how long, and what we judge it by.
Our suggestion
Start with Option 1

It is the smaller change for both of us. Your player software, your release process and your installed screens stay as they are, and the whole integration lives in one backend on each side.

The one thing to establish first is how quickly your cloud can reach a specific player. If that path already exists, this is largely plumbing.

And it is not a one-way door
Option 2 is the answer if that check goes the other way. The event is identical in both: same fields, same meaning, only delivered somewhere else. Moving later is a change of delivery, not a redesign.
