01 · Method

Receipts matched to checkout visits by time correlation

Each receipt is matched to the shopper at the tills when it closed — the camera and register clocks are in sync, so presence peaks exactly at the closing timestamp and falls away within seconds as people pay and leave. Recognized staff are excluded before analysis; every match carries a confidence score, and all headline findings were re-checked on the high-confidence subset. Over eight weeks the match rate holds at 96%, and 72% of linked receipts carry a full visual profile of the buyer.

02 · What drives basket size

Basket value is set by what's in the cart, not who's holding it

The single clearest driver of how much a shopper spends is which brands they buy. Baskets built around branded premium food — the VAFO portfolio (Brit, Carnilove) — average 571 Kč; SuperZoo's own private labels 498 Kč; third-party accessories and generics far less. The second driver is how they shop: a shopper who took a cart spends 651 Kč, more than 1.5× the 426 Kč of someone carrying nothing — and the cart is visible on camera before a single item is scanned.

Why it matters. Two things the camera can read at a glance — is the shopper carrying a cart, and are they reaching for branded food — predict basket size far better than who the shopper demographically is. That's a live signal for staffing the branded-food aisle and for what to surface on screens to a cart-pushing shopper mid-trip.

03 · Personas × what they buy

Personas spend about the same — but buy strikingly different animals' worth of goods

The amount each persona spends is broadly similar — basket values sit within a narrow 399–457 Kč band, and the spread is not statistically robust. Where personas differ sharply is in which pet department they shop, and that difference is highly significant (composition test p < 0.001):

Read: the camera can't tell you a shopper will spend more — but it can tell you what they came for. A Suburban-Men-40s face at the door skews toward the aquarium and reptile aisles (high-margin, specialist gear); a student toward the rodent and small-pet wall. That's a merchandising and screen-targeting signal even though every persona's ticket lands near the store average.

05 · What the camera reads about spend

Visual signals that track basket value

  • Shopping tool (strongest, p = 0.0001): cart 651 Kč › basket 550 › empty-handed 426 › reusable bag 395. Intent is visible on entry.
  • Sex (p = 0.01): male-presenting shoppers average 450 Kč vs 416 for female — a modest but real gap, consistent across the window.
  • Apparent affluence runs backwards (p = 0.01): shoppers who read as premium-dressed spend less (308 Kč) than mainstream-dressed (445 Kč). In a pet store, spend tracks the pet's needs and the shopper's mission, not the owner's wardrobe — a useful correction to the intuition that "affluent-looking = bigger basket."
  • Build (p = 0.05): a weak signal — heavier-set shoppers average marginally larger baskets.

Honest null: the model's direct guess at a shopper's spending tier barely moves basket value (p = 0.05, and premium-tier actually lands below mid-range). What people visibly own predicts their pet spend far less than what they're visibly doing — carrying a cart, reaching for branded food.

06 · Scope & caveats

Read before quoting numbers

  • One store, 48 camera days (23 Jun – 9 Aug; POS begins 16 Jun but the checkout camera came online 23 Jun). Two registers, one camera covering both.
  • Matching is probabilistic. 96% of receipts link, but the busy till means several shoppers are often in view; matches carry confidence weights and every headline was re-checked on the high-confidence subset. Category cells (40–180 receipts) are directional.
  • Buyers only. The checkout camera sees people who bought; conversion questions need the sales-floor camera.
  • Privacy: de-identified aggregates; every reported figure combines ≥ 10 receipts; sensitive attributes used only in aggregate.

Method: 55 daily POS exports (store CS314, 16 Jun – 9 Aug 2026) matched to anonymous checkout visits by time correlation — a shopper is credited with a receipt when their departure aligns with the receipt closing; recognized staff excluded; every match confidence-weighted and findings re-checked on the strict subset.
