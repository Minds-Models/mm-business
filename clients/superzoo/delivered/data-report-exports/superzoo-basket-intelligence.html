A · Basket anatomy

Small, focused trips — with a long high-value tail

The typical basket is two items, 288 Kč, and stays in one department (78%) — people come for one animal's needs and leave. But the value tail is long: the top 10% of baskets exceed 964 Kč and the top 5% pass 1,350. Multi-department trips are the minority (22%) but, as §C shows, they're where the money is.

C · What builds a bigger basket

Breadth and depth, not brand — cross-department trips are the prize

Basket value rises cleanly with two things the store can influence at the shelf and the screen: how many departments a shopper touches, and how many items they pick up. A treat in the basket adds ~100 Kč on average.

Lever: a shopper who crosses from one department into a second nearly doubles their basket (383 → 606 Kč). Only 22% do. The cross-sell kits in §B are the bridge — the small-pet owner who adds bedding, the dog owner who adds a leash. Getting one more department into the trip is the single biggest basket lever in the data.

06 · Scope

Read before using

  • Descriptive + associational. §A–C are direct store facts (all 9,064 baskets). §E and the affinity lifts are correlational hooks, strongest where counts are large.
  • Small-department affinities sit on modest counts (40–90 baskets) — directionally solid, precise enough to act on as merchandising hypotheses, not guarantees.
  • Personas cover ~70% of linked baskets; §E is on that subset.
  • One store, buyers only — same scope as the rest of the pilot.

Method: 9,064 checkout baskets (store CS314, 16 Jun – 9 Aug 2026). Anatomy and value distribution over all baskets; cross-category affinity via co-occurrence lift on product types (support ≥ 40); spend drivers by basket breadth/depth; persona signatures via spend-share lift vs the store (personas on linked baskets).
