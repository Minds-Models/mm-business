01 · Method

Orders matched to checkout visits by time correlation — after removing staff

The order feed carries no customer identity, so each order is matched to the shopper standing at the tills when it closed. Two things shape the match here. First, the store runs three registers under a single checkout camera, so several shoppers are usually in view at once — the match picks the one whose departure lines up with the order, and carries a confidence score used to re-check every finding on a strict subset. Second, this location has many staff on the floor; they were filtered out before analysis using an appearance-based scan plus an automated visual check, so employee movement doesn't pollute the shopper picture.

02 · Personas × spend

The athletic core spends the most: top personas out-spend the store by 6–15%

Athletic Young Men and Men in their 30s–50s are the highest-value shoppers per visit — 566 and 554 Kč against the 491 Kč store average (Athletic Young Men significant at p = 0.02). The three women's personas run below average on ticket size (370–426 Kč) but, as the next section shows, buy a distinctly different mix — the value gap is partly a category-mix gap, not just a spending one.

04 · Product categories × who buys them

Category buyers split sharply by sex — a second, independent confirmation

Looking at it from the product side and using only the raw visual sex attribute (no persona model involved), the same story appears: some categories are strongly male, others female. Because this uses a single raw feature rather than the persona bundle, it's an independent check that the signal is real.

05 · Single features × spend

What a visitor visibly is carries spend signal

  • Sex: male-presenting shoppers average a higher order value than female (p = 0.007) — consistent with the persona ranking, visible in this one raw attribute.
  • Build & activity signal: body-composition archetype (p = 0.04) and visible activity orientation (p = 0.05) both track order value — the more athletic the visible build, the larger the basket, fitting a store whose top baskets are protein-and-creatine loads.
  • Category composition is where features speak loudest: sex alone cleanly separates creatine buyers (male) from snack and amino buyers (female), as shown above.

Honest null: the model's visual guess at spending tier (how affluent someone looks) does not predict order value here. In a fitness store, what someone buys is driven by their training goal, not their apparent budget — useful to know when deciding which visual signals deserve weight.

06 · Scope & caveats

Read before quoting numbers

  • One store, 21 trading days (23 Jun – 16 Jul), one Prague location with three registers and a single checkout camera.
  • Matching is probabilistic. The busy three-register checkout means several shoppers are usually in view, so the typical order has ~4 candidate shoppers; matches carry a confidence score and all headline findings were re-checked on the high-confidence subset. Treat category cells (10–30 orders) as leads, not settled facts.
  • Order value is whole-basket. The feed gives an order total, not per-item prices, so category analysis uses item counts, not spend — directionally sound, but a category's share of revenue can't be stated from this data.
  • Staff removed up front. This location has heavy staff presence; employees were excluded before analysis via an appearance scan plus an automated visual check, tuned to avoid mislabelling real customers as staff. Some staff inevitably remain, adding noise (which dilutes differences, never invents them).
  • Buyers only. The checkout camera sees people who bought; questions about conversion or browsers who left empty-handed need a different vantage point.
  • Overall persona spread is borderline at this sample size (p ≈ 0.07 across all seven personas); the top persona is individually significant. A few more weeks of data settles it.
  • Privacy: analysis on de-identified aggregates; every reported figure combines at least 10 orders; sensitive attributes used only in aggregate.
