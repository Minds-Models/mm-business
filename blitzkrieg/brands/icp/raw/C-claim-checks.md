# Adversarial checks of the founder's three primary-research notes

## CLAIM: "At P&G you can use AI, but you are told all the time that any mistake or bad data is on you — you personally have to be able to say why you used it in the report." (i.e. AI-assisted or novel-source numbers create personal career risk for the person who puts them in a deck.)
**VERDICT:** partly-true-overstated (confidence: medium)

**REASONING:** The literal half of the claim is confirmed almost verbatim by a named primary source: P&G's then-CIO Vittorio Cretella said of chatPG that "the accountability and the outcomes or the content remains with the employee, which is paramount," and that "the employee reviews any content or draft that is produced by generative AI" — enforced by mandatory training plus a signed acceptable-use policy before access. So the founder's interlocutor was accurately reporting real, documented P&G policy. But the founder's operative inference — that this same personal-accountability reflex is what a novel external data source like M&M runs into — conflates two different governance regimes, and that is the half that is wrong. AI-tool accountability is deliberately personalised because P&G cannot contract with a chatbot. A vendor's data is the opposite: P&G's own supplier-facing policy states that P&G "does not accept, and our contract with your company would not permit, an approach that passes potential liabilities onto P&G because of the tools you have chosen," requires suppliers to inform their P&G counterparts when AI is used in P&G work, and may impose specific due diligence — i.e. accountability is pushed *outward and downward onto the vendor*, not held personally by the buyer. The CMK precedent points the same way: P&G's online data-quality work built requirements "into our research purchasing process," demanding predetermined, replicable, standardised criteria, automated enforcement, and per-project auditable reports verifying standards were met. That is institutional cover, not personal conviction. The correct reading is therefore inverted: personal exposure is the *default state that appears when institutional cover is absent*. A mid-level person is not afraid of AI per se; they are afraid of being the only signature on a source with no procurement record, no accreditation, no methodology attestation and no reconciliation against the source of record. Behaviourally this rarely surfaces as an objection — roughly 48.8% of employees admit hiding AI use at work, so the realistic failure mode is silent non-adoption (the buyer nods, then never pastes the slide) rather than a challenge Josef can answer in the room. There is also a harder gate the claim does not anticipate at all: M&M's own product is an AI system performing biometric categorisation under the EU AI Act — age/gender inference is excluded from the Article 5 prohibitions but sits in the high-risk/Article 50 transparency band, and an ad display adapting to perceived age or gender is explicitly given as biometric categorisation even where nobody is identified. In CZ this compounds with ÚOOÚ's February 2024 camera methodology and its 2025 inspection plan naming retail-chain loyalty programmes. For a Czech buyer, "did I procure an unassessed high-risk AI system" is a far more concrete career risk than "is this number right." Finally, scope: the claim was collected at a company that M&M's own target screen has already disqualified — DECISION.md records P&G's intelligence function sitting in Bucharest for two decades and removes P&G on autonomy grounds, with drugstore/HPC assigned zero 2026 seat revenue. Seven or eight of the surviving local-pen targets (GymBeam, CBI/DanCzek, Kofola, Mattoni, JIP) are owner- or family-signed firms where a CMK-style methodology gatekeeper does not exist locally at all. A CZ jobs.cz sweep for "insights manager" returned 54 postings of which essentially one was a genuine insights role, and at a bank rather than an FMCG brand — thin evidence, but consistent with the rump-org-chart prior. So the claim is a true and useful description of the Bin B multinational archetype and is largely irrelevant to the Bin A firms that must actually produce the two paying seats by 21 November.

**REFINED STATEMENT (what Josef should actually carry into a meeting):**
The claim is accurate about AI tools and inaccurate about data vendors, and it points at the wrong companies for this quarter. Accurate half: P&G genuinely does personalise AI accountability — its CIO is on record that "the accountability and the outcomes or the content remains with the employee," and access to chatPG requires passing governance training and signing an acceptable-use policy. Since 2 February 2025, EU AI Act Article 4 has forced every EU subsidiary into an equivalent documented policy, so a Czech employee of any multinational now has a signed AI undertaking in their file. Inaccurate half: a third-party data source is not governed that way. P&G's own supplier policy pushes AI liability outward — it "does not accept... an approach that passes potential liabilities onto P&G because of the tools you have chosen," requires vendors to disclose AI use to their P&G counterpart, and may demand specific due diligence; and CMK's data-quality regime lives in the research purchasing process, demanding predetermined, replicable, standardised criteria and per-project auditable verification reports. So personal exposure is not the rule — it is the residue left when institutional cover is missing. Practical translation: a mid-level buyer is not asking "can I defend AI," they are asking "is there paper with someone else's name on it." And because roughly half of employees hide AI use rather than argue about it, the failure mode is silent non-adoption, not an objection Josef gets to answer. Two corrections of scope. First, the binding gate in CZ is not data quality but AI Act and GDPR classification: age/gender inference is biometric categorisation — excluded from the Article 5 bans but landing in the high-risk/Article 50 transparency band, with an ad display adapting to perceived age or gender cited as the textbook example — layered on ÚOOÚ's Feb-2024 camera methodology and its 2025 inspection plan naming retail chains. The buyer's real career risk is procuring an unassessed high-risk AI system, and M&M's own file already records a CZ counterparty (SuperZoo RFI §3) explicitly fearing high-risk classification. Second, this whole dynamic belongs to Bin B. Operating rule: for Bin B, sell nothing until the buyer-safety dossier exists and enter as a paid TEST rather than as a SOURCE; for Bin A, do not deploy this framing at all — it manufactures a doubt the founder-signer did not have.

## CLAIM: "People in brands want to know what exactly they should do with the data — ideally someone or something tells them." (Prescriptive beats descriptive; a dashboard/data feed loses to a recommendation.)
**VERDICT:** true-for-some-roles-only (confidence: medium)

**REASONING:** The claim is directionally right but stated at the wrong level of generality: it is a claim about ANALYST CAPACITY, not about "people in brands". The strongest evidence for it is the incumbents' own product segmentation. NIQ built Byzzer explicitly to give "recommended actions to take on price, promotion, assortment, and market expansion" so manufacturers "don't have to mine for insights", and aimed it at brands without "an army of analysts"; five years later it shipped Ask Arthur Chat (Apr 2026) for users who want answers "without requiring advanced analytics expertise", and Kantar (KaiA) and Circana (Complete Why) have built the same layer. But NIQ aimed both prescriptive products at SMB / non-analyst users, not at enterprise insights teams — which is a vendor with perfect information concluding that big-brand insights functions do NOT want to be told what to do. That is the disconfirming half, and the GRBN 2023 maturity study confirms the mechanism: asked how they relate to stakeholders, 46% of CI professionals say they "facilitate decision-making" and 40% say they "provide insights and recommendations", while only 4% say they take orders — producing the recommendation IS the insights function's identity and status. So a vendor arriving with a recommendation is complimentary to the trade wallet and threatening to the method veto, which is exactly the "insights vetoes, trade pays" doctrine already in the pack. The decisive question for M&M is therefore not global archetype but CZ/SK reality, and there the evidence is unusually clean: a jobs.cz search for "consumer insights" returns no brand-side insights role in the whole country (it returns trade marketing, brand manager, sales); "insights manager" returns 54 hits of which the only genuine insight roles are RETAILER-side (Tesco's CE Lead Insight Performance and Capability Manager — a Central Europe role, Penny, Česká spořitelna); and profesia.sk returns literally zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia. The local buyer instead looks like Magnum ICC ČR's "Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK" (75–90k CZK), one person for two countries, whose job description is literally to "turn consumer, shopper and customer data driven insights into winning commercial plans" — he is contractually the interpreter, has no analyst, and will buy the interpretation. SIMAR's 2025 numbers close the loop: the Czech research market fell 5.5% to 3.96bn CZK, which SIMAR attributes partly to "projects moving from the Czech market to the management of foreign entities" and to AI tools — local analytic capacity is not merely thin, it is being actively exported. Two caveats keep this from being a clean "confirmed". First, the strong form ("something tells them") over-reads what the market actually buys: Circana's flagship is Complete Why — a diagnostic, not a prescription engine — and the black-box objection is real, so an unexplained recommendation gets rejected on method even by people with no capacity to check it. Second, and specific to us: P&G's recorded feedback was "complementary" plus "the hurdle is the budget bucket" — appetite for prescription does not create signing authority, and the pack's own screen says only 8 of 43 legal entities hold a genuine local pen. Prescription raises willingness to pay; it does not move the signature. Finally, note the shape of the trade buyer's real want: he is not asking "what should I do", he is asking "what do I put in front of the retailer next Tuesday" — the deliverable that wins is a defensible slide he can present as his own, which is the same thing the pack already calls co-design credit.

**REFINED STATEMENT (what Josef should actually carry into a meeting):**
In a Czech or Slovak FMCG organisation there is almost no local insights analyst — jobs.cz returns no brand-side consumer-insights role in the country and profesia.sk returns zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia — so the person who will actually use our data is a trade marketing / category / key-account lead, frequently one person for CZ+SK, whose own job description already obliges him to "turn shopper data into winning commercial plans". He will not do the analysis, and he will pay materially more for an interpreted, decision-shaped deliverable than for a feed or a dashboard. But "prescriptive" has to mean a written "so what" plus the two or three moves it implies, with the method visible in the footnote and the finding presentable as HIS find — not an opaque recommendation engine. Where an insights / CMK function does exist, recommending is that person's job and status (only 4% of insights professionals describe themselves as order-takers; 86% say they facilitate decisions or provide recommendations), and in CZ that person usually sits in Warsaw, Bucharest or Vienna rather than Prague. So: prescription sells to the wallet, method sells to the veto, and one deliverable must carry both layers. Falsifiable test to run in every methodology meeting: "Who writes the 'so what' slide on your NIQ/GfK read today, and where does that person sit?" If the answer names a local person, we are selling data and should price the interpretation low; if the answer is "nobody", "the agency", or a foreign city, we are selling the interpretation and it is the majority of the price.

**IMPLICATIONS:**
- [packaging-of-deliverable] Ship a two-layer artefact, always the same shape: page 1 is the decision layer — one finding, one 'so what', two or three named moves, in the buyer's own commercial vocabulary; page 2+ is the method annex — n, sample window, k≥25 suppression, age error bands, what is measured vs modelled. Insights people read page 2 and everyone else skips it, and both behaviours are fine. Never ship the dashboard as the headline deliverable; the dashboard is proof of continuity, not the thing being bought.
- [product] Build the chat/ask-a-question layer, but scope it as an ANSWER engine over our own aggregates with citations back to the query and sample, not as a recommendation engine. NIQ (Ask Arthur Chat), Kantar (KaiA) and Circana (Complete Why) have all shipped this layer between 2024 and 2026, so its absence is now a visible gap; but Circana's flagship is deliberately diagnostic ('the why'), and an unexplained recommendation fails the method veto. Every answer must be able to show its own denominator on demand.
- [pricing] Price the interpretation as the majority of the seat, not as a bundled extra, and price the raw feed deliberately unattractively. The comparable is that NIQ sells prescription (Byzzer) as a separate product to buyers without analysts — which is precisely what a CZ/SK subsidiary is, even inside a global giant. Concretely: quarterly written read + review call carries the €40k founding seat; a data-only variant should be quoted higher per unit of value or not offered, so nobody can buy the cheap half and self-serve.
- [which-persona-to-target] Lead with trade marketing / category / KAM in every CZ/SK approach and treat insights as a mandatory second thread to be neutralised, never as the entry point. Do not spend the Blitzkrieg's remaining weeks hunting a Czech 'insights manager' — the search evidence says the role does not exist brand-side in CZ and does not exist at all in SK. Where a target's insights function is clustered in Warsaw or Bucharest, book the local trade lead first and only then ask him who has to bless the method.
- [outreach-message] The opener should promise a conclusion about their shopper, not a capability: 'your premium buyer is on average twelve years older than your campaign targets' beats 'we capture 88 attributes'. But do not promise to tell them what to do in the first line — that reads as consulting and invites the 'we have our own analytics' reflex. Promise the finding and the fact that it is receipt-verified; let the meeting reveal that we also write the so-what.
- [meeting-tactics] Add one scripted question to the standing consumption-format block (execution guide §3.4): 'Who writes the so-what slide on your NIQ/GfK read today, and where does that person sit?' Log the answer as a field alongside the Excel/dashboard/API/chat format field. It is the single cheapest test of whether we are selling data or interpretation to this account, and it also surfaces the veto-holder's location before we can be ambushed by them.
- [meeting-tactics] Offer co-design credit explicitly and early to whoever owns the incumbent data relationship — the read is presented internally as their find, with a named seat on a brand advisory micro-board. The GRBN data says 86% of insights professionals define their role as facilitating decisions or providing recommendations; handing them our recommendation as their recommendation converts the structural loser into the champion. If the brand mentions its agency, use the scripted line: 'would it help if we briefed them alongside you?'
- [paper-and-legal] Write the interpretation obligation into the founding-seat order as a deliverable with a cadence, not as goodwill: the current §Delivery line ('quarterly read — Excel + written interpretation + review call + dashboard access') should name the written interpretation FIRST and the dashboard last, so the priced object is unambiguous at renewal and at any density-escrow renegotiation. Keep the trading-data firewall and k≥25 language adjacent to it, since the recommendation layer is exactly what will attract the method challenge.
- [outreach-message] Add a missing entry to the objection bank: 'we have our own analytics/insights team.' The honest CZ answer is not to argue capability but to name capacity and scope — 'so does everyone; the question is whether that team is in Prague and whether it has receipt-matched in-aisle observation to analyse. We supply the observation your team cannot generate, and we write the first read so it lands in your language; if your team wants to redo it, we hand over the cuts.' This is currently absent from messaging/objections.md.

## CLAIM: "For some retailers, giving data to brand sales might be hard, as their job is to negotiate with them." I.e. a retailer will resist letting a supplier's sales/KAM function see shopper data because that data changes the balance of power in the annual negotiation / JBP.
**VERDICT:** partly-true-overstated (confidence: medium)

**REASONING:** The claim names a real fear but attaches it to the wrong variable. Everywhere the practice is mature, the supplier's commercial function is the *intended* customer for retailer shopper data, not the excluded one: Sainsbury's Nectar360 sells suppliers three full years of transactional and customer data for their whole category "including sales of both their own and competitor products"; Walmart's Retail Link/Luminate-Scintilla exists to give supplier account teams a "single source of truth"; the category-captain model deliberately hands one supplier category-wide competitor data to write the planogram; and the entire JBP method is built on removing the "whose data is right" argument from the buyer-KAM table. The strongest documented UK regulatory episode runs the opposite way to the claim: the Groceries Code Adjudicator raised with Tesco allegations that the Chinese walls to dunnhumby had been breached and suppliers were being pressured to *buy* dunnhumby data, with one supplier saying dunnhumby "acts as a conduit between the supplier and the buyer" — the risk regulators actually saw was the retailer weaponising data sales inside the negotiation, not the KAM weaponising data against the buyer. So the blanket form of the claim is contradicted. What survives is narrower and sharper on three axes. First, the *cut*, not the function: retailers hoard cross-retailer comparison, cost/margin, forward plans, own-label-versus-brand and range/listing inputs, and "only producers with serious negotiation leverage get detailed, real-time data access" — access is itself a negotiated privilege. Second, the *counterparty*: the retailer's objection to M&M is not "my supplier's KAM must not know this" but "why is a third party monetising my stores to my suppliers while I get software" — fear #5 in M&M's own `14-retailer-data-objection.md`, disintermediation, not negotiation. Third, and this is the genuinely new risk M&M creates that the founder's source may be groping toward: M&M's output lets a supplier independently *audit the retailer's own claims about its shopper base and its in-store execution* (who actually walks in, what converts), which strips the retailer of its monopoly on describing its own customer — a negotiation asset that needs no margin data at all. M&M's own SuperZoo file already found the higher-stakes version of this: an absent brand would use the read to negotiate *listing entry*, "arguably higher stakes for the retailer because it touches who gets on the shelf at all." On CZ specifics: Tesco ČR already runs Tesco Media & Insight with dunnhumby off ~2.6m Central-European Clubcard households and held its first Tesco Media Supplier Forum in June 2024, and Rohlík Insights and Kaufland's own platform exist — so a Czech precedent for selling supplier-facing shopper data exists, but it is media-led and thin. None of M&M's actual estates (Plaček/Super zoo, Teta, ČEPRO/EuroOil, GymBeam, Sportisimo) has a supplier-facing data business, which means nothing to cannibalise but also no policy and no muscle memory — the objection will be raised by a person improvising. Czech law does not block the sharing: Act 395/2009 as amended (effective 1 Jan 2023, transposing Directive 2019/633) regulates the *buyer's* conduct toward suppliers, not data given to them; the live legal exposure is instead the Tesco/dunnhumby shape — a retailer with significant market power brokering or pressuring supplier purchases — which matters directly because M&M's first JTI meeting is convened by ČEPRO. Net: not fatal, but it does determine which cuts are sellable, who may be named as the seller, and what has to be in the paper before the KAM meeting.

**REFINED STATEMENT (what Josef should actually carry into a meeting):**
The fear is real but it is not about the brand's sales function — it is about three specific cuts, and about who owns the pipe. Everywhere retailer data monetisation is mature, the supplier's KAM and category team are the *intended* readers: Sainsbury's sells suppliers three years of transactional plus customer data including competitor SKUs; Walmart's whole rationale is a shared "single source of truth" with its suppliers; the category-captain model hands one supplier the category; JBP exists to end the "whose data is right" argument. Even in Czechia the precedent exists — Tesco ČR sells Clubcard-backed insight to suppliers through dunnhumby. So a retailer who says "not to their sales people" is stating a reflex, not an industry norm, and the correct response is not reassurance but a cut list. What a Czech retailer can legitimately refuse, and what M&M should volunteer to prohibit before being asked, is exactly three things: (1) named-retailer × named-brand × named-competitor performance; (2) cross-chain comparison that ranks this retailer against a rival; and (3) any read usable to argue *listing or range entry* — the highest-stakes harm, which M&M's own SuperZoo analysis identified and which the current toxic-cut clause does not yet cover. Beyond those, the genuinely novel exposure M&M creates is not margin leakage at all: it is that an independent measurement of who actually walks into the store and what converts turns the supplier into an *auditor of the retailer's own claims about its shopper base and its execution*. That is the thing to put under the retailer's veto and 90-day priority, because it is the thing they will actually feel. And the live legal risk in CZ points the opposite way from the founder's claim: the Tesco/dunnhumby episode was a regulator investigating a retailer for pressuring suppliers to buy its data — so with ČEPRO convening the JTI KAM meeting, M&M must be, and must be documented as, the sole seller with no fee or benefit flowing back to the retailer from a supplier's purchase.

**DIFFERS BY ROLE:**
- Key Account Manager / National Account Manager (brand side) — claim FAILS as a blanket rule: globally and in CZ this is the primary consumer of retailer-supplied shopper data, and M&M's own plan correctly names the KAM (the ČEPRO/EuroOil account KAM at JTI) as the target. Claim HOLDS narrowly for named-retailer attributed scorecards, cross-chain benchmarks and listing/range-entry reads. In CZ this role exists locally, is a real person with a real budget line, and at Bin-A entities reports to a country commercial director who can sign.
- Trade marketing / shopper marketing — claim FAILS. This is the least threatening recipient from the retailer's point of view (spend flows back into the retailer's stores) and per M&M's own value map holds the biggest wallet. In small CZ subsidiaries this is frequently the same person as the KAM or reports into the same sales director — so the 'give it to trade marketing, not sales' compromise is often cosmetic and Josef should not offer it as if it were a real firewall.
- Category management / category development — claim FAILS most clearly. Retailers actively arm this function (category-captain model); it is the function retailers ask to be better informed. Exists locally in CZ at larger suppliers; at smaller ones it is folded into the KAM role.
- Consumer & Market Insights (P&G: CMK; Nestlé: Market Intelligence; others: Consumer Insights) — claim FAILS entirely as a data-access objection; this is the safest recipient. But there is a trap specific to CZ: in many of the Bin-B entities this role is clustered out of the country (Warsaw, Bucharest, Vienna), so 'restrict it to insights' is a routing instruction that moves the deliverable — and the approval chain — out of the Czech Republic and out of founding-seat pace. The safe recipient is often the non-local one.
- Brand / marketing manager — claim FAILS and is irrelevant; this function has no seat at the annual negotiation. Usually exists locally in CZ but with media budget influenced by an agency of record (a structural loser per the strategy pack).
- Sales director / commercial director — the retailer's genuine worry, and where the claim retains its strongest form. This is the person who would carry an M&M read into a JBP. Exists locally with signature authority at exactly the Bin-A entities M&M is targeting (CBI/DanCzek, BAT CZ, Kofola, Mattoni, VAFO, Nutrend, Big Shock).
- Country GM / registered statutory officer — signs, does not consume. Claim does not apply. Relevant only because at JTI this is the CzHuSk cluster GM, i.e. not purely Czech.
- Retailer-side (for completeness, because this is who raises the objection): the buying/commercial director and the owner raise it; the category manager and the marketing/retail-media owner are usually neutral to positive because M&M output makes their own store look measurable. At M&M's estates there is no retail-media or data-monetisation owner at all, so the objection has no natural internal champion to answer it — Josef must supply the answer unprompted.

**IMPLICATIONS:**
- [product] Ship two output tiers with a hard technical boundary, not a policy boundary. Tier 1 (masked channel norms + own-brand-only reads) is unrestricted and any brand function may hold it. Tier 2 (named-retailer attributed) requires per-cut written retailer opt-in. Then add the cut the current framework misses: any read usable to argue listing or range ENTRY — including 'absent brand' / white-space / distribution-gap reads — must sit in Tier 2, because M&M's own SuperZoo analysis established that listing entry is a higher-stakes harm to the retailer than margin on existing SKUs. Separately, treat 'audience audit' cuts (traffic composition, conversion, dwell, shopper mix versus the retailer's own media claims) as Tier 2 by default; that, not margin, is the genuinely new power M&M puts in a supplier's hands.
- [packaging-of-deliverable] Never produce a document whose title or framing is negotiation ammunition, in either direction. The strategy pack already killed the 'Destroy Your Suppliers' framing for retailers; apply the mirror rule to brands — no 'use this with your retailer' section, no retailer-comparison page, no scorecard layout. Package the seat deliverable as a shopper/category study addressed to trade marketing with an insights-signed methodology annex, and accept in your own head that the KAM will read it. Also: build the deliverable so it is forwardable to the retailer without embarrassment — the single best test is 'would I be comfortable if Plaček's buying director saw the exact PDF the JTI KAM got?'
- [paper-and-legal] Three additions before the JTI meeting. (1) A 'sole seller' clause: M&M is the sole seller of the seat, the retailer receives no fee, rebate, commission or other benefit contingent on any supplier purchasing from M&M, and the retailer's introduction confers no purchase obligation — this is the direct antidote to the Tesco/dunnhumby pattern the GCA investigated, and in CZ the mirror exposure sits under Act 395/2009 (as amended 1 Jan 2023) where the retailer, not M&M, is the regulated party. Scope note: that Act covers agricultural and food products, so it bites hardest on beverages, energy drinks and food-adjacent pet and forecourt assortment, and not on drugstore/HPC or apparel. (2) A use restriction in the brand paper: the licensee may not use the outputs to support arguments for listing or range entry at a named retailer, extending the existing toxic-cut ban from margin use to listing use. (3) Extend veto, embargo, 90-day priority and toxic-cut language in the retailer mandate to PROSPECTIVE / non-stocked brand buyers, which the current mandate does not cover.
- [meeting-tactics] Raise it yourself in minute two of every retailer meeting, and raise it as a cut list rather than a reassurance. Say: 'Your suppliers' account managers will read this — that is how Tesco already works with dunnhumby, in this country. The question is not whether they see it, it is which three cuts I never build. Here they are; cross out anything you want added.' Handing over the veto list converts an emotional objection into a procurement checklist and moves the retailer from gatekeeper to editor. Never argue that brand sales will be kept away from it — that is a promise you cannot keep and the retailer's own commercial director knows it. Conversely, in the brand meeting, never say the words 'use this in your negotiation'; say 'this is what your buyer will accept as evidence' — same value, forwardable.
- [outreach-message] Stop asking retailers for permission to sell to brands and start asking which cuts they want to approve. The outreach library's line 'Pokud tohle u vás řeší spíš kolega z insights nebo trade marketingu' is right for brand outreach; add the retailer-side mirror: an explicit one-line statement, in the first email, that outputs are k≥25 aggregates, chain-masked by default, quarterly-lagged, with a written veto — before they have time to construct the objection. And keep two strictly separate artifacts: nothing sent to a brand KAM may be a document you would not send to the retailer's buying director.
- [which-persona-to-target] Keep the KAM as the buyer — the evidence supports M&M's existing choice and the founder's source does not overturn it — but change who is named on the paper. Buyer and champion: KAM / sales director (exists locally with budget at the Bin-A entities). Named recipient of the deliverable: trade marketing (safest, biggest wallet, and the function the retailer least objects to). Methodology gate: insights / CMK / Market Intelligence — but check first whether that role sits in Prague or in Warsaw/Bucharest/Vienna, because at the Bin-B entities routing to insights hands the decision to a cluster and destroys founding-seat pace. At JTI specifically, note the budget owner is the CzHuSk cluster GM, so the 'safe' insights routing and the signature path are already partly out of the country — plan the meeting to get the KAM's local sponsorship first and the cluster approval second, not the reverse.
- [pricing] Do not offer the retailer a revenue share, a discount pool for 'their' suppliers, or any brokered-purchase economics, however tempting it looks as a way to buy the mandate. That is precisely the structure that drew the regulator to Tesco/dunnhumby, and in CZ it converts a commercial arrangement into a significant-market-power exposure for the retailer's own compliance team — which will kill the mandate faster than the data objection ever would. Price the retailer relationship in rights-for-software as designed (D1), and price the brand seat independently. If the retailer prices the strategic risk genuinely, buy the rights for cash (the inverted site-licence fallback) rather than paying them out of brand revenue.
