# cluster-3-gatekeepers

## ROLE-EXISTENCE VERDICT

SPLIT VERDICT ACROSS THE CLUSTER, and the split is the finding.

(1) INSIGHTS / CMK / MARKET INTELLIGENCE, COUNTRY LEVEL — LARGELY A MIRAGE IN THE CZECH MARKET, AND ABSENT ENTIRELY IN SLOVAKIA. Primary search, 4 Sep 2026: jobs.cz "insights" returns 30 live postings with not one brand-side FMCG consumer-insights role (the real insight roles are retailer-side — Penny Market "Performance Insights Specialist" — bank-side, betting-side and agency-side: dunnhumby Praha); jobs.cz "consumer insights" returns 10 postings, none an insights role; "shopper insights" returns three, the closest being STOCK Plzeň-Božkov's "Business Analyst – Insights & Reporting – Central Europe (CZ/SK/DE)" — i.e. even a Czech-headquartered spirits house staffs insight as a three-country cluster job; profesia.sk returns zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia. Where a multinational has the function it sits abroad (P&G Bucharest for twenty years, Colgate Warsaw, Haleon RHCS, L'Oréal's CMI posting in Warsaw, Unilever CMI at cluster level while Customer Development is physically staffed in Prague). The work itself is real; it lives inside a trade-marketing / category / business-analysis hybrid, one person often covering CZ+SK, whose own job description obliges them to turn shopper data into commercial plans. Verdict: EXISTS LOCALLY WITHOUT BUDGET, usually without the title, and with a veto only.

(2) CEE CLUSTER INSIGHTS LEAD — REAL, WITH REAL CONTRACT BUDGET, AND CLUSTERED ABROAD. Documented shape: Molson Coors' "Consumer Insight Manager CEE" covers nine markets including Czechia and Slovakia and is charged with leading CEE research strategy and managing agency partnerships; JDE posts a Junior Consumer Insights Manager C&EE in Bucharest. Czechia is 3–8% of their world. Verdict: CLUSTERED ABROAD (with Prague as an occasional seat for regional roles), buys comparability and coverage, and is structurally a 2027 buyer — do not pace at founding speed.

(3) EX-NIQ / EX-GfK / EX-KANTAR PERSON INSIDE A BRAND — REAL BUT RARE, AND THE STRATEGY PACK'S CLAIM NEEDS CORRECTING. The pack says they "convert fastest". The evidence supports only half: they convert fastest to COMPREHENSION and slowest to SIGNATURE, because they apply exactly the standards our current footprint fails (n=1 store, 4–10% brand-worn fill, no cross-visit key, run-scoped segmentation). They also mostly did not land at brands — in Czechia the ex-incumbent population sits at retailers, agencies, banks and betting firms. And the supply pool is contracting: the Czech research market fell 5.5% to 3.96bn CZK in 2025, with SIMAR naming the export of projects to foreign entities and AI as the causes. Verdict: EXISTS LOCALLY WITHOUT BUDGET, worth more as a paid methodology attester or advisory-board member than as a prospect.

(4) LEGAL / PRIVACY / DPO / CORPORATE AFFAIRS — REAL EVERYWHERE, DECISIVE IN TOBACCO, AND NEVER A BUYER. jobs.cz searches for "gdpr" and "ochrana osobních údajů" return 30 postings with none at an FMCG brand — in-house CZ legal is nought to two people, the DPO is frequently external (Czech practice expressly allows a law firm to hold the role), and anything privacy-adjacent escalates to a group function. In tobacco, Corporate & Regulatory Affairs exists locally, is senior, and per the founder's own doctrine is the real gatekeeper. Verdict: EXISTS LOCALLY WITHOUT BUDGET, with an absolute veto — and this is the persona the founder's banked P&G-derived framing does not mention at all, even though it is the one most likely to kill an M&M deal quietly.

(5) PROCUREMENT — MOSTLY CLUSTERED, AND ABSENT AT THE TARGETS THAT MUST ACTUALLY CLOSE. 106 live procurement postings in Czechia and not one is an FMCG brand's local indirect/marketing buying desk; the FMCG shape is regional (Mondelez EBSC Bratislava plus a Warsaw procurement hub; Molson Coors runs a Procurement Director CEE from Prague for nine markets, measured on "aligned annual savings"; Nestlé and Mars run global research procurement with preferred-supplier panels). Verdict: CLUSTERED ABROAD OR REGIONALLY SCOPED, holds no budget but controls vendor creation, spend classification and payment terms — and can block a purchase that has already been approved.

(6) FINANCE CONTROLLER / CFO — REAL LOCALLY, BUT ONLY A DISTINCT ACTOR AT BIN A. At owner-managed Czech and Slovak firms the controller plus the owner are the entire approval chain and can sign €40k where filed net profit supports it; at multinational subsidiaries they own the budget line and the PO but defer to regional procurement on vendor creation. Verdict: EXISTS LOCALLY WITH BUDGET MECHANICS but not with the decision, except in Bin A where it is the gate.

THE OPERATING CONSEQUENCE. Four of these six roles do not exist in Czechia as the founder's three-role doctrine imagines them, and the doctrine's own instruction — three named people per company — should become four, with legal added and insights demoted from entry point to mandatory second thread. For the seven or eight Bin A owner-signed targets that must produce the two paying seats by 21 November, this entire cluster collapses to one external lawyer and one controller, and the "we can make you safe" framing is actively counterproductive there because it invents a doubt the owner-signer did not have.

### Day in the life
Monday: refresh the NIQ/Nielsen databook and the internal sell-out file, chase the sales admin for last week's numbers. Tuesday: build the category story for a chain's category review — planogram rationale, must-stock list, a shopper slide at the front that nobody will question. Wednesday: monthly business review; explain a share point lost in modern trade and be told the answer is "promo depth". Thursday: JBP prep with the KAM, then an agency call about a tracker wave nobody has budget to extend. Friday: a promo post-evaluation in Excel, and an ad-hoc "can you pull who buys this" request from a brand manager that they answer with a two-year-old Ipsos segmentation because there is nothing else. Cadences that own their calendar: monthly business review, chain category reviews (spring and autumn windows), JBP season from October, promo cycle every 2–4 weeks, budget lock October–November.

**THINK & FEEL**

### Gains
- A finding that contradicts something the business believes about its own shopper :: it is the only kind of slide that gets remembered in a review, and it makes them the person who found it
- Their name on the methodology review of a new source :: converts the threat of an unselected vendor into a credit line, which is the cheapest promotion mechanism they have
- A written "so what" they can present as their own :: they have no analyst; a two-layer deliverable (decision page + method annex) gives them a finished argument and a defensible footnote
- A defensible answer to the retailer's "my data says different" :: receipt-verified observation is a different question, not a competing number, so it cannot be argued down
- Getting to ask hard questions and be taken seriously :: peer status, which is scarce in a role that mostly refreshes files
- One fewer ad-hoc: a standing quarterly read absorbs the recurring "who buys this" question

### Objections

- **""Co přesně to měří? Jaký je vzorek a reprezentativita?" — 'What is the sample, and how is it projected? We already buy Nielsen.'"**
  - *Really about:* Two things at once. (a) Status: they own the incumbent relationship and a source they did not bring in threatens their authority — the strategy pack's 'quiet loser (a)'. (b) Genuine competence: in a rump org they are the only person who can be blamed for a bad number, and they have no analyst to check it for them. The question is a test of whether we know what we do NOT measure.
  - *Best answer:* 'We are not a projected panel and we never will be. There is no universe, no weighting and no projection — it is a census of the people who walked past a camera in one store, and every cell is suppressed below 25 people. So it cannot tell you share and it cannot tell you the market; NIQ still does that. What it does is answer the one question retail measurement structurally cannot: who was standing in front of the shelf, and at GymBeam, which of them paid — 92% of sessions matched to receipts, 2,622 orders. Read the method annex first and tell me where it is weak; I would rather you find it than a client.' Then offer the co-design line: their name on the methodology review, the read presented internally as their find.
- **""Personas už máme. Máme segmentaci od Ipsosu/Kantaru.""**
  - *Really about:* Half-true and half-defensive. Declared segmentations in CZ are usually attitudinal, survey-built, two to four years old and not linked to any purchase. But saying so directly attacks a project they commissioned and defended internally.
  - *Best answer:* 'You almost certainly have better attitudinal segments than we could build. Ours are not a replacement — they are observed, in-aisle, per visit, and where the retailer gives receipts they are joined to a basket. The honest framing is that yours tell you who people say they are; ours tell you who walked in on a Tuesday at 4pm and what left the store with them. If your existing segments are good, the interesting exercise is to test whether they show up at the shelf at all — that is a finding either way, and a null is a publishable result for us.'
