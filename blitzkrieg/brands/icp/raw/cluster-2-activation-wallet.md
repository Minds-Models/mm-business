# cluster-2-activation-wallet

## ROLE-EXISTENCE VERDICT

Four humans, four different verdicts — and only two of them are buyers.

(1) TRADE / SHOPPER MARKETING MANAGER — EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH BUDGET. This is the only role in the CZ/SK FMCG rump org chart of which that is reliably true, and the strategy pack's "biggest wallet" claim survives the test, with two corrections. jobs.cz on 4 Sep 2026 returns ~391 postings matching "trade marketing" against 176 "brand manager", 104 "category manager" (overwhelmingly RETAILER-side buying roles), 8 matching "shopper" (about three real), and — per the banked sweep — 54 "insights manager" with essentially one genuine insights role, at a bank. profesia.sk returns 12 "trade marketing" postings in all of Slovakia and not one brand-side trade marketing manager, which is why the CZ holder so often carries both countries (Magnum ICC ČR advertises one "Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK" at 75–90k CZK/month for CZ+SK, with no analyst attached). CORRECTION 1: the role sits in the SALES line, reporting to the commercial director, not under marketing. CORRECTION 2 — and this is the honest answer to the brief's hypothesis — he has the highest WILLINGNESS in the cluster and only medium AUTHORITY. His money is largely pre-committed to the chains inside the annual listing negotiation, he has a services-procurement route (agencies, POSM, field) but frequently no data-subscription route, and at Bin B subsidiaries the wallet is local while the pen is not. Verdict: yes, he is the actual buyer of the FIRST purchase — but of a 300k CZK study out of an activation/test bucket, not of a EUR 40k seat, unless the company is owner-signed Bin A.

(2) FIELD SALES / MERCHANDISING / PERFECT STORE — LARGELY A MIRAGE AS A BRAND-SIDE BUYER IN CZ/SK, and a well-evidenced no here saves the founder weeks. jobs.cz "merchandiser" returns ~41 postings, essentially all retailer-side visual merchandising (Lindex, GAP, Inditex, Modivo) or field technicians (Allwyn); "field sales" returns 76 with no clearly brand-side FMCG store-execution role. The function has been OUTSOURCED to BTL houses — ppm factum (the largest Czech BTL agency, publicly naming Mondelez, Unilever, JDE, Henkel, Mattoni and Nestlé among partners), 4P&P, GHP CZ, A1 Team, Vieste — and the junior version is codified as a state occupational standard (NSK 66-039-N) rather than a brand career track. Where the title exists it is multi-country: a Perfect Store Manager at Mondelēz covering CZ-SK-HU is a real, publicly held Prague-area title. Decisively, ZERO of his KPIs overlap with our data: availability, facings, share of shelf, planogram compliance, price checks and out-of-stock are all things M&M structurally cannot produce, and he already has better-fitting local incumbents (DataSentics Shelf Inspector, built for Red Bull Česká republika and deployed at Globus and Albert; NIQ In-Store Vision; Asseco Mobile Touch; Trax/FORM). Model him as user, influencer and a warm path to the sales director — never as an economic buyer.

(3) BRAND / MARKETING MANAGER — EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH BUDGET, BUT NOT THE IN-STORE WALLET. Real and well-populated (Kofola alone advertising senior brand roles across Prague, Ostrava and Bratislava; Mattoni a Pepsi-portfolio Brand Manager for CZ/SK; a top-FMCG Senior Group Brand Manager at 90–140k CZK/month). POPAI CE's advertiser research is precise about the split: in FMCG companies the trade marketing manager decides in-store deployment, while brand/marketing managers dominate that decision in non-FMCG — even though brand/marketing managers are named as involved in the in-store media decision in 84% of cases. His budget is media-and-production shaped and agency-executed, and at Bin B subsidiaries the research line behind him sits in Bucharest, Warsaw or Vienna. He is the right buyer for the mid-tier "insight and personas" segment and the wrong first door at a giant.

(4) THE AGENCY OF RECORD — EXISTS LOCALLY, CONTROLS CLIENT MONEY WITHOUT OWNING IT, AND IS THREE DIFFERENT HUMANS. The Czech media market ran ~USD 1.33bn in agency billings in 2025, led by the independent Knowlimits (13.7%), then OMD, EssenceMediacom, PHD and Wavemaker, with Omnicom the largest network. The most important local fact for M&M is not on the strategy pack's radar: KNOWINSTORE, part of KNOWLIMITS Group, exclusively represents Teta drogerie's advertising inventory AND runs KNOWDATA — an in-store analytics product built with Pygmalios that tracks shoppers via WiFi, RTLS and 3D stereoscopic cameras. The nearest thing to a direct Czech competitor sits inside the country's largest media group and holds one of our named pipeline estates. Meanwhile dunnhumby has a Prague office actively hiring a Go-to-Market Propositions Lead to commercialise Tesco's in-store digital screens to media agencies, on top of Tesco Media & Insight's ~1.6m Czech Clubcard households. The agency's published position, however, cuts our way: GroupM Nexus, in SPIR's own retail-media series, states that retail-media conditions are set by the retailer inside listing negotiations, that "cenové podmínky nejsou stanoveny tržně a možnost vyhodnocování účinnosti kampaní je minimální", and demands "Transparentnost – ideálně nezávislé měření výsledků kampaně". The agency is asking out loud for what we are. Verdict: not a seat buyer in 2026; a pitch-tool buyer, a genuine route to brands, a real competitive threat, and the "quiet loser (b)" all at once. One agency master is NOT worth three direct brand seats this year — it inserts an intermediary into every relationship, it is unresolved research (R5), and agency money is pitch-shaped and lumpy. The right structure is the one already in the paper: one named agency of record on the brand's seat, back-to-back, held at one.

Cross-cutting: the P&G consumption-format datapoint ("quarterly Excel is enough; real-time is not needed; chat is wanted for insight") holds for this cluster with one correction. Quarterly is right — our own publication lag enforces it anyway. Excel is right for the trade marketer, who lives in Excel. But the primary artefact for all four is not a file, it is ONE SLIDE with the scope statement printed in its own footer, plus a method annex nobody but insights will read. And "chat" is aspiration, not availability: the only chat assistant that exists runs on a synthetic demo dataset and is scheduled, not shipped.

## PERSONA: Trade / Shopper Marketing Manager (CZ+SK)  `[trade-shopper]`
**CZ titles:** Trade Marketing Manager, Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK, Trade marketing specialista/specialistka, Manažer podpory prodeje, Vedoucí trade marketingu, Head of Trade Marketing, Shopper Marketing Manager (rare in CZ, near-absent in SK), Channel & Category Development Manager, Trade Marketing Manager / On Trade
**One line:** The one person in a Czech FMCG organisation who owns real in-store money, has no analyst, and cannot prove that any of it worked beyond the chain's own sell-out curve.
**Org position:** Reports to the sales / commercial director (in FMCG this function sits in the SALES line, not under marketing — the reporting line is the single most misread thing about this role), or in owner-managed Czech firms directly to the jednatel. Below him: usually nobody, or one trade marketing specialist/trainee and a BTL agency account team. Sits in Prague, and very frequently covers CZ+SK as one person (Magnum ICC ČR advertises exactly one 'Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK' at 75–90,000 CZK/month for both countries); at Mondelez the neighbouring Perfect Store role is already CZ-SK-HU. VERDICT: EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH BUDGET — and it is the only role in the CZ/SK FMCG rump org chart of which that is reliably true. Evidence of relative density on jobs.cz (4 Sep 2026): ~913 postings matching 'key account manager', 391 matching 'trade marketing', 176 'brand manager', 104 'category manager' (overwhelmingly retailer-side buying roles), 8 matching 'shopper' of which about three are real, and — per the banked sweep — 54 for 'insights manager' with essentially one genuine insights role, at a bank. In Slovakia the whole 'trade marketing' search on profesia.sk returns 12 postings and not one brand-side trade marketing manager, which is why the CZ holder so often carries SK as well. Caveat that must be stated in every account: the wallet is local, the SIGNATURE frequently is not — at Bin B subsidiaries the money is his and the pen belongs to the commercial director, the country GM or a cluster.
**Real in:** Genuinely real, with local money, at: Kofola (CZ+SK, owner-adjacent), Mattoni 1873, Plzeňský Prazdroj, CBI/DanCzek, Nutrend, Big Shock, Dermacol, VAFO, GymBeam (as a hybrid with the owner), Emco, AG Foods, Plaček — i.e. the Bin A local-pen list. Real but cluster-constrained at BAT CZ/SK, JTI, PMI, Coca-Cola HBC, Mondelez CZ (whose trade/Perfect Store roles are already CZ-SK-HU), Nestlé Česko, Unilever ČR (where the equivalent function is called Customer Development and is physically staffed in Prague, which is why the Unilever autonomy question in the target file is the right one). Weakest fit: Sportisimo-style private-label clusters where the decision sits in central merchandising rather than a discrete activation budget. In tobacco, the equivalent money sits with trade/category rather than brand marketing because tobacco advertising is legally constrained — the playbook's 'trade, never brand marketing' rule is correct.
**Buying roles:** economic_buyer, champion, user  |  **Budget:** He touches the trade / activation / podpora prodeje line, not a research line. Structure of his money in a CZ or CZ+SK subsidiary: (a) trade terms and listing fees paid to the chains — the largest number, but contractually committed inside the annual negotiation and not redirectable; (b) in-store activation: POSM production, secondary displays, pallet islands, coolers, sampling/hostesses, field agency retainer; (c) increasingly, retail-media spend bought from the chain. Sizing (INFERENCE, arithmetic shown, not a sourced figure): POPAI CE measures in-store at 24% of total marketing investment for the market, and CZ in-store advertising spend at roughly 10bn CZK. A CZ/SK FMCG business doing 1–3bn CZK with marketing at 3–8% of revenue implies roughly 12–36m CZK of in-store/activation money under this role's influence. So 300,000 CZK (a study) is 1–2% of that line and is normally within his own or his director's sign-off; 1,000,000 CZK (≈EUR 40k) is 3–8% of the line and, at a multinational subsidiary, escalates to the commercial director or country GM and usually to procurement, because he has a services-procurement route (agencies, POSM, field) but rarely a data/vendor-subscription route. CAN he sign EUR 40k? At an owner-managed Bin A firm where he reports to a jednatel: often yes, with the owner in the room. At a Bin B subsidiary: almost never alone — the money exists, the signature does not. Salary anchors for the role: Magnum ICC ČR Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK 75–90,000 CZK/month; Jooble's CZ average for the title 77,248 CZK/month.  |  **Confidence:** high

### Day in the life
Monday: commercial meeting with the sales director and the KAMs — last week's sell-out by chain, what the chain's report showed, what promo is running, which display did or did not get built. He arrives with the chain's own numbers because they are the only numbers he has. Tuesday: chain-facing prep — a display or promo proposal for a specific buyer, sized against the leaflet calendar (letáková akce) and the chain's promo slots; and the leaflet calendar is the real master schedule, because 58% of Czech food and drugstore spend goes through promotion and 64% for branded products. Wednesday: agency day — the BTL/field agency (ppm factum, 4P&P, GHP CZ or similar) brings photo reports from store visits, POSM production timelines, and a proposal for the next sampling wave; the POSM warehouse and the production deadline are real constraints on his week. Thursday: field ride-along or a store check with the OZ/merchandiser route, phone camera in hand. Friday: Excel. Always Excel — a promo evaluation deck for the sales director, built from the chain's sell-out plus his own spend, with a per-mechanic cost per incremental unit that he knows is not really incremental. Cadences that own his year: the annual negotiation with the chains, which the Czech trade press describes as occupying both sides from November to the end of the year; the Q4 budget lock in October–November; the chain's category review windows; the promo cycle at 2–4 week leaflet intervals; a monthly business review; and the conference calendar he actually attends — Czech Retail Days (6 Nov), POPAI DAY at O2 universum (19 Nov), Samoška in Olomouc (late January), Retail Summit (10–12 March).

### Empathy map

**SEE**
- The chain's sell-out report, arriving late, in the chain's own hierarchy, as the only external proof his activation worked
- His own Excel: promo calendar by chain, by mechanic, by week, with spend on one axis and sell-out on the other
- Photo reports from the field agency — shelves, displays, competitor placements — arriving as PDFs and phone pictures
- The leaflet (leták) calendar of every chain, which is the real master schedule of his year
- A KAM sitting between him and the buyer, owning the relationship he funds
- Digital screens appearing in stores he does not control: Tesco's 108 screens in 36 CZ stores, programmatic inventory in ~20 Albert hypermarkets, electronic shelf labels across Billa, Lidl, Albert, Penny
- POSM sitting in an agency warehouse, and a production deadline
- Competitor secondary displays he photographs on his phone and pastes into a deck
- NIQ / GfK panel decks that describe what sold, never who was standing there

**HEAR**
- From the boss: 'a o kolik nám to zvedlo sell-out?' — the only accepted proof of an activation
- From the retail buyer, inside the listing negotiation: the terms for in-store visibility, priced by the chain and not by a market — GroupM Nexus's Michaela Vejmelková writes it plainly: 'Podmínky bývají stanoveny maloobchodníkem v rámci vyjednávání o zalistování produktů... Cenové podmínky nejsou stanoveny tržně a možnost vyhodnocování účinnosti kampaní je minimální' (spir.cz)
- From the same source, the demand the market is making: 'Retailer musí umožňovat práci s daty, nabízet pokročilé analytiky kampaně' and 'Transparentnost – ideálně nezávislé měření výsledků kampaně'
- From the CZ retail-media consultant Matěj Novák: 'nejsou k dispozici data o výkonu', 'zadavatelé nemají informace o návratnosti a nemohou své investice optimalizovat', and on our exact territory 'Velkou výzvou tady bude měření v prostředí kamenných obchodů' (spir.cz)
- From the association research he half-believes and half-quotes: in-store takes 24% of all marketing investment; 67% now say evaluating that spend is 'určitě důležité' against 32% a decade earlier — but only 49% actually measure it (POPAI CE / Daniel Jesenský, zboziaprodej.cz)
- From the trade press about the market he sells into: 'Češi jako slevoví rekordmani' — 58% of food and drugstore spend on promotion, 64% for branded products (SOCR ČR / NielsenIQ)
- From the in-store industry's own leader, Pavel Kozler of Knowinstore: 'Shopper marketing není ještě stále na mediálním trhu zcela etablován' and 'Neexistuje jednotný systém pro jeho implementaci'
- From the conference stage he will sit in on 19 November: POPAI FORUM 2026's theme, verbatim — 'High-tech, Low Attention – Retail pro skutečné zákazníky'
- From incumbents' copy aimed at him: NIQ In-Store Vision promising 'complete measurement of any retail landscape — yours or your competitors', and dunnhumby promising 'closed-loop measurement' that 'connects ad exposure with real sales impact'
- From the job ad his own HR wrote for the role: turn 'consumer, shopper and customer data driven insights into winning commercial plans, growth opportunities and market execution priorities' — for two countries, with no analyst attached (Magnum ICC ČR, jobs.cz)

**SAY**
- 'Musím to obhájit před obchoďákem' — I have to defend it to the sales director
- 'Dej mi to na jeden slide' — put it on one slide
- 'Sell-out to neukázal' / 'v akci to jelo, mimo akci ne'
- 'Kolik nás stálo to vystavení a co to přineslo?'
- 'To si zalistujeme na jednání' — we'll negotiate that at the annual talks
- 'Řetězec nám na to nedá data' — the chain won't give us the data for that
- 'Jsem na to sám na Česko i Slovensko' — I cover both countries alone
- In public, on a POPAI panel: the argument that in-store deserves a bigger share of the mix — POPAI CE found 60% of advertisers think the in-store share should be higher than 24%, and 12% think it should be half the budget or more
- Writing his own job ads: 'spolupráce s Key account managery', 'příprava trade plánů a doporučení v oblasti category managementu', 'vyhodnocování efektivity marketingových aktivit' (Grafton CZ, Trade Marketing Manager – Retail)
- What he will not say to a vendor in minute one: that he has no idea whether last year's display programme worked

**DO**
- Builds and defends the annual promo/activation plan in Excel, against the chain's leaflet calendar
- Negotiates display slots, pallet islands and secondary placements inside the KAM's annual negotiation with the buyer
- Signs off POSM artwork and production runs; manages the POSM warehouse through the agency
- Briefs and rides the field agency (ppm factum, 4P&P, GHP CZ, A1 Team) and reads their photo reports
- Reads the chain's sell-out report and reconciles it against his own sell-in and spend
- Runs sampling/hostess waves and tastings, which POPAI CE research finds are the in-store format Czech shoppers accept most positively
- Buys retail media from the chain on conditions set inside the listing negotiation, without market pricing and with minimal ability to evaluate effectiveness
- Takes phone photos of competitor displays and puts them in a deck
- Attends POPAI DAY, Czech Retail Days, Samoška; enters and judges in-store work at POPAI AWARDS / Best of In-Store
- Writes the 'so what' slide himself, because there is no local analyst to write it for him

**THINK & FEEL**
- Fear: that most of the display and POSM money is waste and that one day somebody with a model will prove it — after he has signed three more years of it
- Fear, sharper: that retail media becomes the chain's new tax, priced by the chain, unmeasurable by him, and that his budget migrates into it whether or not it works
- Ambition: to be the person who brought a genuinely new argument to the annual negotiation instead of re-arguing last year's terms
- Resentment, unspoken: the KAM owns the relationship, the brand manager owns the glamour, and he owns the invoice
- Loneliness: at a CZ or CZ+SK subsidiary he is frequently one person with no analyst; the job ad says he must turn shopper data into commercial plans and no one is hired to do the turning
- Pride in test-and-learn: he changes mechanics every quarter and is comfortable with a partial answer that arrives fast — his provenance bar is materially lower than an insights function's, and it drops further when a purchase is framed as a test rather than a source of record
- Quiet career risk he will not voice: signing a camera-based vendor and being the person who procured an unassessed AI system
- What would make his year: one finding that contradicts what his own commercial team believes about their shopper, delivered in language he can repeat

### Pains
- [critical] He cannot prove an activation worked beyond a sell-out bump he did not measure and cannot audit :: POPAI CE finds 67% of Czech advertisers now consider evaluating in-store spend 'definitely important' (up from 32% a decade earlier) while only 49% actually measure it; and their information about effectiveness comes 83% from internal sources, 33% from the chains, 29% from research agencies (POPAI CE survey via zboziaprodej.cz)
- [critical] The chain sets the price and the terms of his in-store visibility inside a negotiation about listing, and he cannot evaluate what he bought :: 'Podmínky bývají stanoveny maloobchodníkem v rámci vyjednávání o zalistování produktů... Cenové podmínky nejsou stanoveny tržně a možnost vyhodnocování účinnosti kampaní je minimální' — Michaela Vejmelková, GroupM Nexus, SPIR
- [high] Promotion has eaten the category and he is the person who administers it :: 58% of Czech food and drugstore spend goes through promotion; 64% for branded products against 43% for private label (SOCR ČR / NielsenIQ, Feb 2026). He is optimising discount depth, not building brands, and he knows it
- [high] He has no analyst. The interpretation is contractually his job and nobody is hired to do it :: the Magnum ICC ČR CZSK job description obliges one person, for two countries, to 'turn consumer, shopper and customer data driven insights into winning commercial plans'; SIMAR reports the CZ research market down 5.5% to 3.96bn CZK partly because projects move 'do správy zahraničních entit'
- [high] Retail media is arriving as a new tax with no measurement attached :: 'nejsou k dispozici data o výkonu', 'zadavatelé nemají informace o návratnosti a nemohou své investice optimalizovat', and 'Velkou výzvou tady bude měření v prostředí kamenných obchodů' — Matěj Novák, CRUXO, SPIR
- [medium] Nobody has standardised anything, so every chain's in-store offer is a bespoke argument :: 'Neexistuje jednotný systém pro jeho implementaci' and 'Shopper marketing není ještě stále na mediálním trhu zcela etablován' — Pavel Kozler, Knowinstore, MAM
- [medium] The legal frame around what he pays the chain is unsettled :: the 3% ceiling on supplier payments to a chain under the significant-market-power act was struck down by the Czech Constitutional Court while the rest of the act stands, so 'what may I legitimately pay for visibility' has no clean answer
- [medium] His most important week of the year is the annual negotiation, which runs from November to the end of December, and he walks into it with the counterparty's own numbers
- [low] Status: he funds the relationship, the KAM owns it, and the brand manager gets the Effie
- [NOT SOLVED BY US — critical to say out loud] He needs incremental sales lift, availability and out-of-stock. We produce none of these. One store with no control store is not a lift study; we have no shelf compliance, no OOS, no share of shelf; and our own publication lag is designed to make brand-facing reads useless inside a live negotiation cycle — which is exactly the moment he wants them most

### Gains
- One slide he can carry into the Monday commercial meeting that nobody in the room has seen before :: status with the sales director, and the first time in years he brings a new argument rather than a defence
- A defensible reason to kill one activation format :: freed budget he can redeploy, which is how a trade marketer visibly earns his salary
- A finding that contradicts the brand team's assumption about the shopper :: internal authority; the trade function is usually the last to be believed on 'who the shopper is'
- Being credited internally as the one who brought the source in :: people promote what they co-authored; he presents the read as his find
- Not having to ask the chain for permission to know something :: independence from the buyer, which is the emotional core of the role
- A method footnote he can hand to legal without a fight :: removes the personal exposure of procuring a camera-based AI vendor
- Language for the annual negotiation that is not adversarial to the buyer :: he has to sit across from that person again in twelve months

### Goals & KPIs
- Sell-out / sell-through by chain and by mechanic (the number his bonus is actually written on)
- Numeric and weighted distribution; secondary-display count executed vs planned
- Promo ROI as his company calculates it — usually incremental units over promo cost, using the chain's sell-out as the incremental measure
- Share of shelf / podíl na regále, where it is audited
- Trade spend to plan; POSM cost per store; not overspending the activation line
- Execution compliance reported by the field agency (photo-verified display build rate)
- Increasingly: a retail-media spend number he is asked to justify without any ability to evaluate it
- Soft but real: the sales director's opinion of whether trade marketing 'brings something' to the annual negotiation

### Jobs to be done
- When I am building next year's activation plan, I want one thing about our shopper that my sales director does not already believe, so I can bring an argument to the plan instead of a defence
- When the chain prices a display slot inside the listing negotiation, I want an independent read on who actually passes that fixture, so I can argue about value instead of accepting a price I cannot evaluate
- When I have to choose between a pallet island, a cooler and a sampling wave, I want to know which one meets the shopper I am targeting, so I stop funding the format with the best photo report
- When I run a promo mechanic, I want to know who took it, so I can tell whether I discounted to my own buyer or bought a stranger
- When the sales director asks 'did it work', I want a defensible answer that is not just the chain's sell-out curve, so I stop being graded on someone else's number
- When legal asks what this camera thing is, I want a document with someone else's signature on it, so my name is not the only one on the method
- When I sit in the annual negotiation, I want language that strengthens the relationship rather than attacks the buyer, so I can use it again next year

### Trigger events
- Late September to mid-November: next year's budget lock. A brand not in conversation by the end of November is buying out of a locked budget and waits a full cycle
- November to end of December: the annual price-and-terms negotiation with the chains — Czech trade press describes it as occupying both sides from November to year-end. He is unreachable during it and highly reachable in the four weeks before it
- A chain's category review window opening, or a lost/won listing — the single best discovery question remains 'co se u vás poslední dobou změnilo?'
- A chain launching or expanding in-store retail media inventory that he is now expected to buy: Tesco's 108 screens in 36 CZ stores, Albert's programmatic inventory in ~20 hypermarkets, Teta's inventory represented exclusively by KNOWINSTORE
- A new KAM or a new buyer on the account — the relationship resets and so does the evidence base
- A failed launch or a display programme that visibly did not move sell-out; a competitor winning a permanent secondary placement he wanted
- Q4/Q1 promo peaks: Christmas, then the January reset, then Easter — each with a leaflet calendar locked weeks ahead
- Conference dates that put him in a room and in a receptive mood: Czech Retail Days 6 Nov 2026, POPAI DAY 19 Nov 2026 at O2 universum, Samoška 27–28 Jan in Olomouc, Retail Summit 10–12 Mar
- A reorganisation that folds SK into his role, or folds trade marketing and media budgets together — SPIR notes that with retail media 'došlo ke sloučení marketingových a prodejních rozpočtů'
- Effie entry season (28 Jun – 18 Aug) if his company enters in-store work; POPAI AWARDS / Best of In-Store entries in the autumn

- TRAP: 'category manager' in Czech overwhelmingly means the RETAILER's buyer/nákupčí (Albert, Globus, Teta, JIP all advertise it that way). Say 'category manager' to a brand and he will think you mean the person across the negotiating table. The brand-side equivalent is folded into trade marketing or the KAM
- TRAP: 'personalizace', 'obrazovky', 'digital signage' — these put us in the AV category and kill the data conversation; banned in our own doctrine
- TRAP: 'detekce věku' / 'ověření věku' — always 'odhad věku s intervalem spolehlivosti' (age estimation with error bands). One careless phrase ends the tobacco category

### How to reach them
Best route, by an order of magnitude: a retailer-convened introduction — the ČEPRO→JTI pattern. A CZ chain convening the supplier's commercial function is not taboo; Tesco ČR ran a Tesco Media Supplier Forum with dunnhumby in June 2024, and a chain-brokered intro converts a cold vendor into a peer in one step. Second route: the POPAI DAY / Czech Retail Days / Samoška floor, where this exact person spends three days a year and where a methodology conversation is unremarkable. Third: cold email, Tue–Thu ~07:45, from the founder's address, ≤120 words, one question CTA, no attachment on first touch, opening line about HIS world. Working opener (CZ): 'Měříme v místě prodeje u jednoho českého řetězce — kdo u regálu skutečně stojí, ne co se prodalo. ### Kill risks
- His money is pre-spent. The largest part of the trade wallet is committed to the chains inside the annual negotiation; a EUR 40k vendor line has to be found, not reallocated. Willingness is high, free cash is not.
- He buys services, not data. He has a procurement route for agencies, POSM production and field work; he frequently has no route for a data subscription, which is precisely P&G's banked 'the hurdle is the budget bucket'.
- Sell-out is his currency and we do not produce it. If he cannot connect our read to a sales number, the read is interesting and unbudgeted — the 'complementary, nice-to-have' cap, which is exactly the classification that killed the EUR 25k floor.
- The publication lag is aimed at his most valuable moment. Our own doctrine deliberately makes brand-facing reads useless in a live negotiation cycle — which is the one week of the year he would most want them.
- n=1 store is genuinely too thin for a national activation plan; a display decision is made across a chain, not in one shop.
- At Bin B subsidiaries he has the wallet and not the pen: the CZ trade lead frequently covers CZ+SK for one person, and the signature sits with the commercial director, the country GM, or a cluster.
- Camera-based procurement is a quiet veto he will not voice — the failure mode is silent non-adoption, not an objection we get to answer.
- Competitive reality inside his own market: KNOWINSTORE/KNOWDATA (Pygmalios) already sells in-store camera analytics in CZ and sits inside KNOWLIMITS, the country's largest media group, holding the Teta inventory. He may already have been pitched something that sounds identical.

### Objections

- **""Tohle mi ale neřekne, jestli se to prodalo." / "A o kolik nám to zvedlo prodej?" — 'This doesn't tell me whether it sold. How much did it lift sales?'"**
  - *Really about:* He is bonused on sell-out and on the JBP number, not on shopper understanding. He also knows that the one thing he currently CAN show his sales director is a sell-out bump from the chain's report, so any new source that does not connect to sell-out is a slide he cannot use in the Monday commercial meeting. Behind it is also a budget-code problem: 'insight' has no line in his P&L, 'activation' and 'field/agency services' do.
  - *Best answer:* Honest answer in two halves. Where the retailer has given us receipt-level POS — one store at GymBeam, 92% match, 2,622 matched orders, 1.29m CZK — we can show which shopper segment ended up in which basket, with the basket count printed on every cell. Where they have not, we measure who was in front of the fixture and how long they engaged, and we do not model the sale; we label it observation, not measurement of sales. What we never do is claim incrementality: one store with no control store is not a lift study and I will not sell it as one. If lift is the only thing that matters to you this year, buy the study as a test out of your activation budget, not a measurement seat, and judge us on whether the read changed one decision.
- **""Data z prodejen mi dává řetězec." / "Tesco nám nabízí Clubcard data, Teta má Knowinstore." — 'The chain already gives me store data; Tesco has Clubcard, Teta has Knowinstore.'"**
  - *Really about:* He is not claiming the incumbent is good — he is signalling that in-store money is already contractually committed to the retailer inside the listing negotiation, and that adding a vendor means finding new money rather than reallocating. GroupM Nexus says the quiet part publicly: retail-media conditions are 'stanoveny maloobchodníkem v rámci vyjednávání o zalistování' and 'možnost vyhodnocování účinnosti kampaní je minimální'. He is testing whether we understand that his wallet is pre-spent.
  - *Best answer:* Correct, and we are not asking for that money. Clubcard tells you what a card-holder bought; KNOWDATA tells the retailer how people moved. Neither tells you who was standing in front of your fixture — the age band with its error band, the spending tier, the group they came in with. That is the only thing we sell. And unlike the chain's own read, we are not the seller of the media we are measuring, which is exactly the 'nezávislé měření' your own agency association is asking for. Practically: this competes with a display programme or a field-agency line, not with the chain's terms.
- **""Kamery? To nám neprojde přes právní/GDPR, a s tím AI Actem už vůbec." — 'Cameras won't clear our legal/GDPR, and certainly not under the AI Act.'"**
  - *Really about:* Career risk, not conviction. He has signed an internal AI-use undertaking (EU AI Act Art. 4 has applied to every EU deployer since 2 Feb 2025) and he knows age/gender inference reads as biometric categorisation. He does not want to be the person who procured an unassessed high-risk AI system for a display programme. He will not argue this — he will nod and never mention it again, which is the real failure mode.
  - *Best answer:* Take the paper before the data. No facial recognition, no identities, no biometric templates, no cross-visit tracking — person_id is per visit and there is no re-identification code path; age is an estimate with error bands, never detection or verification; nothing leaves as anything but aggregates of 25 people or more. The deployer under the AI Act is the retailer, the provider is us, and you as a buyer of k≥25 aggregates are neither. You should not have to defend our method personally — we will sign a methodology warranty with a named person on it, and you can hand it to your legal team before you spend a koruna. If your policy requires disclosure of a vendor's AI-derived inputs, tell me now and we arrive pre-disclosed.
- **""Jeden obchod? To mi nic neřekne o trhu." — 'One store? That tells me nothing about the market.'"**
  - *Really about:* He has been trained by NIQ/GfK to think in national panels and weighted distribution. He is also protecting himself from a slide his sales director could tear apart. This is the objection that is fully correct and cannot be argued away.
  - *Best answer:* He is right, and the rule is ours before it is his: one store means this store, one chain means this channel, never the market. So do not buy a market read from us this year — nobody can sell you one honestly at this footprint. Buy the question one store can answer: does the shopper who actually engages with your fixture look like the shopper in your brief? That is a briefing-input question, not a share question, and if the answer is 'no' it is worth more than another decimal place on your panel. When density arrives, the same instrument scales; if it does not arrive, the density escrow refunds you.

## PERSONA: Field sales / merchandising / Perfect Store execution owner  `[field-perfect-store]`
**CZ titles:** Obchodní zástupce (OZ), Regionální manažer prodeje, Area Sales Manager, Field Sales Manager CZ & SK, Merchandiser, Supervizor merchandisingu, Specialista pro merchandising (a codified Czech occupational qualification, NSK 66-039-N), Perfect Store Manager (rare, and normally multi-country: CZ-SK-HU), Sales Force manager (agency-side)
**One line:** The person who proves the display was built and the shelf was full — and whose every KPI is about the shelf, not the shopper, which is why he is the sub-persona most likely to be a mirage for us.
**Org position:** Reports to the sales / commercial director, or to a regional capability lead; below him sit OZs (obchodní zástupci), merchandisers, and — much more often in Czechia — an outsourced field agency. VERDICT, and it is the most consequential verdict in this cluster: as a titled brand-side role, THIS BARELY EXISTS LOCALLY IN CZ/SK, and where it exists it is usually a multi-country hybrid WITHOUT local vendor budget. The evidence is direct: a 'Perfect Store Manager' at Mondelēz International covering CZ-SK-HU is a real, publicly held title (Prague metro area) — i.e. one person for three countries. A jobs.cz sweep for 'merchandiser' returns ~41 postings, essentially all retailer-side visual merchandising (Lindex, GAP, Inditex, Modivo) or field technicians (Allwyn), not brand-side FMCG shelf auditors. A 'field sales' sweep returns 76 postings with no clearly brand-side FMCG store-execution role. The function has not disappeared — it has been OUTSOURCED: ppm factum (the largest Czech BTL house, 20+ years, publicly naming Mondelez, Unilever, JDE, Henkel, Mattoni and Nestlé among its partners), 4P&P, GHP CZ, A1 Team, Vieste and MPP all sell 'outsourcing obchodních zástupců' and merchandising as a service. The Czech state even codifies the junior version as an occupational standard (NSK 66-039-N, Specialista/specialistka pro merchandising) rather than as a career track inside brands.
**Real in:** As a titled role, only at the larger multinationals and usually multi-country: Mondelez (the CZ-SK-HU Perfect Store Manager is a real, public, currently-held title), Coca-Cola HBC, Nestlé, Unilever, the tobacco majors, Plzeňský Prazdroj / Heineken (whose CZ 'KEY ACCOUNT MANAGER pro moderní trh' ad shows how the account and field functions merge). At GymBeam, with one flagship store, the function is the store team. So: real as a JOB, mostly unreal as a BUYER, and not real at all at the Bin A firms that must produce the two paying seats by 21 November.

### Day in the life
Monday: route planning and last week's coverage — how many stores were visited, how many photo reports came back, which displays were built and which were not. Tuesday: the agency call — the BTL account manager brings the visit report, the exception list (missing POSM, wrong facings, competitor takeover of a promo end), and an invoice discussion. Wednesday: in the field with an OZ or a merchandiser, doing store checks with a phone; if the company uses image recognition, photographing shelves into an app that returns planogram compliance and share-of-shelf in seconds — Czech deployments exist (DataSentics Shelf Inspector, built for Red Bull Česká republika and running at Globus and Albert scale). Thursday: reconciling the compliance score with the trade marketing plan — did the display we paid for actually get built, in how many stores, for how many days. Friday: the compliance dashboard, a photo appendix and an escalation list to the KAM for the stores where the chain did not execute. The cadence that owns him: the promo cycle. Every leaflet wave is a build-and-verify sprint, and Czech promo waves run every two to four weeks. The other cadence is the reset — a category review at a chain triggers a planogram change and a wave of store work.
