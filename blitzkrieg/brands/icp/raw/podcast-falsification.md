# Podcast corpus falsification pass (The FMCG Guys, 232 eps, Mar 2024 - Sep 2026)

Run 7 Sep 2026. 4 miners + 1 synthesis, 199 corpus searches. Every claim tested for REFUTATION first.

**Corpus caveat: Bin-B multinational voices. Our eight 21-Nov targets are owner-managed Czech firms.**

## SYNTHESIS HEADLINE

The most likely-wrong thing in our persona set is not any single claim — it is that the pain we sell into is, in this corpus, almost entirely voiced by vendors who sell the fix, and almost never by the brand-side buyer we have personified. I traced every articulation of "who was physically in the aisle and did they buy" and they resolve to STRATACACHE/Walkbase, Ailet, EyeSee, Barrows and mom&pop — companies selling in-store sensing. The nearest brand-side voice, Ben Reynolds' "as a brand marketer I would have killed for" that data, is a vendor recalling a former life. That is the same circularity our internal audit already caught us in, imported from a new source. Second, when the corpus does say what brands actually converge on as proof, it is not audience or identity: STRATACACHE's CEO states the one universal data point all brands agree on is "the change in case volume" — sell-out lift, not who was standing there. Third, demographic segmentation is actively disparaged by a senior voice (Wolt's Prince Charles / Prince William line) as a weak proxy versus basket data, which is a live objection to any pitch leading with inferred age or gender at the shelf. Fourth, six of our claims are simply ABSENT after hard falsification across four miners and my own follow-ups: the buyer routinely disputing supplier numbers, the category manager defending an incumbent NIQ/Kantar relationship, the agency of record blocking a measurement purchase (the phrase appears in 1 of 232 episodes), "which budget bucket does this come from" as a procurement obstacle, trade/shopper holding a lower evidence bar than insights, and trade as "the biggest discretionary budget in the building". Fifth, one claim is not merely unsupported but inverted: retailers do not withhold shopper data — they sell it aggressively and charge suppliers "massive amounts of money" for it; what stays behind the wall is raw record-level data, which is a much narrower and more honest wedge. Sixth, the strongest surviving evidence is a brand-side confession, not a vendor pitch: Keurig Dr Pepper's marketing director says he deliberately does not measure tactic-by-tactic "because if we did it's rarely going to be positive". Seventh, and most uncomfortable for the 21 November list: the one voice that speaks directly to owner-managed firms (ADM's Charlotte Bailey-Sane) says gut-feel marketing survives precisely in "privately owned businesses" and dies where there are shareholders or VC — meaning our eight Czech targets sit in the segment this corpus identifies as the least evidence-hungry, and a measurement-led pitch is aimed at the wrong motive there. Eighth, the corpus hints the European in-store measurement buyer is the retailer, not the brand: retailers are described as now specifying measurement before rolling out in-store retail media, and in Europe the retailer rather than the brand owns and funds the in-store display estate. Finally, almost none of the organisational machinery in this corpus — global and regional insights hubs, MMM programmes, clean rooms, JBP centralisation, agency-of-record politics — transfers to a Czech owner-managed firm with a short decision chain, and we should stop reasoning about our targets through it.

## WHAT IT CHANGES

### [CRITICAL] Every articulation of our core gap ("who is in the aisle, did they buy") comes from vendors selling in-store sensing — STRATACACHE/Walkbase, Ailet, EyeSee, Barrows, mom&pop. No brand-side executive in 232 episodes states it as their own felt pain. The one apparent exception (Ben Reynolds, "as a brand marketer I would have killed for") is a Walkbase VP recalling his former role.

- **Affects:** The entire pain narrative underpinning the brand ICP; the §3.1 methodology; any deck slide asserting brands are asking for this
- **Action:** Stop citing this corpus as demand-side evidence for the gap. Reclassify it as supply-side category evidence (the gap is real enough that multiple funded vendors sell into it) and mark the demand side UNVALIDATED pending primary interviews. Never let a vendor quote stand in for a buyer quote in outreach.

### [CRITICAL] The only measurement standard the corpus says all brands agree on is change in case volume. Chris Riegel (STRATACACHE CEO): "there's one kind of universal point of data capture that all brands agree with, which is the change in case volume... if a Danone, if a L'Oreal is saying I put retail media campaigns into the store and now I'm moving 10% more product... that's the true measurement they're looking for."

- **Affects:** Product framing, headline output metric, pricing narrative
- **Action:** Reframe the offer's headline output as sell-out / case-volume lift attributable to an activation, and demote audience composition to supporting colour. An audience-first framing aims at a metric brands demonstrably do not converge on.

### [HIGH] Demographic segmentation is explicitly attacked as a weak proxy. Salomon Tenenbaum (Wolt): Prince Charles and Prince William are "the exact same demographic. Obviously you're not going to target [them] the same... what's in your basket is going to really produce a lot more shopper insights than anything else."

- **Affects:** Any pitch leading with inferred age/gender at the shelf; the objection bank
- **Action:** Add verbatim to the objection bank as the primary demographic objection and pre-empt it: position inferred demographics as an in-aisle audience denominator (who had the chance to buy) rather than as a targeting segmentation competing with basket data. Never claim demographics beat basket data.

### [HIGH] Claim inverted, not merely unsupported: retailers do not withhold shopper data. Van Cauwenberge (ex-Chief Customer Officer Europe, Coca-Cola): "Massive amounts of money today are being charged for the supplier to get access to relevant shopper data from the retailer. But in many cases the actual use of the data obtained is rather limited." Nectar360 is a whole business built on selling it. What is withheld is record-level data, which stays in clean rooms (GDPR reinforced, per bol.com).

- **Affects:** The persona claim 'retailers won't give suppliers shopper data'; competitive positioning
- **Action:** Rewrite to: retailers sell aggregate shopper insight and audience segments at high prices with poor realised ROI; they do not release record-level data. That reframes our competitor as an expensive, low-yield retailer data product — stronger and defensible, unlike a fictional refusal to share.

### [HIGH] Our ICP is the segment the corpus says is least moved by evidence. Charlotte Bailey-Sane (ADM): gut-feel marketing "is going to come more in those businesses that are either challenger brands... or it's going to come through those kind of privately owned businesses. If you are backed by a venture capitalist or if you have shareholders, I don't think you're going to be doing as much of the really gut feel based marketing."

- **Affects:** The eight owner-managed Czech targets closing by 21 November; the measurement-led pitch itself
- **Action:** Do not lead with evidentiary rigour into owner-managed firms. Lead with what an owner buys — negotiating leverage against the chain, and being first in their category to hold it. Keep measurement as the delivery, not the hook. Caveat honestly: one UK voice generalising, not Czech evidence.

### [HIGH] The European in-store measurement buyer may be the retailer, not the brand. STRATACACHE describes retailers increasingly specifying measurement before rolling out in-store retail media; Iceland's retail media head notes that in the US the brand funds and manages in-store displays whereas in Europe the retailer largely owns the estate.

- **Affects:** Whether the brand ICP is the right ICP at all for in-store measurement
- **Action:** Treat as a falsification test for the brand ICP, not a settled finding. Ask directly in §3.1 who signs for in-aisle measurement in a Czech chain relationship. If the answer is the retailer, the brand persona is a co-sell influencer and the pricing ladder needs re-basing.

### [MEDIUM] Strongest surviving support is a brand-side admission of deliberate non-measurement. Richard Rodgers (Marketing Director, Keurig Dr Pepper): "Not looking at a tactic by tactic because if we did it's rarely going to be positive... we can't get the clear reporting and the KPIs that we are essentially looking for." Corroborated by Ailet's client who "has never been measuring planogram compliance because there was simply no tool for it."

- **Affects:** Outreach copy; the one claim assertable with a named brand-side source
- **Action:** Make this the lead evidence line for the whole persona set — the only place a brand-side operator says on record that per-tactic proof does not exist and that he avoids looking. Retire weaker vendor-sourced pain quotes in its favour.

### [MEDIUM] Trade/shopper does NOT have a lower evidence bar than insights. No guest makes the comparison, and insights leaders describe their own function as comfortable with directional data (Kimberly-Clark's Sharon Lewis: "it's not perfect, it's going to be directional").

- **Affects:** Persona claim 3 for the trade/shopper persona; outreach copy
- **Action:** Delete the claim — it is unfalsifiable as stated and the adjacent evidence cuts against it. Replace with the supported, narrower point: shopper/trade sits organisationally distant from the insights information flow (Greg Deacon), so it does not inherit insights' data.

### [MEDIUM] "Biggest discretionary budget in the building" is an unsupported superlative. What is supported is Troy Townsend (Zitcha): trade budgets are "multibillions... a big big big structure of money" and "we don't really know all of the things that happen in that trade realm", plus Marie-Clare Puffett confirming retail media is funded by reallocation, "not new money".

- **Affects:** Pricing narrative; budget-sizing slide
- **Action:** Soften to "large and badly instrumented, and the pool retail media is being raided from". Flag internally that our line item is zero-sum against an existing shopper budget, not new spend — which changes the objection we will meet.

### [MEDIUM] "The agency of record quietly blocks the measurement purchase" is absent, and the term is wrong for this market — "agency of record" appears in 1 of 232 episodes. What exists is inertia (REWE: "agencies stick to what they are accustomed to") and a real structural gap (Shalion's Lindberg: agencies "don't have the guard rail metric to compare against"). Two vendors explicitly position agencies as collaborative partners.

- **Affects:** Persona claim 4 for the brand/marketing persona; channel strategy
- **Action:** Drop the obstruction narrative and the term. Keep only the structural-incentive form: the agency lacks the margin/profitability guardrail to grade its own incrementality model. Do not build a channel strategy assuming the agency is an adversary.

### [MEDIUM] Three org-shaped claims are contradicted by named senior voices: country insights being hollowed out (Danone's Stammkoetter: "we're country centric... the GM running Indonesia knows Indonesia much better than I can", with STADA and BSH agreeing), local teams holding no NPD authority (PepsiCo's Patilinet on "local jewels" where the local leader owns strategy and execution; Diageo Caribbean stage-gate ideation), and procurement/budget-bucket as the decisive obstacle (never said by any FMCG buyer).

- **Affects:** Any persona asserting where decision authority sits
- **Action:** Convert all three from assumptions into per-account diagnostic questions. Note plainly that none of this machinery exists in an owner-managed Czech firm, so these claims should not be carried into the November targets at all.

### [LOW] "Personas are commoditised" is not what the corpus shows — the word appears in 10 of 232 episodes, always in passing. What it shows is stronger: consumer understanding is protected turf. Claudia Calori (VP Global Marketing, Philips): "that's definitely something that I will never outsource in terms of competence... watch out because the temptation of outsourcing is always there."

- **Affects:** Whether we lead with personas or with measurement
- **Action:** Keep the strategic conclusion (do not lead with personas) but change the stated reason: not because personas are cheap, but because arriving with them attacks the client team's self-definition and loses. Lead with what the internal team cannot produce for itself.

### [LOW] Category growth, not brand growth, is the currency with the buyer — but it is aspirational, not a mechanical gate. Van Cauwenberge: "if you are a customer you'll probably not be interested in selling more of brand A if this is a zero sum game that results in selling less of brand B." Same source: "many CPGs don't really have a true category mindset yet."

- **Affects:** The retailer-facing value proposition our brand clients would carry
- **Action:** Build the output so a brand can present it as category evidence to its buyer, not only brand evidence. This is the clearest transferable mechanism in the corpus and survives the multinational-to-Czech translation better than anything else here.

## QUESTIONS THIS CORPUS COULD NOT ANSWER (take to 3.1 meetings)

- Who actually signs for in-aisle measurement in a Czech chain relationship — the brand or the retailer? The corpus suggests European in-store display estates are retailer-owned and that retailers now specify measurement before rolling out in-store media, which would make our brand persona an influencer rather than the buyer. This is the single question most capable of invalidating the whole brand ICP and it must be asked first.
- When you last bought an end-cap, pallet or in-store activation from a Czech chain, what did you receive back as proof, and did you accept it? Ask for the actual artefact, not a description of it. The corpus establishes that per-tactic proof rarely computes but says nothing about what a Czech retailer hands over.
- Do you get store-level or SKU-level sell-out data from your chains, at what cadence, and at what price? Van Cauwenberge says European retailers charge suppliers heavily for shopper data with poor realised ROI; we have no idea whether that market exists in Czechia at all, and the answer determines whether we compete with a retailer product or fill a vacuum.
- If you could prove one number to your buyer at the next annual negotiation, what number would it be? Do not offer options. The corpus says case-volume change is the multinational answer; we need to know whether an owner-managed Czech firm says the same, says share of shelf, or says something we have not anticipated.
- Who in your company would this budget come from, and does that person also control the trade spend? Retail-media money is reallocated from shopper and trade budgets rather than added, so we need to know what we displace in a firm where those lines may not be separated at all.
- You are the owner. What would make you buy this in a single meeting, and what would make you never take a second one? The corpus explicitly says gut-feel decision-making survives in privately owned businesses, so the buying trigger for our targets may not be evidentiary — we should hear the actual motive rather than assume proof is it.
- How do you feel about a system that infers the age and gender of people at your shelf? Ask about the owner's own comfort and about GDPR exposure separately. The corpus gives one hard objection (demographics are a weak proxy versus basket data) and one regulatory flag (bol.com on GDPR limits), but no Czech read on either.
- When you present activation results internally, what does the artefact look like and who reads it? PepsiCo's line that the CMO writes the deck rather than opening the SaaS platform implies the deliverable matters as much as the data — we need to know whether an owner-managed firm wants a dashboard, a monthly one-pager, or a conversation.
- Has a Czech chain ever contested your numbers in a negotiation, and if so how was it settled? This is genuinely absent from 232 episodes despite nine-plus search phrasings across four miners. It may still be true; the corpus simply cannot tell us, so it must be validated primary.
- What did you buy in the last two years to help you sell into retailers, and why did you stop using it? A failed-purchase question surfaces the real evaluation criteria and the real churn risk far better than asking what they want.

# PER-CLAIM VERDICTS


## Brand / marketing manager and agency of record (as seen through the eyes of senior FMCG marketing, insights, and commerce executives at multinationals — Philips, Foot Locker, PepsiCo, Mercado Libre, Kimberly-Clark, and adjacent ad-tech/agency vendors — not Czech owner-managed firms)

Ran 20+ distinct query phrasings across the corpus (varying vocabulary: trackers/panels/surveys vs. observed/computer vision/RFID; CFO/MMM/econometrics; personas/segmentation/insights; agency of record/conflict of interest/in-housing), plus targeted sqlite full-passage pulls and coverage counts. Claim 2 (CFO defense via ill-fitting instruments, MMM rising) is the strongest-supported claim, with a direct, senior, on-the-nose quote from a VP Marketing at Foot Locker. Claim 3 is also well-supported but for a different mechanism than "commoditized personas" — the corpus shows large-brand insights/understanding-the-consumer is a jealously-guarded, never-outsourced internal competency (Philips VP Global Marketing), which produces the same strategic conclusion (don't lead with personas) via a different causal path. Claim 1 is overstated as stated: claimed/survey data does dominate the brand marketer's own toolkit, but genuinely observed shelf/in-store behavior exists in the corpus (computer vision, RFID/Bluetooth tracking, eye-tracking, digital shelf) — it's just not owned or generated by the brand itself, and senior operators compensate with personal store walks rather than instrumentation. Claim 4's structural-conflict half is supported (an agency literally lacks the margin/profitability metric to grade its own incrementality model; in-housing case studies show agencies losing budget and mandate when brands build internal capability) but the "quietly blocks" half is an absence — after genuinely trying six-plus phrasings, no episode shows an agency of record actively obstructing a brand's own measurement purchase; what's documented instead is inertia and gatekeeping-by-habit, and in at least two cases agencies/vendors are explicitly framed as non-conflicted enablers.

### [partly-true-overstated / high] Everything a brand marketer knows about the shopper is CLAIMED behaviour (trackers, household panels, surveys), never OBSERVED behaviour at the shelf.

**For:** Multiple senior marketers describe their default toolkit as panels/trackers/surveys: Sharon Lewis (Head of Digital & Data LatAm, Kimberly-Clark) — 'I'm a big fan of this big traditional insights and reports and consumer panels... you cannot run a consumer panel every other day'; Claudia Calori (VP Global Marketing, Philips) references 'household panel data' as the vocabulary of the discipline; Tara Loftis (Global President, L'Oréal Dermatological Skincare) — 'we survey 12,000 Sephora shoppers... How are you responding to our brand?'. Most tellingly, Ben Reynolds (VP Business Dev, Walkbase, an in-store analytics vendor) says explicitly that brand marketers have historically lacked observed data: 'the brands would love it, as we all — as a brand marketer, I would have killed for to understand which end caps were the most traffic in and most had the highest engagement.'

**Against:** Genuinely observed shelf/in-store behavior does exist and is discussed at length: Peter Vasilyev (Business Dev Director, computer-vision shelf-audit vendor) describes cameras achieving 95%+ accuracy reading shelf facings and prices; Ben Reynolds' own episode catalogs infrared/optical counters, RFID, Bluetooth cart-tracking and computer vision deployed in stores; Joris de Bruyne/Joanna Dumont (EyeSee) discuss eye-tracking and virtual-store simulation explicitly to get past 'what people say' to 'what was actually happening in the store.' Separately, senior CPG operators (The CPG Guys, hosts with FMCG backgrounds) treat physical store walks as a non-negotiable practice: 'I believe deeply, deeply, deeply that store walks should be the way of life for anybody in a commercial role in CPG' — i.e., some brand-side people DO personally observe the shelf, even if not systematically.

**Refined:** The brand marketer's OWN in-house toolkit is dominated by claimed/survey/panel instruments (household panels, brand trackers, shopper surveys) — that part holds. But 'never observed' is false: observed shelf-level behavior (computer vision, RFID/Bluetooth path tracking, eye-tracking, digital-shelf scraping) exists in the market and brands do get access to it — just not as something the brand itself generates or owns; it's mediated through retailers, retail media networks, or specialist vendors, and is patchy/fragmented rather than systematic. The gap senior marketers themselves acknowledge is not 'we have zero observed data' but 'we have no first-party, owned, continuous observed signal, so we personally walk stores and buy third-party observation piecemeal.'

### [confirmed-with-caveats / high] The brand marketer must defend the marketing budget to a CFO using instruments that were not built for it, which is why marketing mix modelling is rising.

**For:** Direct, on-the-nose, senior quote: Slavka Jancikova (VP Marketing EMEA, Foot Locker) — 'I don't know of anyone who has come up with a tool that can with certainty measure how much you're going to get back in return for investing in brand media... Trust me, I've had a lot of conversations with our lovely CFO... MMM — that's probably more relevant for brands like Coca-Cola that spent huge amounts of money... there are all these limitations.' Sorin Patilinet (PepsiCo) confirms the CFO-facing framing: marketing effectiveness has topped internal surveys 'every year' and 'will a CMO go into a SaaS platform to get his latest data or will he write the PowerPoint deck for the CFO? I think it's the latter.' The Mercado Libre CPG Guys episode: 'you go to your CFO, your CFO doesn't have more money.' MMM itself is discussed at length and treated as ascendant across an entire dedicated episode (Ammar Basit, Global Marketing Effectiveness Lead) plus later episodes (NIQ's Salim Bachatene describing an 'ensemble approach' combining econometric regression with incrementality testing as 'standard MMM type practice' as of 2026) — evidence the trend is real and continuing, not a one-off.

**Against:** MMM is explicitly described as purpose-built for exactly this budget-justification task, not an ill-fitting instrument: Ammar Basit calls it 'a scientific, credible methodology used industry-wide... tried and tested for many, many years.' So the 'not built for it' framing fits ad-hoc/vanity digital attribution and gut-feel better than it fits MMM itself, which is the tool marketers reach for precisely because it WAS built for this. Pierre-Yves Calloc'h (CDO, Pernod Ricard) also notes a real limitation: MMM only works reliably on 8-10 of a wide portfolio's brands with reasonable accuracy — a genuine methodological gap, but a different one than 'wrong tool.'

**Refined:** Confirmed: brand marketers face real, recurring pressure to justify budget to a CFO using instruments (survey-based brand trackers, vanity digital metrics, gut feel) that were not designed for financial accountability, and this pressure is a documented driver of MMM/econometric-regression adoption, now often blended with incrementality testing. Refine the framing, though: MMM itself is not an 'instrument not built for the job' — it's the credentialed answer marketers reach for, but its own real limits (only reliable at scale, only for well-funded brands, doesn't work portfolio-wide) leave a persistent, articulated gap that a mid-size or CEE brand without Coca-Cola-scale budget cannot close with MMM alone.
