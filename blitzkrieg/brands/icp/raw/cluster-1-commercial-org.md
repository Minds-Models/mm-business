# cluster-1-commercial-org

## ROLE-EXISTENCE VERDICT

FOUR ROLES, FOUR DIFFERENT ANSWERS — and only two of them are real ICP.

(1) KAM / NKAM — EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH AN ACCOUNT BUDGET, WITHOUT A NEW-VENDOR SIGNATURE. This is the densest, best-evidenced commercial role in CZ/SK FMCG. jobs.cz returns 912 live "key account manager" ads and 75+ explicitly FMCG; profesia.sk returns 192, including a live tobacco one — Imperial Brands Slovakia, Bratislava, from €3,350/month + annual bonus, whose remit is verbatim "manage and develop a portfolio of strategic key accounts", lead "ročných jednání a obchodného plánovania", and own "customer P&L, budgets and trade investments". Real people hold the title at our exact targets (Martin Vaculík, NKAM at Plzeňský Prazdroj; Pavel Císař, Head of Key Account & Strategic Development, Prazdroj; Vilém Škrabánek, International KAM, Kofola; the repo's own Martin Prokeš at CCHBC, "manažer prodeje pro čerpací stanice"). Median CZ pay ~86,402 CZK/mo after five years (platy.cz, n=704). He holds trade-investment money for HIS account — but nothing in any ad, anywhere, gives him authority to onboard a new external data vendor for CZK 1m. He is a user and a champion, never the economic buyer.

(2) Sales / Commercial Director (obchodní ředitel) — EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH THE PEN, at Bin-A entities only. jobs.cz carries a live "Obchodní ředitel/ka pro český trh | FMCG" at 140,000–180,000 CZK/month. At owner-managed Czech/Slovak firms (Kofola, Mattoni, VAFO, Nutrend, Big Shock, Dermacol, CBI/DanCzek, GymBeam, AG Foods) and at country GMs who are registered statutory officers (BAT CZ's Štěpán Michlíček, Nestlé's Lenka Škopová, Mondelez's Tomáš Kautský), this is the person who can sign €40k without the signature leaving the country. At Bin-B subsidiaries the title exists and the authority does not. This is the ONLY one of the four who is an economic buyer.

(3) Category Manager / Category Development — MOSTLY A RETAILER-SIDE TITLE IN CZ; brand-side it exists as a thin, two-country hybrid at maybe 15–25 supplier entities. A jobs.cz "category development" search returns 40 ads of which only three are supplier-side category roles; the highest-paid "Category manager" on the page is TETA drogerie at 90,000–110,000 CZK — a RETAILER, one of our own estates. Where it does exist brand-side it is explicitly regional-hybrid: Nestlé Česko's Zdeněk Sokol is "category development manager CZ/SK" (quoted in Zboží a Prodej); STOCK Plzeň-Božkov advertises "Category Management Specialist – Central Europe (CZ/SK/DE)" — one person, three countries, run out of Prague-Jinonice. Real, useful, methodologically literate, NO budget. He is the technical gatekeeper and our best internal author.

(4) The commercial / RGM analyst — LARGELY A MIRAGE IN CZ/SK BRAND-SIDE. A jobs.cz search for "revenue growth management" returns 27 ads and not one brand-side RGM role; the two closest are Tesco Stores ČR's "Price & Promo Optimization Manager" (retailer) and AB InBev's "Commercial Finance Expert – Discounts & Accruals" (a global brewer with a Prague shared-service footprint). The one genuine analytical bench sits where the Czech entity IS the regional hub: STOCK Plzeň-Božkov is simultaneously hiring "Business Analyst – Insights & Reporting – CE (CZ/SK/DE)", "Business Analyst – Data & Automation – CE" and a "Business Analyst Team Leader – CE" — a team of three-plus for three countries, in a Czech-headquartered company. Coca-Cola HBC has one "Sales Data Analyst (Route-to-Market Planner)". By contrast Magnum ICC ČR hires ONE "Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK" at 75–90k CZK to "turn consumer, shopper and customer data driven insights into winning commercial plans" for two countries with no analyst attached. Verdict: assume this persona does NOT exist at a Bin-A owner-managed target; assume he DOES exist at STOCK-shaped hub entities, at brewers, and at the tobacco majors' cluster; and when he exists, he is a verifier and a possible saboteur, never a buyer. Do not spend founder hours hunting him — but never send a deliverable that could not survive him. This mirrors the prior finding that jobs.cz returns no brand-side consumer-insights role in the whole country and profesia.sk returns zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia.

ON THE CENTRAL TENSION (KAM as user vs the toxic-cut ban and the publication lag): the KAM is a CHAMPION AND A READER, not the buyer of the thing he wants most. The cut he actually wants — his share versus his named competitor inside the named chain, this month — is the exact cut the strategy pack names as toxic and the exact cut the quarterly lag is designed to defang. That is not an accident to be worked around; it is the price of the retailer mandate that makes any of it exist. ČEPRO pointing us at the JTI KAM is an ACCESS decision, not a product spec — the playbook's own objective for that meeting is a density requirement on record, a named budget owner and a conditional LOI, explicitly "NOT a 2026 invoice". What survives the lag and the masking, and is genuinely valuable to a KAM, is everything that is (a) about HIS OWN brand in absolute terms, not relative to a competitor, (b) about the SHOPPER rather than the trading relationship, and (c) structural rather than timeliness-dependent. A demographic and behavioural profile of who converts on his SKUs does not decay in a quarter. And the lag is not only a cost: because the retailer holds 90-day insight priority and a veto, anything the KAM receives has ALREADY been seen and approved by his buyer. That converts our biggest apparent weakness into the single strongest honest claim we can make to a KAM: "this is not ammunition your buyer will resent — it is evidence your buyer has already accepted." Against a 2,000-household GfK panel and a NIQ scan read that any buyer can contest, an uncontestable, receipt-verified, buyer-approved read from the buyer's own store is a better commercial instrument than a contestable weapon.

## PERSONA: Key Account Manager / National Account Manager (brand side)  `[kam]`
**CZ titles:** Key Account Manager (KAM); National Key Account Manager (NKAM); Key Account Manager – moderní trh; Key Account Manager FMCG; Manažer prodeje pro čerpací stanice; Obchodní manažer pro klíčové zákazníky; Senior Key Account Manager; Head of Key Accounts & Strategic Development; Key Account Manager – Slovakia
**One line:** The person who sits across the table from one or two chain buyers all year, owns that account's volume, listings, promo plan and trade terms, and walks into every meeting needing an argument the buyer cannot dismiss.
**Org position:** Reports to the obchodní ředitel / sales director (CZ, or CZ+SK combined). Typically 3–8 KAMs in a CZ FMCG subsidiary, each owning 1–3 chains; the tobacco majors have one named KAM per fuel/convenience network, which is precisely the ČEPRO/EuroOil seat. At Bin-B subsidiaries the KAM may dotted-line into a CEE customer-development lead in Warsaw or Vienna. ROLE EXISTS LOCALLY, WITH A TRADE/PROMO BUDGET FOR HIS OWN ACCOUNT, BUT WITHOUT AUTHORITY TO SIGN A NEW EXTERNAL DATA VENDOR. Evidence both ways in one ad: Imperial Brands Slovakia's live KAM posting has him owning "customer P&L, budgets and trade investments" and leading one team member — real money, but account money, not procurement authority. Sometimes he manages one junior; sometimes at a small Bin-A firm he IS also the trade marketer and the category person.
**Real in:** Every named target that sells through chains or forecourts. Tobacco: JTI, Philip Morris ČR, BAT CZ/SK, Imperial Brands (a live KAM ad in Bratislava right now at €3,350+/mo). Beverages: Kofola, Mattoni, Coca-Cola HBC (Martin Prokeš's public role "manažer prodeje pro čerpací stanice" is literally this persona at a forecourt account), Plzeňský Prazdroj (uses the NKAM abbreviation), Big Shock, STOCK. Food/pet/HPC: Nestlé Česko, Mondelez CZ, Unilever ČR, VAFO, Plaček's third-party wholesale arm, Sarantis SK, Dermacol, Nutrend, AG Foods, Intersnack. At the smallest Bin-A firms (Big Shock, Nutrend) the KAM sits one step from the owner — which is the fastest path from user to signature that exists in this market.
**Buying roles:** user, champion, influencer  |  **Budget:** Touches trade marketing / trade investment for HIS account — in a CZ FMCG subsidiary this is typically the largest discretionary commercial line in the building, and the Imperial Brands SK ad puts 'budgets and trade investments' explicitly in his hands. Realistic personal discretion without escalation: roughly 50,000–500,000 CZK per decision (a placement buy, a promo mechanic, a small activation), rarely more. His own salary is 51,000–112,000 CZK/month (80% band, platy.cz, n=704; ~86,402 after five years); the SK tobacco equivalent starts at €3,350/month. CANNOT SIGN €40k. He can, and routinely does, get a CZK 150–300k study or activation approved by his director inside one cycle — which is exactly the shape of our T0 study, and is the reason to sell the study through him and the seat through his boss.  |  **Confidence:** high

### Day in the life
Monday: internal weekly commercial call — sell-out per chain versus plan, out-of-stock (OSA) list, distribution gaps, promo evaluation from the last leták. Tuesday: two hours rebuilding the same three Excel tabs he rebuilds every week — chain sell-out, NIQ scan extract, his own shipment data — because they do not reconcile and no analyst exists to make them. Wednesday: the buyer meeting. 45 minutes, a 12-slide deck, one ask (a listing, a facing, a promo slot, a price move), and the buyer opens with his own numbers. Thursday: trade-marketing planning — where the quarter's paletové místo and gondola money goes, which secondary placements to buy. Friday: field visit or a store walk, photographing planograms on his phone. Seasonally: from late August he is building the ceník for next year and the annual conditions ask; September to December is the roční jednání; from 1 January the new conditions are live and he is already behind. Category reviews land roughly twice a year, spring and autumn. He personally attends Samoška in January and, if his director pays, Retail Summit in March.

### Empathy map

**SEE**
- His buyer's screen across the table, showing the chain's own sell-out and a private-label line growing faster than his brand — private label reached 28% of Czech retail revenue in 2025 (SOCR ČR + NielsenIQ, Feb 2026).
- A promo-share number that will not go down: 58% of all Czech FMCG revenue was sold on discount in 2025, and for BRANDED products it was 64% versus 43% for private label — his category is being taught to buy on price.
- A NIQ scan extract in Excel that tells him what sold and never who bought it, and a GfK/YouGov panel read built on 2,000 Czech households standing in for 4.5 million.
- His own shelf, photographed on his phone, and a planogram that does not match the one the chain agreed.
- Competitor activity in the leták he did not know was coming.
- His account's rolling P&L, in the same spreadsheet shape his predecessor used.
- Slides from his own trade-marketing colleague that say the campaign 'ran' and never that it worked.
- LinkedIn: peers at other suppliers posting photos of secondary placements and awards.

**HEAR**
- From his director: 'Kolik jsme udělali za měsíc a kde jsme proti plánu?' — the monthly business review question, asked before anything else.
- From the buyer, the sentence that ends most meetings: 'Vaše čísla neodpovídají našim.' (Your numbers don't match ours.) The whole JBP method exists, in its own vendor literature, to remove 'the debate over whose data is right'.
- From his own JD, verbatim: 'Analyze customer performance, market trends and commercial data to identify opportunities' and lead 'ročných jednání a obchodného plánovania' (Imperial Brands Slovakia KAM ad, profesia.sk, Aug 2026).
- From a Czech agency ad for the same role: 'Péče o klíčové zákazníky v ČR… vyjednávání obchodních a cenových podmínek, realizace promo aktivit, monitoring trhu' (ACjobs, KAM FMCG, do 65 000 Kč).
- From the industry about itself: Retail Summit 2026's stated theme was 'zdravé vztahy' between retailers and suppliers built on 'vzájemný respekt, jasně nastavená pravidla a efektivní sdílení dat' — data sharing is publicly named as the missing ingredient.
- From NIQ's own coaching copy aimed at him: a category review is 'a meeting between a supplier and the retailer to go over reporting on the performance of a category', typically bi-annual, and he should arrive with 'numbers that back your brand'.
- From the retailer side of the same market: Tesco tells Czech suppliers Clubcard data 'can support a brand's strategy with Tesco' — off ~1.6m Czech Clubcard households — so he already knows a chain will sell him shopper data if the chain feels like it.
- From procurement, when he tries to buy anything: 'to musí přes centrálu' / 'to není v rozpočtu na letošek'.

**SAY**
- 'Potřebuju argument, který nákupčí nesmete ze stolu.' (I need an argument the buyer can't sweep off the table.) The single most useful sentence to be able to answer.
- 'Ukaž mi, že to roste kategorii, ne jen nás.' — the actual test any listing or facing ask has to pass; the industry's own advice to suppliers is that 'the brands that win shelf space are those who show the buyer how adding their product grows the total category'.
- 'Máme to v Nielsenovi?' — the reflex question about any claim, in one word.
- 'Kdo to u nás kupuje?' — and he currently cannot answer it about his own SKUs in a specific store.
- In his own job ads, when he writes them for a junior: 'vynikajúce komunikačné a vyjednávacie zručnosti', 'dobré analytické schopnosti', 'pokročilá znalosť práce na PC (Excel, PowerPoint)' (Sarantis Slovakia KAM FMCG ad, from €2,500/mo).
- 'Zalistování mi vezme celý rok.' — listing timelines are his standing complaint.
- 'Jedeme 64 % v akci a pořád málo.' — the promo-share fatalism, in his own numbers.
- What he will NOT say in front of the buyer: anything that sounds like he has independent data about the buyer's store.

**DO**
- Rebuilds the same reconciliation workbook weekly in Excel — chain sell-out, his shipments, a NIQ extract — and pastes the result into PowerPoint by hand.
- Prepares a 10–15 slide buyer deck per meeting: category trend, his brand versus that trend, distribution gaps, the promo plan, one ask.
- Builds the annual ceník and conditions ask from late August; negotiates September–December; signs for 1 January.
- Buys secondary placement, paletová místa, gondola ends and leták slots out of his account's trade budget.
- Walks stores and photographs planogram compliance on his phone; escalates OSA by email.
- Pulls the category or trade-marketing colleague into a meeting when the buyer asks a category-level question he cannot answer alone.
- Attends Samoška (January, Olomouc) and, where the company pays, Retail Summit (March, Prague) — largely to have corridor meetings with buyers.
- Forwards anything interesting to his director rather than acting on it alone.

**THINK & FEEL**
- Fear: losing a listing or a facing on his watch. A delisting is the one event that is visibly his fault.
- Fear he will not say aloud: that his buyer knows more about the shopper in that store than he does, and that the retailer's own private label is being armed with exactly that knowledge.
- Ambition: to be the director. In CZ/SK the KAM→obchodní ředitel path is the normal one, and the jump is roughly 86k to 140–180k CZK/month.
- Resentment: that head office measures him on share while giving him a panel of 2,000 households and a scan file, and that the answer to every request is 'ask the region'.
- Quiet suspicion of new vendors: the last three 'insight' pitches were decks he could not use in a buyer meeting.
- Genuine appetite for one contradicting fact about his own shopper — it is the only thing that makes a buyer put down their own printout.
- Anxiety about being caught with data about the retailer's store that the retailer did not sanction. He will ask about this before he asks about price.

### Pains
- [critical] He cannot answer 'who actually buys my SKUs in your store' — his incumbent read is scan data (what sold) plus a 2,000-household claimed-purchase panel, neither of which describes a shopper standing at his shelf :: GfK CZ consumer panel is 2,000 households representing 4.5m Czech households; NIQ is retail measurement, not shopper observation.
- [critical] The whose-numbers-are-right argument eats his meeting. He and the buyer arrive with different figures and spend the first fifteen minutes reconciling :: the JBP method is explicitly marketed as 'eliminating misalignment and debate over whose data is right'.
- [high] Promotion dependency he cannot escape: 64% of branded FMCG revenue in CZ sold on discount in 2025 versus 43% for private label, and private label took 28% of the market :: SOCR ČR + NielsenIQ annual analysis, Feb 2026.
- [high] No analyst. He is the analyst. The Czech commercial org has no bench — 'revenue growth management' as a search term returns no brand-side role in the country, and the comparable local hire (Magnum ICC, 75–90k CZK) is one person covering CZ+SK with a JD that already obliges him to turn shopper data into commercial plans :: jobs.cz searches, Sep 2026.
- [high] He is measured on an account whose data he does not own; the chain decides what he sees, and access is itself a negotiated privilege — 'only producers with serious negotiation leverage get detailed, real-time data access'.
- [medium] Listing and delisting cycles are slow, opaque and mostly outside his control; a category review comes round roughly twice a year and a missed one costs him half a year.
- [medium] Trade spend goes out with weak proof of return; his trade-marketing colleague can prove a campaign ran, not that it moved a shopper.
- [low] Deck production. Real, repetitive, and worth an hour a week — but nobody buys software for it at this level.
- [WE DO NOT SOLVE THIS] His single hottest pain — a defensible, current, named-competitor share read inside the named chain to use in the live negotiation — is exactly what the retailer mandate forbids and what the quarterly publication lag is designed to defang. We must say so in minute three, unprompted.
- [WE DO NOT SOLVE THIS] Repeat-purchase, loyalty and frequency inside the chain: there is no cross-visit identity anywhere in our schema and there never will be, so 'did the campaign bring the same shopper back' is structurally unanswerable, not merely unbuilt.

### Gains
- One fact about his own shopper that his buyer has never heard :: it changes the temperature of a meeting and it is remembered as HIS find, which is career currency in a company where nobody else has a fact.
- An argument the buyer cannot contest on method :: a receipt-verified read from the buyer's own store, which the buyer has already seen and vetoed on, cannot be dismissed the way a 2,000-household panel can. He stops losing the first fifteen minutes.
- A category-growth argument rather than a share-grab argument :: it is the only kind of ask that gets granted, and it makes him look like a partner rather than a supplier pushing SKUs.
- Proof his trade spend moved a shopper :: it protects his budget in the internal review and gives him a reason to ask for more.
- Being the person who brought a new source into the company :: promotion fuel, and the co-design credit doctrine says to hand him exactly this.
- Not being embarrassed :: a deliverable he can leave on the table without the buyer accusing him of espionage. He will value this more than he admits.
- Time back on the reconciliation workbook :: minor to us, real to him at an hour a week.

### Goals & KPIs
- Net revenue / net sales value on his account versus plan, monthly and annually.
- Volume and value share within the account (and this is the KPI our product legitimately cannot serve).
- Numeric and weighted distribution; number of active listings; new-listing wins per review cycle.
- Share of shelf / facings won versus agreed planogram, and planogram compliance in store.
- Promo plan delivery: number of leták slots, secondary placements, and promo uplift versus baseline.
- Trade terms and trade spend as a percentage of gross sales for the account; customer P&L contribution (explicitly named in the Imperial Brands SK KAM ad).
- OSA / out-of-stock rate on his SKUs in that chain.
- Annual bonus tied to the above — 'atraktívny kvartálny bonus' (Sarantis SK) or 'annual bonus scheme' (Imperial Brands SK).

### Jobs to be done
- When I walk into the annual negotiation, I want one fact about this chain's shoppers the buyer has not seen, so I can stop the meeting being a price conversation.
- When I ask for a facing or a new listing, I want evidence that my SKU grows the total category, so the buyer has a reason to say yes that survives his own boss.
- When I propose a secondary placement or an adjacency, I want a basket number attached, so the ask is about the chain's basket value rather than my volume.
- When I have spent trade money, I want to show it moved a shopper and not just that it ran, so I keep the budget next year.
- When my director asks why share moved, I want a shopper-side explanation and not just a price-index chart, so I look like I understand my account.
- When the buyer challenges my numbers, I want a source he has already approved, so we skip the reconciliation argument.
- When a new buyer is appointed at the chain, I want something to open the relationship with that is not a discount.

### Trigger events
- Late August to October: he builds and sends next year's ceník to the chains — Czech retailers publicly report receiving supplier price lists for the following year in autumn. This is when he is actively hunting arguments.
- September to December: the roční jednání itself; new commercial conditions signed for 1 January. First contact must land before this closes.
- October to November: his own company's budget lock for the following year — a brand not in conversation by then buys out of a locked budget.
- January: SOCR ČR + NielsenIQ publish the annual Czech retail analysis (promo share, private-label share); Samoška congress, 27–28 January, Olomouc. Both reset the internal conversation.
- March: Retail Summit, Prague (10–12 March 2026, theme 'zdravé vztahy' and 'efektivní sdílení dat') — corridor meetings with buyers.
- Spring and autumn: category review windows, roughly bi-annual per NIQ's own description.
- Anytime, and the strongest triggers of all: a delisting notice, a lost planogram slot, a competitor's surprise listing, a NEW BUYER appointed at his chain, a failed launch, or his account missing plan two months running. Ask 'co se u vás poslední dobou změnilo?' in every call.
- Tobacco-specific: any step-change in the nicotine-pouch / heated-tobacco transition, which is where the category's growth, budget and internal attention sit — open there, never with 'shopper analytics'.
- November: Czech Retail Days (5–6 Nov) and POPAI CE Awards (19 Nov) — late-season networking, and the last useful first-meeting week of the year.

### How to reach them
Never cold, if it can be avoided — the ČEPRO/EuroOil route (retailer convenes, we are introduced) is worth ten cold emails, and the repo already has a verbal greenlight for exactly that on the tobacco track. Where cold is the only option: founder address, Tue–Thu around 07:45, ≤120 words, no attachment, one question-CTA, subject in Czech 'data o nakupujících v obchodě — dotaz na metodiku'. First line about HIS world, not ours. The opener that converts is a counterintuitive fact plus a request for his opinion, not a pitch: 'V jedné české prodejně jsme přes pokladní účtenky ověřili, kdo u regálu skutečně nakupuje — vyšlo nám něco, co odporuje tomu, co se o té kategorii říká. Nechci vám nic prodávat, chci 20 minut na váš názor na metodu.' Then the routing P.S., which is the real work of the email: 'Pokud tohle u vás řeší spíš kolega z category nebo trade marketingu, komu to mám přeposlat?' Ask for 20 minutes, never 'a meeting'. Do not quote a price; if asked, 'to závisí na pokrytí, které byste potřebovali'. Ask the autonomy question only at the END: 'Kdyby si firma chtěla objednat českou studii o nakupujících, řádově milion korun, byl by to váš podpis, nebo by to muselo přes region?'

### Kill risks
- He is not the economic buyer and there is no evidence anywhere that he can be. Every hour spent selling him the seat rather than using him to reach the director is an hour lost against a 50-hour-per-account budget and only eight slots.
- The cut he wants most is the cut we will never build. If the relationship is founded on an implied promise of competitive intelligence, it breaks the moment the paper arrives — and it breaks with the retailer too.
- He may be structurally forbidden from receiving it: a retailer improvising a 'no' — and none of Plaček, Teta, ČEPRO, GymBeam or Sportisimo has any supplier-facing data policy or precedent to fall back on — is most likely to draw the line at the supplier's sales function specifically.
- At n=1 store, everything he receives is 'this store', never 'the chain'. For a KAM whose account IS the chain, that gap may make the read commercially uninteresting no matter how good it is.
- In tobacco he is inside a compliance regime where one careless phrase about age ends the category — and he knows it, so he will be more conservative than we expect.
- If he is at a Bin-B subsidiary, his enthusiasm routes the decision to Warsaw or Bucharest and destroys founding-seat pace. The Crozier rule holds: never pace a Bin-B account as founding-speed.

### Objections

- **"'Fajn — a můžete mi dát můj podíl proti [konkurentovi] v tomhle řetězci? To je to jediné, co potřebuju do vyjednávání.'"**
  - *Really about:* His bonus is share within that account. He is asking the only question his KPI sheet asks. He is also testing, half-consciously, whether we are the kind of vendor who over-promises — because he has been burned by decks he could not use in a buyer meeting.
  - *Best answer:* 'Ne, a nikdy. Ten řez — jmenovaný řetězec × vaše značka proti jmenovanému konkurentovi — je jediná věc, kterou jsme se zavázali nikdy nepostavit, protože je to podmínka mandátu od obchodníka. Bez toho mandátu neexistuje nic z toho ostatního.' Then give him the reframe immediately, because it is true and it is better than a hedge: what he receives instead is a read his buyer has already seen — the retailer has 90-day priority and a veto on every output — so it is the one source in his folder the buyer cannot dismiss on method. A contestable weapon he cannot use beats nothing; an uncontestable fact the buyer has already accepted beats both. Then name what he CAN have, concretely: who converts on his own SKUs, receipt-verified; adjacency lift with basket counts; whether the placement he paid for was engaged with; daypart shopper mix. Never imply a workaround, and never suggest the masking is thinner than it is.
- **"'Kvartálně a se zpožděním? Moje jednání je příští úterý. To je mi k ničemu.'"**
  - *Really about:* He assumes the value of data is its freshness, because the only data he currently gets — sell-out, promo results — decays fast. He has never had a source whose value was structural.
  - *Best answer:* 'Ano, se zpožděním — a to je záměr, ne kompromis. Ten lag je přesně to, co obchodníkovi umožnilo nám to vůbec dovolit.' Then the honest argument: what we deliver does not decay. The fact that the shoppers converting on his range at that forecourt skew a decade older than his campaign target, or that a given adjacency lifts the basket 35× on 51 baskets, is as true in March as it was in December. What decays is ammunition, and ammunition is the one thing we are contractually forbidden to sell him. Add the practical version: 'Do jednání příští úterý vám nedám nic. Do jednání za rok vám dám čtyři věci, které tam nikdo jiný nemá.' If he needs something next Tuesday, we are the wrong supplier for next Tuesday and should say so.
- **"'A ví o tom náš nákupčí? Protože jestli zjistí, že mám data o jeho prodejně, mám problém.'"**
  - *Really about:* Genuine relationship risk, and it is the objection he raises before price. He is right to raise it — none of our estates has any supplier-facing data policy or precedent, so an improvised retailer 'no' is a real possibility.
  - *Best answer:* Answer it before he asks it, in minute two. 'Nic z toho nevzniká bez podepsaného mandátu od obchodníka. Obchodník každý výstup vidí první, má 90 dní přednostní přístup a má právo veta na každý řez. Výchozí stav je, že se řetězec nejmenuje — reportujeme kanál, ne konkrétní síť; jmenovitá atribuce je jen na písemný souhlas případ od případu.' Then hand him the test we apply to ourselves: 'Nic, co dostanete, by nám nevadilo poslat nákupnímu řediteli té sítě ve stejné podobě.' That is the forwardability litmus, and it is a real internal rule, not a reassurance. Never promise that his sales function will be kept away from anything — that is a promise we cannot keep and his own commercial director knows it.
- **"'Máme Nielsena. A centrála kupuje panel. Co mi tohle přidá?'"**
  - *Really about:* The incumbent-currency reflex, and a budget-classification worry underneath it: he cannot see which line this comes out of. It is also a status question — the NIQ relationship belongs to someone, and it may not be him.
  - *Best answer:* 'Nielsen vám říká, co se prodalo. Panel vám říká, co dvě tisícovky domácností uvedly, že koupily. Ani jedno nepozoruje člověka u vašeho regálu.' Be precise and complimentary about the incumbent — never 'better than NIQ', ever, and especially not in tobacco, where NIQ sits inside the parent company of the estate we are selling a read on. The honest positioning is complementary and additive: the 'who' on top of their 'what'. And name the budget bucket for him out loud, because that is his real blocker: this is a trade/shopper marketing spend, not a research spend — his own director's line, not the region's.

- Wallet size against filed net profit. At AG Foods €40k is 25% of net profit, at Dallmayr Vending 86%. Those are study-only accounts and pitching a seat there wastes a slot.
- Vendor onboarding as a veto separate from budget: Nestlé, Mars and PMI all run global research procurement with preferred-supplier panels, so the spend can be approved and the vendor blocked.
- The camera method escalating above him: PMI would route to corporate affairs and global insights, BAT to a privacy/legal sign-off above Prague, Nestlé to a group data-protection function. A yes from him is not always a yes.
- Retailer-relationship risk. If he believes buying this could sour a chain relationship, he will decline politely and never say why. Pre-answer it with the mandate, the veto and the 90-day retailer priority, unprompted, in minute two.
- Capacity: eight live enterprise negotiations at ~50 founder-hours each is the entire budget. Slot nine does not exist, and no new enterprise logo after 15 November — so the last useful first meeting is the week of 3 November.

### Objections

- **"'Co to nahrazuje? Nemám na to řádek. A z jakého rozpočtu to jde?'"**
  - *Really about:* The budget-bucket problem, which is the recorded number-one obstacle from the one primary conversation on file: 'the hurdle is the budget bucket'. It is a classification problem, not an affordability problem — €40k is 0.03–0.7% of filed net profit at every large target on the list.
  - *Best answer:* 'Nenahrazuje nic. Jde to z trade/shopper marketingu, ne z výzkumu a ne z inovací.' Then make the size real against something he already spends: he is defending net revenue in a market where 64% of branded FMCG revenue moves on discount — the seat costs less than a mid-sized promo mechanic and is the only line on his data shelf his competitors do not also buy. Then give him the mechanic rather than the argument: this year's study order (CZK 300,000, fully credited against the seat) plus next year's subscription order, signed in one sitting, with a PO raised before year-end. One signature, two budget years. And do not let it split into 'study now, subscription in spring' — that is how a seat becomes a study. If procurement stalls the subscription, get the study order signed anyway; a signed order and a raised PO is what commits budget, an invoice commits nothing.
- **"'Jedna prodejna. To přece nemůžu vydávat za trh.'"**
  - *Really about:* He is right, he knows he is right, and he is checking whether we will admit it. This is the single fastest credibility test in the whole process, and most vendors fail it.
  - *Best answer:* 'Máte pravdu a my to nikdy netvrdíme. Jedna prodejna znamená ta prodejna — ne ta síť a ne trh. Jeden řetězec v kategorii je pohled na kanál, nikdy tržní průměr ani benchmark.' Then convert the limit into the commercial structure, honestly: this is why year one carries a density escrow — if the agreed coverage is not live on the agreed date, the second invoice is never issued and the first is refunded in full. The escrow is the answer to the real objection underneath, which is buying data that does not fully exist yet. And offer the falsifiable question back: 'Při jakém pokrytí by to pro vás bylo rozhodovací?' Getting his density requirement on the record is worth more to us than a yes.
- **"'Kamery v prodejně. To nám neprojde přes právní / přes centrálu.'"**
  - *Really about:* For tobacco this is the actual gatekeeper, not a stalling tactic — legal is the real decision-maker on that track. For the multinationals it is a routing question that may take the decision out of the country entirely: PMI would route to corporate affairs and global insights, BAT to a privacy/legal sign-off above Prague, Nestlé to a group data-protection function.
  - *Best answer:* Lead with compliance before anything else, in paragraph one, in writing: no facial recognition, no identities, no biometric templates, no cross-visit tracking, momentary processing, outputs only as anonymous aggregates of 25 or more people, and age as an ESTIMATE WITH ERROR BANDS — never 'age detection' and never anything that could be read as verification or as targeting minors. State affirmatively that no personal data of the brand is processed, therefore no brand-side DPA is required; that is what makes their legal review fast. Then ask the routing question directly, because the answer determines the whole account: 'Potřebuje cokoli kamerového podpis nad Prahou?' A hesitation there is worth more than another week of desk research.
- **"'Zajímavé. Ale je to nice-to-have. Doplněk.'"**
  - *Really about:* The price cap, spoken. He is classifying us as a persona deck — interesting, unbudgeted, bought once — which caps the deal in study territory and kills the seat. This is the recorded failure mode with a name.
  - *Best answer:* Do not argue with the classification; change what is being classified. Persona decks ARE nice-to-have and sophisticated brands already have better ones — so never lead with personas at a large buyer. Lead with closed-loop measurement: which shopper segment bought which SKU, at the shelf, matched to the receipt. Then ask the question that finds the path over the cap rather than arguing under it: 'Co by z toho udělalo must-have místo nice-to-have?' Listen to the answer — it is usually a KPI his own bonus is tied to. And hold the floor: below €40k it is a study, not a seat, and taking €25k trains exactly the 'complementary' cap he just articulated.

### Day in the life
Monday: pulls the period's NIQ scan extract and rebuilds the category file — category value and volume trend, segment shares, price ladders, new-SKU tracking. Tuesday: works on a planogram in Spaceman or Shelf Architect (or the chain's own tool), running space scenarios, checking facings against agreed range. Wednesday: sits in a KAM's buyer meeting as the category voice — presents the category story, the range recommendation, and the promo evaluation; is the person who answers when the buyer challenges a definition. Thursday: builds the category review deck: category trend first, brand performance relative to that trend, gaps, risks, then the range and planogram proposal. Friday: store visits, planogram compliance photos, and a promo post-evaluation nobody will read. Twice a year: the category review cycle proper (spring and autumn), which is the closest thing his job has to a deadline. Annually: contributes the category argument to the JBP and to the annual conditions negotiation. He is the person who reads a method footnote, and he is the only one in the building who will.

**THINK & FEEL**

- [high] Two or three countries, one person, no analyst — the STOCK ad is one seat for CZ/SK/DE; the Magnum ICC equivalent is one lead for CZ+SK at 75–90k CZK with nobody underneath.

- [medium] Private-label pressure at 28% share, where the chain holds both the data and the decision.
- [medium] Status: he is a support function with no budget, and his influence depends entirely on being unarguable.
- [WE DO NOT SOLVE THIS] Competitive share and range benchmarking within a named chain. This is the core input to a range recommendation, and it is precisely the prohibited cut. We will never give him it, and pretending otherwise is how we lose the mandate.

### Objections

- **"'Jak se to srovná s Nielsenem meziročně? Protože jestli se to nedá srovnat, do category review to nedám.'"**
  - *Really about:* Comparability is his professional religion and the precondition of everything he does. It is also the honest core of the incumbent's moat. He is not being obstructive; he is describing his job.
  - *Best answer:* 'Nesrovná se, a nemá se srovnávat — je to jiný jmenovatel.' Say it first and say it plainly: NIQ measures sales through tills; a panel measures claimed purchase in 2,000 households; we observe people at a fixture and match a subset to receipts at one store. Three different denominators, and pretending otherwise would fail his check in the first minute. What we CAN commit to is internal comparability: stable definitions, stable column names, the same suppression rule and the same cell counts every quarter, so OUR series is comparable to itself year on year from delivery one. Then hand him the authorship: ask him to define the cuts before we build them, and give him the credit line so he presents the read internally as his find. People promote what they co-authored, and he is the one person here who can kill us on method without writing a cheque.
- **"'Kolik případů je v té buňce? A jaký je interval spolehlivosti u toho věku?'"**
  - *Really about:* He is protecting himself from being contradicted in front of a buyer — the specific humiliation his whole job is organised to avoid. He will find the weak cell, and if we have not found it first we lose him permanently.
  - *Best answer:* Give him the annex before he asks, and make the discipline visible in the artifact itself rather than in a promise: every published cell suppressed below 25 persons; every lift figure carries its own basket count on the face of the chart; age is a decade bucket with 'unknown' as a first-class value, never a point estimate and never sharper than a decade; measured versus modelled labelled on every layer, every time. Include at least one honest null. Then state the limits he has not yet asked about, before he finds them: brand-worn observations are pooled only, never per-brand, because fill is 4–10%; and our visitor counts are the photographed subset, materially smaller than a door counter by design. One inflated number ends this entire buyer class — say that out loud; he will recognise it as his own standard.
- **"'Tohle by mi řekl náš vlastní shopper výzkum, kdybychom nějaký měli. Nebo agentura.'"**
  - *Really about:* Partly a status defence — producing the interpretation is his role and his standing — and partly a real question about whether we displace an existing agency relationship, which would make us a political problem as well as a purchase.
  - *Best answer:* Concede the first half genuinely: yes, the interpretation is his job and we are not trying to take it — which is why the deliverable is built to be re-authored under his name, with the method annex behind it so it survives challenge. Then be concrete about what no shopper study can do: a claimed-behaviour study asks people what they did; we observe what they did and match a share of it to receipts at 92% in the one place a real POS join exists. On the agency: if he mentions one, do not route around it — 'pomohlo by, kdybychom je vzali na jednání s vámi?'
