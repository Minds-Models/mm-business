# Audit: capability-fit + completeness critic

## HEADLINE

The single most important finding is that the persona set promises a product we do not have, to buyers we may not legally serve yet, at companies the target screen has already disqualified. The capability error is systemic and specific: 'at the fixture', 'at the instrumented zone' and 'at the facing' appear in seven use cases across all four clusters, but there is no fixture, zone, shelf or planogram entity anywhere in the schema — attention_seconds measures time facing the CAMERA on an ad screen, the conversion funnel groups at store level and makes no POS join at all, so 'who looked at your product and put it back' is not a thing we can measure, and it is the flagship promise of three of the four clusters. The rights error is equally systemic: file B's 'Deliverable TODAY' ladder is a capability statement built on GymBeam and Super zoo, and every researcher read it as a permission statement, producing roughly twenty [today] labels that are unlawful for a third-party brand while rights signed across the business stand at zero. Underneath both errors sits a commercial one that nobody aggregated: three of the four highest-value payers the set identifies — the owner as estate-holder, the private-label lead and Placek's distribution arm — are all inside the mandate partner, and at Placek all three are one legal entity, so the 21 November goal is currently most likely to be met by retailers paying cash, which inverts the model's central premise. Run the pack's own four filters — local pen, estate match, margin test, rights status — across all 25 personas and the workable third-party brand set collapses to roughly two companies, VAFO and BAT CR, both blocked on an unsigned mandate. Meanwhile the one revenue track that needs no mandate at all, the apparel Brand-Worn Index that DECISION.md calls the easiest 2026 signature, has zero persona coverage and zero use-case coverage from four independent researchers, and is gated on a fill-rate experiment due 1 October that none of them records. Cluster 3's insights-vacancy finding replicates cleanly on independent search — jobs.cz returns no brand-side FMCG consumer-insights role and profesia.sk returns zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia — which means the doctrine 'trade marketing pays, insights vetoes' is half-false for the eight companies that must actually close, though job ads measure hiring rather than headcount and this reversal still deserves a LinkedIn title test before it is written into the plan. Two personas are missing that block the plan rather than fund it: the EuroOil station tenant and Teta franchisee whose per-site consent decides whether the density escrow can ever be satisfied, and the tobacco CORA officer who is a different human from the DPO with a different fear. And every 'critical' pain in the set is our own value proposition restated, evidenced by our own endpoint, against the only primary brand voice on file, who called the product complementary and said his employer already had better personas. Twenty-five personas rest on one real conversation, and every client facts.yaml still lists contacts as empty.

## FINDINGS

### [CRITICAL] not-deliverable
**Fixture-level, zone-level and shelf-level attribution does not exist in the product, but appears in seven use cases across all four clusters as if it did. `attention_seconds` is a per-visitor column on `visitors_v2` measuring time facing the CAMERA that sits on the ad screen (the engaged predicate is literally `facing_camera_frame_count > 0`); `conversion-funnel` and `store-performance` group at STORE level; there is no fixture, zone, shelf, planogram or facing entity anywhere in the schema, and the registry's only spatial field is `merchandise_location` on the shopper, not on the store. 'Who stood at your fixture', 'the instrumented in-store zone versus the store baseline', 'the own-brand facing versus the branded facing' and 'the reset fixture' are all invented product.**

- *Affects:* C1 kam UC3 (trade-spend proof at the fixture he funded); C1 category-manager UC3 (execution audit at the fixture); C2 trade-shopper UC1 (audience read on an instrumented zone); C2 field-perfect-store UC2 (before/after at the reset fixture); C4 owner UC2 (who stopped in front of the shelf); C4 private-label UC2 (own-brand facing vs branded facing); C4 npd UC2 (attention conversion for the new line)
- *Fix:* Rewrite every 'at the fixture / at the zone / at the facing' as 'at the instrumented screen location, store-level'. Before any such promise, require a per-store screen-placement map and state in the deliverable which categories are physically adjacent to a camera. Ban the words shelf, facing and planogram from all brand-facing copy.

### [CRITICAL] not-deliverable
**The 'conversion funnel' does not measure conversion to purchase. `conversion-funnel` reads only `visitors_v2` and stages by `attention_seconds` >=1.5s and >=2.5s with NO POS join at all. It is a screen impression-to-engagement funnel. Selling it as 'the person who picked it up, looked, and put it back' — the single most-repeated promise in the whole persona set, and the flagship of three of the four clusters — is a category error that an ex-incumbent or a commercial analyst will find in one meeting.**

- *Affects:* C4 owner UC2 (non-buyer read); C4 private-label UC2 (the non-buyer at his own facing, called 'the single sharpest gap in his entire data stack'); C2 trade-shopper UC1; C1 kam UC3; C1 category-manager UC3; C3 insights-cz UC2
- *Fix:* Rename it in all materials to 'screen impression -> engagement staging'. The only honest buyer/non-buyer statement we own is the receipt-matched visitors-versus-orders gap (7,475 visitors, 2,622 matched orders, GymBeam, 21 days) — which is store-level and cannot say which product was declined. Say that out loud instead.

- *Affects:* C1 kam UC1; C1 sales-director UC3; C2 trade-shopper UC1/UC2/UC3; C2 brand-mktg UC1/UC2; C2 field UC1; C2 agency UC1; C3 insights-cz UC2/UC3; C3 insights-cee UC2; C4 owner UC1/UC2/UC3 (for the brand-only companies in the same persona)

### [CRITICAL] breaks-a-hard-rule
**C4 owner-jednatel UC5 proposes supplying age-band composition of a regulated category as evidence for what an industry association submits to a legislative process (the under-16 energy-drink bill). This breaches four rules at once: age is an estimate with error bands and in a public submission the bands will be stripped and it will be read as detection or verification; the read would be published into a live regulatory fight, which is the opposite of the publication lag and the retailer's veto and 90-day priority; a public submission identifies the estate; and an age-inferring system used to argue about minors is the AI Act's own textbook biometric-categorisation exposure, in the country whose regulator took 5,284 complaints in 2025.**

- *Affects:* C4 owner-jednatel UC5; every regulated category (tobacco, nicotine, energy drinks)
- *Fix:* Withdraw the use case. Age-band composition may be delivered as an internal, k>=25, lagged, retailer-vetted exhibit only, with a written prohibition on external, regulatory or public-affairs use in the licence.

## CROSS-CUTTING INSIGHTS

- THE PROPAGATION VECTOR. All four researchers read grounding file B's 'Deliverable TODAY' ladder as a rights statement when it is a capability statement — it is explicitly built on 'GymBeam and/or Super zoo', and Super zoo has NO disclosure greenlight at all while rights signed across the business are ZERO. That single misreading produced roughly twenty [today] labels that are unlawful for any third-party brand. B should be amended at source with a rights column, or the same error will recur in every downstream document.
- THE PITCH LEADS WITH THE WEAKEST THING WE HAVE. The single most-repeated promise across all four clusters is the non-buyer / attention read ('who stopped, looked and walked away'). It is built on attention to a CAMERA ON AN AD SCREEN, at store level, with no fixture resolution, no POS join, and a column with a documented outlier tail exceeding physical presence time. Meanwhile the genuinely strong, genuinely differentiated, genuinely shipped capability — persona x basket signature, receipt-matched, with the cell count printed on every figure (small pet + carrier at 35.8x lift on n=51 baskets, 54% attach) — is treated as supporting material. The pitch is inverted relative to the product.
- THE MONEY IS COMING FROM THE WRONG SIDE OF THE MODEL. Three of the four highest-value payers the set identifies — the owner as estate-holder, the private-label lead, and Placek's distribution arm — all sit inside the mandate partner, and at Placek all three are the same legal entity (ICO 28995911). If the 21 November goal is met by those buyers, it is met by RETAILERS PAYING CASH, which inverts the central premise that retailers pay in rights and brands pay cash. That may be the right commercial answer, but it has to be a decision taken in writing, not a result discovered in December.
- THE HONEST ANSWER TO 'DOES ANY PERSONA HAVE BOTH BUDGET AND AN UNMET NEED WE CAN SERVE?' IS: ONE, CONDITIONALLY. Run the four filters the pack itself specifies — local pen, estate match, margin test, rights status — over the 25 personas and the surviving intersection is thin. Beverages is estate-orphaned and formally killed; drugstore/HPC carries zero 2026 seat revenue; pet supports one or two seats and its giants take 0.69% and 0.00% of the measured shelf; tobacco runs on modelled POS with no shelf audit and an unknown instrumentable station count; Sportisimo and Teta are stores_live: 0. That is the most important sentence in this audit.
- THE POLITICAL DOCTRINE IS HALF-FALSE WHERE IT MATTERS MOST. 'Trade marketing pays, insights vetoes' is a good description of a Bin-B multinational and a poor description of the eight companies that must actually close. I replicated cluster 3's searches: no brand-side FMCG consumer-insights role on jobs.cz, zero shopper-insights roles in all of Slovakia. There is no veto-holder to sell through at Bin A, and the real vetoes are elsewhere — legal/DPO and tobacco CORA locally, and the cluster abroad at Bin B. Two of the four researchers still treat insights as a mandatory second thread. Caveat that must be honoured: job ads measure hiring, not headcount, and no agent ran a LinkedIn title search, so this doctrine reversal is directionally right but not yet proven.
