# 🎯 Target Personas

This page defines the key buyer personas that outreach must target. A meeting is only qualified if the contact matches one of these personas and the company clears the ICP filters.

**Read this before building any list. The buying centre is two people, not three.** At the eight working targets it is the statutory officer plus one commercial lead, and they are frequently the same human. Three separate researchers each claimed him as their own persona. Collapse them, or 8 accounts x 3 contacts x 50 hours exceeds the founder's entire calendar before outreach starts.

---

# Persona 1: Owner / jednatel / statutory-officer country GM

**Status:** Primary. Must win. The only signature that provably stays in the country.

**Seniority:** Statutory officer, registry-verified, or he is not this persona. A prokurista is not a statutory officer.

**Typical titles:** jednatel, majitel, zakladatel, společník a jednatel, předseda představenstva, člen představenstva, generální ředitel (GŘ), Country General Manager CZ & SK, CEO, provozní ředitel / COO where he is simultaneously jednatel.

**Demographics:** Predominantly male (roughly 80%), age 45 to 65, owner or co-owner of a Czech or Slovak family firm, resident in or near the town of the company seat rather than in Prague. Board tenures of 20 to 30 years are normal in this set. Runs the business on Excel and his own memory, not on a BI tool. Reads Forbes Česko and specifically the annual Největší rodinné firmy ranking, Hospodářské noviny, Seznam Zprávy Byznys. Attends Samoška in Olomouc in late January. (Pattern observed across the 43-entity registry screen and press coverage, not a survey.)

**What keeps them up at night:**

- Being the generation that loses it. The Czech family-firm succession wave is live and only about a third of firms cross it.
- The chain: a de-listing, a lost facing, another two points of zadní marže.
- The foreign discounter private label eating his tier from below.
- He has no measurement of who stands in front of his product and does not buy it. Every data source in his world is transactional and describes people who already bought.
- He will not say aloud that he does not know who picks his product up and puts it back. He says "znám svého zákazníka" and half believes it.

**What they want:**

- One number about his own shelf that he did not know, that contradicts something he believed, and that he can act on this quarter.
- To be the first in his category to have it. "First in Czech pet, forecourt or sports nutrition" is a Forbes sentence.
- A decision he can make alone, this quarter, with no procurement and no cluster. His autonomy is the thing he is proudest of.
- Tržby, EBITDA and marže growth against the named rival he benchmarks himself against.

**What they know (and don't know):**

- Knows his own shelf, his margins and his obrátka better than anyone. That part is true and must never be contradicted head-on.
- Is not a NielsenIQ client and knows the price would not survive his own margin test. His substitute is free trade-press headlines and NIQ's free session at Samoška.
- Carries money risk and opportunity-cost risk, not career risk. Nobody can fire him. "We can make you safe" invents a doubt he did not have and reads as weakness.
- Has no research reflex, no research line and no analyst. His accountant asks only whether it is náklad or investice.

**Key competitors they may reference:** NielsenIQ (free headlines, not a subscription), his own daily sales sheet, his loyalty file if he is also a retailer.

**First objection:** "Já jsem v té prodejně každý týden. Svého zákazníka znám." Behind it is status and identity, not scepticism. Agree first, then name the gap: he knows the people who bought, we measure the people who picked it up and put it back.

**Never say:** Never contradict znám svého zákazníka head-on. Never lead with method, GDPR reassurance, or any version of "we can make you safe".

**Minds & Models value proposition:** One number about your own shelf that you did not know, that you can act on this quarter, and that nobody else in your category has.

# Persona 2: Trade / Shopper Marketing Manager (CZ+SK)

**Status:** Must win. Highest willingness in the set, and the buyer of the first purchase.

**Seniority:** Manager level, not a director, and it does not matter. He owns the largest discretionary in-store line in the building and has the lowest provenance bar of any persona.

**Typical titles:** Trade Marketing Manager, Trade Marketing & Customer Strategy Lead CZSK, Manažer podpory prodeje, Vedoucí trade marketingu, Head of Trade Marketing, Shopper Marketing Manager (rare in CZ, near-absent in SK), Channel & Category Development Manager, trade marketing specialista.

**Demographics:** Frequently one person with no analyst. The job ad says he must turn shopper data into commercial plans, and nobody is hired to do the turning. Spends his budget against the chains' leták calendar, which is the real master schedule of his year. Reads Zboží a Prodej, Retail News and mistoprodeje.cz. Physically reachable on the POPAI DAY, Samoška and Czech Retail Days floor, which is three days a year.

**What keeps them up at night:**

- He cannot prove an activation worked beyond a sell-out bump he did not measure and cannot audit. POPAI CE finds 67% of Czech advertisers now call evaluating in-store spend "definitely important", up from 32% a decade ago, while only 49% actually measure it.
- The chain sets the price and the terms of his in-store visibility inside a listing negotiation, and he cannot evaluate what he bought.
- Fear that most of the display and POSM money is waste, and that one day somebody with a model proves it, after he has signed three more years of it.
- Sharper fear: that retail media becomes the chain's new tax, priced by the chain and unmeasurable by him, and that his budget migrates into it whether or not it works.
- Promotion has eaten the category and he is the person administering it. 58% of Czech food and drugstore spend moves on promotion, 64% for branded goods against 43% for private label.
- Unspoken resentment: the KAM owns the relationship, the brand manager owns the glamour, he owns the invoice.

**What they want:**

- One slide he can carry into the Monday commercial meeting that nobody in the room has seen before.
- A defensible reason to kill one activation format, freeing budget he can redeploy. That is how a trade marketer visibly earns his salary.
- A finding that contradicts the brand team's assumption about the shopper.
- To bring a genuinely new argument to the annual negotiation instead of re-arguing last year's terms.

**What they know (and don't know):**

- Comfortable with a partial answer that arrives fast. He changes mechanics every quarter and is proud of test-and-learn. His provenance bar drops further when a purchase is framed as a test rather than a source of record, which is why he buys first.
- His information about effectiveness comes 83% from internal sources, 33% from the chains and 29% from research agencies.
- Bonused on sell-out and the JBP number, not on shopper understanding. Sell-out lift is the one thing we cannot promise.

**Key competitors they may reference:** The chain's own sell-out report, Tesco Clubcard and dunnhumby, Teta KNOWDATA, and the field agency photo reports (ppm factum, 4P&P, GHP CZ, A1 Team).

**First objection:** "A o kolik nám to zvedlo prodej?" and then "Data z prodejen mi dává řetězec." The second is not a claim the incumbent is good. It is a signal that his in-store money is already contractually committed to the chain inside the listing negotiation.

**Never say:** Never say "insight" or "výzkum". Those words route his spend to a budget he does not control. Never promise sell-out lift.

**Minds & Models value proposition:** Independent proof of what your in-store money actually bought, from a source the chain did not hand you.

# Persona 3: Sales / Commercial Director (country P&L owner)

**Status:** Must win, but a real economic buyer only where he is himself a registered statutory officer.

**Seniority:** Director level, country P&L. At the eight working targets he is usually the same human as Persona 1.

**Typical titles:** obchodní ředitel / obchodní ředitelka, Commercial Director, Sales Director CZ/SK, ředitel obchodu, Country Commercial Manager, Head of Sales, holdingový obchodní ředitel, jednatel.

**Demographics:** Owns the commercial budget: trade marketing and trade investment (the largest discretionary line in a Czech FMCG subsidiary), sales operations, and whatever passes for shopper research locally. Discretionary signature at a locally-owned entity runs routinely from CZK 100k to 1m. Reads Zboží a Prodej including the TOP 50 českého obchodu, MediaGuru, Marketing & Media, and the SOCR ČR annual analysis with NielsenIQ. Attends Retail Summit in Prague, 10 to 12 March.

**What keeps them up at night:**

- Net revenue defended by promotion depth: branded goods at 64% promo share against private label's 43%, in a market where private label is 28% and rising.
- He is losing the shopper-knowledge argument to his own customers. Tesco already sells Czech suppliers Clubcard-backed insight off roughly 1.6m households. His own chains give him sell-out and nothing about people.
- Trade investment, his largest discretionary line, is spent with weak proof of return, and finance asks him about it every quarter.
- Career calculus: nobody was ever fired for buying NIQ. Buying a Czech startup's camera-based read is a personal risk and it is on him, which is why the escrow, the mandate and the method annex are not paperwork, they are his cover.

**What they want:**

- A read no competitor of his has. It is the only thing on his data shelf that is not commodity parity.
- Evidence for the trade-investment conversation with finance, protecting the largest line he controls.
- A decision-shaped deliverable, not a dashboard. In an organisation with no analyst, a written "so what" plus two or three moves is what he is actually buying, and he will pay materially more for it than for a feed.
- One slide. Literally one.

**What they know (and don't know):**

- Knows the market-share number (the NIQ number), gross-to-net, and his distribution and listings by chain, monthly.
- Knows perfectly well that one store is not a market, and is testing whether we will admit it. Admitting it fast is the single fastest credibility move available in the whole process, and most vendors fail it.
- Does not have a research line, and the problem is classification, not affordability. EUR 40k is 0.03% to 0.7% of net profit across the working set.

**Key competitors they may reference:** NielsenIQ, Tesco Clubcard and dunnhumby, his own sell-out and gross-to-net reporting.

**First objection:** "Co to nahrazuje? Nemám na to řádek." This is the recorded number-one obstacle from the only primary conversation on file. It replaces nothing: it comes out of trade and shopper marketing, not out of research and not out of innovation.

**Never say:** Never call it a research subscription. Never claim the market from one store.

**Minds & Models value proposition:** A read your competitors do not have, funded from trade, that changes where next year's investment goes.

# Persona 4: Retailer private-label / own-brand lead buying like a brand

**Status:** Primary. The only persona in the set with no incumbent supplier to displace.

**Seniority:** Business unit lead or senior category role, with a real P&L.

**Typical titles:** ředitel privátních značek, business unit manager privátní / vlastní značky, brand manager privátních značek, manažer vlastních značek, category manager, nákupní ředitel, vedoucí nákupu a rozvoje vlastních značek.

**Demographics:** Runs the own-brand range as a business unit with its own P&L, brand-like margins and a brand-like need to understand a shopper. Sets the tier architecture and the price ladder against the branded benchmark, owns pack design and the claim on the pack, and decides facings between own brand and brands. Reads Zboží a Prodej, Retail News and the BusinessInfo private-label features. Attends Retail Summit and PLMA. Quotes the number his whole industry quotes: private label in 61% of all Czech purchases in 2025, around 90% of discounter baskets.

**What keeps them up at night:**

- He has no read on his own shopper and nobody sells him one. NielsenIQ sells his category to his suppliers. There is no own-brand-facing shopper product at Plaček, Teta, ČEPRO/EuroOil, GymBeam or Sportisimo at all.
- He cannot separate cannibalisation from share-taking, and it is exactly the question his owner asks.
- He never sees the non-buyer: the person who stood in front of the facing, compared it with the brand and walked away with the other one.
- A public own-brand share claim he has to live up to.
- Discomfort that the same read would be sold to the very brand suppliers whose facings he takes.

**What they want:**

- The first read of his own shopper that anyone has ever offered him.
- A defensible answer to cannibalisation versus share-taking.
- Evidence that the pack and the tier are working, not just the price. That upgrades private label from a price play to a brand play, which is the story his whole industry is telling.

**What they know (and don't know):**

- Believes, because his industry keeps telling him so, that the retailer has the best access to consumer preference. Privately knows his evidence is a spreadsheet of what already sold.
- His loyalty programme sees members only, and purchases only.
- He has no incumbent, therefore no defender and no veto to fight. This is the least contested sale in the set.
- Is the fastest internal champion in a chain and usually cannot sign alone. Use him to reach the owner, not as the closer.

**Key competitors they may reference:** The loyalty programme (Super zoo family, Teta klub), POS reporting, and the NielsenIQ category data his suppliers buy.

**First objection:** "Mám věrnostní kartu. O svém zákazníkovi vím všechno." Then the harder one: "Vy tohle chcete prodávat i našim dodavatelům." That is the disintermediation fear, one of only two genuinely real retailer fears, and it is answered with the mandate clauses, never with marketing language.

**Never say:** Never mention that the same read is sold to brand suppliers without naming the firewall in the same breath. Never use his public own-brand claim against him: put the measured number next to it and let him do the arithmetic.

**Minds & Models value proposition:** The first read of your own shopper that anyone has ever offered you, and the answer to cannibalisation versus share-taking.

# Persona 5: Distributor / importer commercial lead

**Status:** High leverage. One door controlling dozens of brands that have no Czech entity at all.

**Seniority:** Commercial director, or at smaller houses the jednatel himself. Go to the pen, never to the category buyer. Landing on a junior buyer is the documented failure mode.

**Typical titles:** obchodní ředitel, ředitel velkoobchodu, jednatel / předseda představenstva, nákupní ředitel, category manager, produktový manažer, brand manager portfolia zastupovaných značek.

**Demographics:** Runs an order guide of thousands of items (PEAL over 5,000, Plaček around 155 brands), a depot, a picking line and a fleet running two to three deliveries a week per customer. His customers are trafiky, forecourt tenants, vets and 400 m² grocers, whose entire assortment he sets. Fights the same accounts as PEAL, GGT, GECO, JIP and CTC. Attends Kongres Samoška in Olomouc in late January and POPAI DAY.

**What keeps them up at night:**

- He argues assortment with sell-out, which describes what left the warehouse and says nothing about who took it off the shelf.
- He is judged by principals on a market he cannot describe. Dozens of the brands he carries have no Czech entity at all, so nobody upstream has ever seen a Czech shopper for them.
- Dead stock and obrátka across a book of thousands of items. Every listing decision is a bet made without a who-layer.
- A competitor wholesaler taking one of his accounts, or a principal threatening to go direct.
- He will not say aloud that he cannot tell a principal why an item moves, only that it moves.

**What they want:**

- Something to put in front of a principal that the principal does not have. It turns him from a logistics partner into the owner of the Czech consumer relationship, which is the most valuable political asset in his job.
- A who-layer on his own order guide, so he can defend a listing, a delisting or a range recommendation with more than obrátka.
- A reason for a customer to take his advice rather than the competing wholesaler's.

**What they know (and don't know):**

- Has two wallets and they behave differently: his own commercial and marketing budget, where a 150,000 to 500,000 CZK study fits comfortably and a EUR 40k seat is arguable at the larger houses, and the principal's marketing contribution.
- Does not know, and will ask, whether this is something his principals should be buying instead of him.

**Key competitors they may reference:** "Moji dodavatelé mají Nielsena", his own ERP and order-guide reporting, and the principals' global decks that say nothing about Czechia.

**First objection:** "Moji dodavatelé mají Nielsena. Proč bych to platil já?" Then the revealing one: "Můžu s tím jít do Albertu a zalistovat tam značku, kterou zastupuju?" That second question is useful intelligence and also the exact use we must prohibit.

**Never say:** Never suggest using it to negotiate against the brands he distributes. That is the asymmetry his principals did not consent to, and it is the fastest way to lose the whole book.

**Minds & Models value proposition:** Something to put in front of your principal that the principal does not have: the Czech shopper for brands with no Czech entity.

# Champions and co-authors, never the buyer

Work these records. Do not forecast against them.

**Key Account Manager / National Account Manager.** The access route to the chain and to his own commercial director. The cut he wants most, his share against a named competitor inside a named chain, is the toxic cut. Refuse it in minute three: refusing is what makes everything else believable.

**Category Manager / Category Development Manager.** The best internal author we have. He turns our read into a category story the buyer cannot counter with his own data. Every output must be presentable to the retailer's buyer as category evidence, not only brand evidence.

**The ex-NielsenIQ / GfK / Kantar / Ipsos person now inside a brand.** The most dangerous reviewer in the market and the fastest convert. Give him the limits table unprompted, before any finding, and send the method annex before the meeting, never after.

**Brand / Marketing Manager.** A secondary user, not a buyer. Lead with measurement and never with personas: personas are what his own function produces, and leading with them insults him.

# Blockers to neutralise

**Legal / Privacy / DPO.** Fears "tohle je biometrická kategorizace podle AI Actu". Article 50 has been live since 2 August 2026, the Czech adaptation act is still unadopted, and there is zero upside for them. Neutralise with a six-page pack that can be forwarded unedited to a group privacy function: the AI Act role map, the GDPR basis, the DPIA, the k>=25 architecture, and the volunteered list of cuts we will contractually never build. Do not lean on GDPR compliance alone: the two nearest in-store vendors already market it, so it reads as parity.

**Tobacco Corporate & Regulatory Affairs.** A different human from the DPO, with opposed incentives. Legal fears GDPR purpose-change and AI Act classification. CORA fears the advertising statute and being seen near a youth-adjacent method. Different fear, different document, separate record.

**Procurement.** "Máme preferované dodavatele na market research." Routing to a regional panel is the difference between a 2026 and a 2027 deal. Pre-empt with a complete onboarding file nobody asked for, and propose the spend classification yourself: trade services, not market research.

**Commercial / RGM analyst.** He will open the file and rebuild it, and his verification decides whether our number is allowed into any internal deck at all. Give him the method annex as a PDF plus the underlying counts in Excel, sent before the meeting.

**Country finance controller / CFO.** The budget line does not exist and the year is closing. Close two budget years in one sitting: a study order against this year, a subscription order for next, with the density escrow as the refund condition.

**Agency of record.** Fears exclusion from the client's read. Offer a named seat on it, and a proprietary audience read for their next pitch.

**The EuroOil station tenant and the Teta franchisee.** Nobody has created these records and both block the plan. Stations are tenant-operated and rent is turnover-based, so their per-site consent decides whether the density escrow can ever be satisfied. They fund nothing and can stop everything.

**Sequencing caveat.** Legal-first is right for a retailer, a DPO or a regulated category, where privacy is likely the first question raised. At an owner-managed firm with no legal function it manufactures a doubt he did not have. Hold the pack and deploy it on request.

# Not personas. Do not spend hours here.

Five were researched and killed outright.

- **CEE / Central-Europe cluster insights lead.** A 2027 buyer at the earliest. If engaged, ask them to define the coverage threshold. Do not ask them to buy.
- **Local NPD / innovation / product and pack.** No research budget anywhere in CZ or SK. The work is a technolog in a factory. Fold the NPD questions into the owner's conversation, where the decision actually sits.
- **Field sales / merchandising / Perfect Store execution owner.** Not one of his KPIs is a metric we produce. Availability, facings, share of shelf and planogram compliance are structurally outside the product.
- **E-commerce manager.** Researched and rejected.
- **Brand-side retail media buyer.** Does not exist in the Czech market at all. This contradicts standing doctrine that names it as a fourth wallet with an easier approval path.

# Rules that apply to every persona

1. **Use-case first, never data first.** The first three slides are three decisions the client makes differently. The observation layer comes second, as the minimum data required. Both the incumbent and the buyer independently say this is what decides the meeting.
2. **Never open with "here is a new signal".** The category's default pitch lands directly on a real scar: buyers describe adding data sources without governance as their biggest failure. "Nám nechybí data" is the sentence waiting for it.
3. **Sell the residual, not the who-question.** A brand buying retailer loyalty data believes the who-question is answered. Answer with what a card-based product cannot see: the unidentified shopper in front of the fixture, and the gap between visitors and buyers.
4. **Demographics are a denominator, never a segmentation.** "Demografie nic neřekne" is the primary objection to a demographics-led pitch, and it is right. Position inferred demographics as who had the chance to buy, never as something competing with basket data.
5. **Admit n=1 before they raise it.** One store means that store, never the chain and never the market. One chain in a category is a channel read, never a market average and never a benchmark.
6. **Every chart carries its own scope footer:** estate, store count, period, n, the k>=25 gate, age as an estimate with error bands, and "this store, not the chain". It will be forwarded without us.
7. **Understate.** Across 232 episodes of FMCG executives, operators' modest caveat-laden numbers read as credible and vendors' big narrative claims read as less so. Put the limits on the same page as the finding.
8. **Drop the latency story.** No voice in 166 hours of corpus asks for real-time insight data. Quarterly Excel is enough. Speed is not a selling point to this buyer.
9. **"Age estimation with error bands", never "age detection".** No facial recognition, no identities, no biometric templates, aggregates only.
10. **Route around whoever the number grades.** An honest measurement grades the last two years of media spend and typically looks worse than what was sold. The failure mode is silent non-adoption, not an objection you get to answer. Target the person who gains from a true number.
11. **The line that answers the biggest objection:** "This is not ammunition your buyer will resent. It is evidence your buyer has already accepted." The retailer holds 90-day insight priority and a veto, so anything a brand receives has already been seen and approved by its buyer. The publication lag is the credential, not the concession.

# Evidence health warning

These personas rest on one real brand conversation, and every client facts file still has an empty contacts list. The supporting corpus is 232 episodes of multinational voices with no Czech, Slovak or CEE speaker on any of these claims: a good falsification surface and a poor demand signal. Weight primary evidence over secondary three to one.

Log what actually happens. After every five meetings, read the "new objection" and "persona not as predicted" fields together. If either runs above about a third, this page is wrong rather than the market, and it gets rewritten from primary data.
