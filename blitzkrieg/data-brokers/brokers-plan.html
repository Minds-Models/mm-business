Why this pillar exists

Distribution velocity, not price

Brokers already hold the budgets and the brand/retailer relationships. That is the fast lane to cash and the reason to accept lower unit economics: we are buying speed and reach, and paying for it in margin. Every rule below exists so that we pay in margin only — never in position.

⚠ The DM Trap — the one way this pillar can destroy the company

The trap, stated plainly

“Tier-1 would want the pure data to combine with other sources” is exactly the DM position — becoming the supply side of someone else's 20–60× spread. Our own doctrine forbids it: never sell raw feeds; own the derived product.

The rule that prevents it — non-negotiable

Price per DERIVED PRODUCT (slot / read / API), never per store, per session or per datapoint. Per-store pricing is supply economics (~€141/store/yr); per-slot pricing is product economics. If a broker proposes per-store or per-record pricing, they are pricing us as the supplier in their spread — decline and reprice, or walk. (Our knowledge of incumbent supply pricing is INTERNAL-ONLY and is never revealed in the room.)

What They Get — and Never Get

Get

Derived outputs only: category reads, segment distributions, indices, API responses above the export gate — k≥25, coarsened time, no session or person identifiers, no record-level rows.

Never

Raw feeds · record-level data · methodology internals · any granularity fine enough to back-engineer the taxonomy. “Schema yes, kitchen no.”

Rights — the correction that must reach Legal in Week 1

A broker knowing the retailer grants us nothing

Attribution is governed solely by our contract with that retailer — not by the broker's existing relationship. Action: the DPA/mandate template needs a channel/reseller clause stating whether the retailer permits attributed data to reach third-party resellers, with per-channel written approval. Without it: aggregate, chain-masked outputs only, regardless of who the broker knows.

Contract Terms in Every Pilot

  • Non-circumvention — they may not approach our retailers or brands with a competing read built on our outputs. (Not a non-compete: their focus differs; the risk is their connections.)
  • No back-engineering / no re-identification covenant, with audit right.
  • 12-month term + renewal repricing. Never perpetual on our side.
  • No exclusivity, ever — the scripted answer to “we'd buy the whole feed exclusively”:
“We don't do exclusivity — it's what makes the read a currency rather than a private asset. What we can do: a category-limited, 12-month first-look window at a premium, against a volume commitment.”
  • Price floor: a broker deal must net ≥50% of what we would realise selling that scope direct — we save the selling cost, they take the rest. Below that the channel is cannibalising, not distributing.

The Two Pilots — Target Shapes

Pilot 1 — White-label read

CEE insight agency or shopper consultancy resells one category read under their brand into accounts we cannot reach. Deliberately narrow: one category, one geography, 12 months, non-exclusive, step-up pricing at renewal.

Pilot 2 — Eval-set / API to an AI-data buyer

Synthetic-consumer or AI-data vendor: the surviving defensible shape of the AI-data line. Same motion, same Oct 15 kill-test — if it converts, it counts as pilot #2. Reserve: retail-media data reseller or a syndicated distributor with no visual layer.

Pilot pricing discipline

€10–30k for a pilot is acceptable only because the scope is narrow — never a full category read at pilot price, which would cannibalise a €105–135k slot. Renewal steps up to the §price-floor rule.
