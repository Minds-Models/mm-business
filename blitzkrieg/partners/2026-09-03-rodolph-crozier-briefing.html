00The walk-out test

Before he leaves, make him say these back in his own words. If he cannot, the meeting is not finished.

He must be able to state

  1. What we sell now, and who pays. Retailers pay in rights and never in cash. Brands pay cash. Media operators and brokers pay in distribution.
  2. The single filter that decides whether a brand is worth an hour. Can this legal entity sign about EUR 40k without the signature leaving the country.
  3. Who inside a brand pays and who vetoes. Trade marketing pays, insights vetoes, never single-thread.

02The pivot story

Ninety seconds. Say it before he asks, and open with the correction rather than waiting to be caught.

Open with this

"Last time we spoke I sold you personalization: screens in stores, content adapted to the person standing in front of them, priced per device. Ignore all of it. We kept the technology and threw away the business model."

The beats, in order

  1. The data was always the base. To personalize anything you first have to read who is standing there. That capture layer is what we actually built, and it turned out to be worth far more than the screen. We describe a shopper across 450+ detectable visual attributes (about 88 captured per person) and match them to the actual receipt. At the GymBeam pilot, 92% of 2,622 orders across 21 days matched to a shopper profile. No faces, no identities.
  2. In-store media is a hard market. Many stakeholders, long chains, slow decisions. The number of parties who have to agree before anything ships is the whole problem.
  3. Retailers do not use in-store media to lift their own sales. They use it to sell space to brands. That single fact reframes everything: in that model the retailer is a landlord, not a user.
  4. So we were not solving any of a retailer's top three problems. We were selling them a channel they resell. Wrong product, wrong buyer, wrong urgency.
  5. Brands have a far bigger appetite for the underlying data, and it scales better. One retailer estate serves many brands. The unit of account moves from revenue per device (EUR 120 per device per month, capex-bound, linear, small) to the category x channel read: built once from the same capture, sold to every brand in that category at close to zero marginal cost. Roughly ten live reads at about EUR 500k each is a EUR 5M business.
  6. We no longer need to own the estate. Our software runs on the chain's existing CCTV, so deployment cost per store collapses, which is why we can hand the retailer the platform free.

What did not get thrown away

Personalization and in-store media stay in the portfolio as an application layer and a partner channel. They are no longer the pitch and no longer how we make money. We are the data infrastructure under retail media, not a competitor to it. That is the NANOVO conversation.

Formal status, so he knows this is decided and not a mood: D2 (19 Aug) demoted personalization to a brand-funded deployment subsidy, never the pitch and never sold standalone. D16 (21 Aug) is an all-in commitment to the data model until 21 Nov, with a dated go/no-go on that date.

If he asks why the next eight months differ from the last eight

The old model had no compounding: every sale started from zero, new store, new install, new price. The new one is a loop.

Rights (retailers) → Data → Proof → Revenue (brands) → Pull, where paid brands make retailer number two say yes.

Proof is reusable across chains, masking gets stronger with every chain added, and outbound turns into inbound. The first turn is the expensive one, and we are in it now.

Be honest about this, unprompted

Rights signed today: zero. Eleven devices live, about 133k unique visitors captured, and none of it legally sellable until a mandate exists. He will find out anyway. Saying it first buys the credibility for everything else.

03What the value is

The section he has to be able to repeat without notes.

The wedge: e-commerce runs on behaviour, physical retail got left behind with receipts. Physical retail knows what sold. Nobody knows who buys.

The licensed sentence, external use approved

"Syndicated data tells you what sold. We show you who buys it, in the aisle, receipt-verified."

Why the data is worth real money, in the order that persuades

  • It has no substitute. Footfall counters count bodies at a door. Shelf cameras, including NIQ's own in-store vision product, look at the shelf and not the shopper. Panels are recall-based and small. Nobody occupies depth x transaction-link, so a brand cannot buy the who-layer anywhere else.
  • It is sold N times, not once. Every brand in a category buys the same read. That is the definition of a measurement currency, and it is why we refuse exclusivity to everyone who asks for it.
  • It attaches to three different budgets inside the same company (section 07).
  • The EU is the moat, not the obstacle. The compliant version of this can only be built the way we built it: no facial recognition, no biometric templates, no cross-visit tracking, aggregates of 25 or more people only. Anyone arriving later has to solve that first.
  • The public price anchor: UZEI, a Czech state agency, pays ACNielsen about 3.7M CZK a year for a monthly sell-out feed, and about 230 to 240k CZK for a single narrow category report. On the public procurement record, so it is quotable.

05Retailers pay in rights, never in cash

They receive the platform, their own store insights, an AI chatbot over their own traffic data and a ready-to-sign compliance pack. What they give is a rights rider.

Why we refuse their money: it makes yes cost nothing, it removes procurement, and it keeps our legal position clean. Under the EU Data Act the party who pays for the analytics can restrict monetisation of the output, so being paid would make our rights position worse, not better.

The ask ladder, and the revenue-share model

  1. Open: 0% share, free platform. The value is their own store insights, deliverable day one, needing no other retailer's data. This is why "you cannot benchmark me yet" never blocks the door.
  2. Revenue share is a real model, not a concession: up to 40% on retailer-attributed products. The frame is "we help you monetize your own audience", a business we build for them rather than a discount we give them.
  3. It is a founding data-partner term. Once a category read exists, later chains in that category no longer need the share to make the deal work, and they do not get it. Scarcity on the supply side, and it is true rather than invented.
  4. Never, at any rung: a share of the cross-retailer aggregate. That is the currency, and sharing it turns us back into a vendor.

Instruction for him

Do not open with revenue share, because it starts a negotiation about percentages before they have understood what they get for free. If they ask "what is our cut", answer with the asymmetry first and money second: "the first thing you get is knowing more about your shoppers than your suppliers do, and then yes, there is a share on anything attributed to you."

The scoring filter, and the part he will get wrong without being told

Score a chain on slots unlocked x density tier reachable, never on store count.

  • Category slot-unlock counts (CZ): grocery 15 to 25, drugstore and HPC 5 to 8, petrol and convenience 2 to 4 (tobacco is the prize), electro and sport 1 to 3, pet specialty 1 to 2, mall landlord 0 (a different product entirely).
  • Four scores, multiplied: sales-cycle speed (company-operated with central IT = 5, hybrid franchise = 3, fragmented co-op = 1) x multibrand depth (high third-party SKU share = 5, heavy private label = 2, all own-brand = 1) x footprint sweet spot (60 to 150 stores = 5, 30 to 60 = 4, 200 to 500 = 3, 500+ = 1) x access path (warm intro = 5, second degree = 3, cold = 2).

A 90-store multibrand drugstore chain outranks a 300-store own-brand chain, and no deployment dashboard will ever tell you that.

  • Co-ops and franchises multiply the cycle by the number of independent owners. A federation's headline store count is not a signature. Target company-operated chains with centralised IT.
  • The second chain in a category is worth more than the 200th store in the first. Crossing from one chain to two roughly triples the price per brand seat, and it is what makes chain-masking honest.

The objection that kills rights deals

"We do not want brands to have our data, they will use it against us." It is not irrational: two of its five components are genuinely true (cross-chain benchmarking, and disintermediation). The answer is to invert the asymmetry.

Say this

"Today the brand knows more about your shopper than you do. They run research departments, you have one analyst per two or three categories, and that gap is already used against you in every trade negotiation. Your category manager will walk into the next supplier negotiation knowing which shopper segments actually convert on their SKUs in your stores, before the supplier sees any version of it. You have never had that."

Backed by contract controls he can name: trading-data firewall (we never ingest cost, margin, sell-through or elasticity), chain masking by default, no competitor-ranking cuts ever, category and brand embargo, publication lag, veto on every published output, k≥25 suppression, 90-day insight priority.

Ownership, in three sentences he should know cold

  1. "You own your footage and you own your till data. We never take title to either, and the footage never leaves your store."
  2. "What we own is the anonymous aggregate that comes out of the far end, and only because you licence it to us. Nothing in it identifies a person, and by default nothing in it identifies you either."
  3. "The licence is perpetual because a benchmark that can be switched off next quarter is not a benchmark. It is not exclusive: you can grant the same right to anyone else tomorrow."

Why perpetual is non-negotiable: a rented data asset prices as a service, roughly 3 to 4x revenue. An owned cross-chain measurement right prices at roughly 8 to 12x. That single clause is the difference between a vendor and a currency. If counsel pushes, we park and escalate. We never drop it to close a week.

06The brands filter

The most valuable single page for him. The filter is not size, and it is not category.

Can this legal entity sign about EUR 40k without the signature leaving the country?

We ran 181 companies through it. 53 shortlisted, 43 unique legal entities, 8 workable. The deletions are the instructive part.

Bin A  Genuine local pen, work these first

Owner-managed Czech or Slovak firms, or a country GM who is personally a registered statutory officer. Consumer Brands International and DanCzek (each jednatel signs alone, the 65% owner is the chairman). BAT CZ, where the GM and the jednatel have been the same person since 2014 and Slovakia runs as a branch of the Czech entity, so one signature covers CZ and SK. Kofola, its own ultimate parent, Prague-listed, nothing above it. Then Mattoni, VAFO, Plaček, Teta, GymBeam, Dermacol, Nutrend, Big Shock.

Bin B  Escalates to a cluster, do not pace as founding-speed

P&G (Bucharest CEE intelligence hub for twenty years, a structural pattern rather than a fresh reorg). Colgate (Warsaw CEE hub owns budgeting). Haleon (RHCS cluster stood up July 2025). L'Oréal, Coty, Henkel, Beiersdorf and Mars CZ (sole officers resident in Poland, Warsaw or Vienna). Farmina, whose Czech entity is capitalised at exactly CZK 20,000, the textbook rep-office signal. GGT, which routes to Bratislava.

The nuance he needs

The parent's nationality is not the filter. The budget's location is. Sportisimo's private-label cluster is 100% Czech-owned and still fails, because the decision sits in central merchandising rather than in a discrete brand-marketing budget.

Bin C  Unknown, so ask

This is his highest-leverage contribution. The question goes at the end of a methodology call, after the method has been explained and before any price is named.

The question, verbatim

"If you wanted to commission a Czech-only shopper-data study, roughly CZK 1m, from a new vendor, would that be your signature, or would it need to clear insights or procurement above Prague?"

A "yes, that's me" moves the account up a tier. A hesitation is worth more than another week of desk research. He should ask it every time and log the answer verbatim.

Three secondary filters, applied after autonomy

  • Wallet, measured against filed net profit, not revenue. EUR 40k is CZK 980,000. If that is a material fraction of net profit, the account prices at the floor and not above it.
  • Estate presence. Is this brand physically on shelf in an estate we have, or will have, rights to? A brand with no presence on our estate has nothing to buy.
  • Speed. Weeks to three months is a possible 2026 seat. Three to six months is a 2027 order. Anything slower is pipeline, not plan.

Capacity, which he must respect

Eight live enterprise negotiations maximum, about 50 founder-hours each, which is 36 hours a week for eleven weeks. Slot nine does not exist. No new enterprise logo after 15 Nov, so the last useful first meeting is the week of 3 November, which means the opening contact has to happen in September.

Trade marketing pays, insights vetoes. You need both. Never approach only one.

The three budgets, which is why the same product has three prices

  1. Sales and trade: negotiate with retailers, choose where to sell and on what terms. The biggest wallet.
  2. Marketing and media: build and test campaigns, find the segment that actually moves.
  3. Product development: find the gap in the line, decide the next product or pack.

Two quiet losers to plan around, not hope past

  • The insights manager who owns the incumbent relationship. A new source they did not bring in threatens their authority.
  • The agency of record, if measurement insources.

Mitigation is structural, not hopeful: sell through them, not around them. Co-design credit, a seat on a brand advisory micro-board, and they present the read internally as their own find. The champion is the gatekeeper, flipped. If a brand mentions its agency, the right move is "would it help if we briefed them alongside you?"

One shortcut worth giving him

Hunt people who moved from NielsenIQ, GfK or Kantar into a brand. They understand syndicated data instantly and convert fastest. LinkedIn filter: past company = NielsenIQ, current company = the brand.

08His role, and what he gets

Door-opener, budget cartographer, and grey hair in the room. Not a closer.

Three jobs, in order of value to us

  1. Doors. Named warm introductions to a specific person, with the reason they should take the call. Volume is not the metric. Correctness of the name is.
  2. Budget cartography. Which budget funds this, whose signature clears it, what band, and when their planning cycle runs. This is research task R2 and it is currently unfilled. The renewal date is the account's calendar.
  3. Posture. Two founders in a room read as a startup. Two founders plus a man who ran a multi-country retail services business for twenty years read as a vendor a category director can buy from without career risk.

Rules of engagement, non-negotiable

  • He opens, we close. He books the meeting and attends. Josef runs the commercial conversation.
  • He never quotes a price. If asked: "that depends on the coverage you would need, which is the first thing Josef will ask you."
  • The sequencing problem is his to navigate (section 09). Safe everywhere: method, privacy design, product shape, the categories we are building. Handle with care: naming a specific chain's data before its mandate is signed.
  • He never offers exclusivity, in any form, to anyone.
  • He does not receive the target list. It carries internal wallet bands, autonomy downgrades and named individuals. He gets the leave-behind, plus a per-account brief for accounts he is actually working.

Already settled, do not reopen

Compensation is agreed and the NDA is signed. Neither is an agenda item, which means the whole meeting is available for the work itself.

The sequencing problem: not a rule, his judgment

  • To sell a brand a specific retailer's data, we need that retailer's mandate.
  • To get the mandate, it helps enormously to show brand demand.
  • Classic chicken and egg. There is no rule that resolves it, and we are not giving him one.
  • Safe in any conversation: the method, the privacy design, the shape of the product, the categories we are building.
  • Handle with care: naming or implying a specific chain's data before its mandate is signed. If that trade looks worth making in a given room, he makes the call and tells us afterwards.

11The five questions he will ask

Answers from the objection bank. Use them verbatim.

Q1Is this legal? Is it not surveillance?

"By design we cannot identify anyone. No facial recognition, no biometric templates, no re-identification, no cross-visit tracking. Sessions are opaque one-time observations. Everything leaves the system as aggregates of 25 or more people. We describe audiences, not persons. That constraint is also the moat: the compliant version of this can only be built the way we built it."

Q2Footfall counters and heatmaps already exist.

"Counters track bodies, a number at the door. We describe shoppers across 450+ detectable attributes, about 88 per person, and match them to actual transactions, 92% at our pilot. Counting is solved and cheap. Understanding who buys is neither. Nobody else occupies depth x transaction-link."

Q3Why will NIQ not just do this?

"Their business is POS feeds and panels. They have no in-store visual shopper layer and no fast legal path to build one in the EU. Their own camera product looks at shelves, not shoppers. We are complementary, the who on top of their what, which makes them a distribution partner or an acquirer rather than a competitor. And our retailer rights are perpetual, so the portfolio compounds."

Q4Retail sales cycles are two years. How is this a three-month plan?

"Tier-one grocery is two years, which is exactly why we do not start there. Our pipeline is founder-led mid-size chains where one person can say yes. Our first eight chains took three and a half months to engage. The giants come later, approached with a proven multi-chain product and brand demand already in hand."

Q5What have you actually sold?

Answer straight: zero rights instruments signed, zero brand contracts signed today. Pilots running, one live retailer procurement process, one verbal greenlight, and a receipt-verified proof at GymBeam. Then: "which is precisely why the next eleven weeks are structured the way they are, and why you are in this room."
