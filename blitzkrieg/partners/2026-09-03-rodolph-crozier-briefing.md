## 2. The pivot story (90 seconds, say it before he asks)

**Open with the correction, do not wait to be caught:**

> "Last time we spoke I sold you personalization: screens in stores, content adapted to the person
> standing in front of them, priced per device. Ignore all of it. We kept the technology and threw
> away the business model."

**The beats, in order:**

1. **The data was always the base.** To personalize anything you first have to read who is standing
   there. That capture layer is what we actually built, and it turned out to be worth far more than
   the screen. We describe a shopper across 450+ detectable visual attributes (about 88 captured per
   person) and match them to the actual receipt. At our GymBeam pilot, 92% of 2,622 orders across
   21 days matched to a shopper profile. No faces, no identities.
2. **In-store media is a hard market.** Many stakeholders, long chains, slow decisions. The number
   of parties who have to agree before anything ships is the whole problem.
3. **Retailers do not use in-store media to lift their own sales.** They use it to sell space to
   brands. That single fact reframes everything: the retailer is a landlord in that model, not a
   user.
4. **So we were not solving any of a retailer's top three problems.** We were selling them a channel
   they resell. Wrong product, wrong buyer, wrong urgency.
5. **Brands have a far bigger appetite for the underlying data, and it scales better.** One retailer
   estate serves many brands. The unit of account moves from revenue per device (EUR 120 per device
   per month, capex-bound, linear, small) to the **category x channel read**: built once from the
   same capture, sold to every brand in that category at close to zero marginal cost. Roughly ten
   live reads at about EUR 500k each is a EUR 5M business.
6. **We no longer need to own the estate.** Our software runs on the chain's existing CCTV, so
   deployment cost per store collapses, which is why we can hand the retailer the platform free.

**What did not get thrown away.** Personalization and in-store media stay in the portfolio as an
application layer and a partner channel (section 04). They are no longer the pitch, and they are no
longer how we make money. See the NANOVO point: we are the data infrastructure under retail media,
not a competitor to it.

**Formal status, so he knows this is decided and not a mood:** D2 (19 Aug) demoted personalization
to a brand-funded deployment subsidy. Never the pitch, never sold standalone. D16 (21 Aug) is an
all-in commitment to the data model until 21 Nov, with a dated go/no-go on that date.

**If he asks "why will the next eight months be different from the last eight":**
The old model had no compounding. Every sale started from zero: new store, new install, new price.
The new one is a loop. **Rights (retailers) to Data to Proof to Revenue (brands) to Pull**, where
paid brands make retailer number two say yes. Proof is reusable across chains, masking gets stronger
with each chain added, and outbound turns into inbound. The first turn is the expensive one and we
are in it now.

**The one thing to be honest about, unprompted:** rights signed today = 0. Eleven devices live,
about 133k unique visitors captured, and none of it is legally sellable until a mandate exists.
He will find this out anyway. Saying it first buys the credibility for everything else.

## 3. What the value is (the section he has to be able to repeat)

**The wedge, in one line:** e-commerce runs on behaviour, physical retail got left behind with
receipts. Physical retail knows **what sold**. Nobody knows **who buys**.

**Our sentence, licensed for external use:**

> "Syndicated data tells you what sold. We show you who buys it, in the aisle, receipt-verified."

**Why the data is worth real money, in the order that persuades:**

- **It has no substitute.** Footfall counters count bodies at a door. Shelf cameras (including
  NIQ's own In-Store Vision) look at the shelf, not the shopper. Panels are recall-based and small.
  Nobody occupies depth x transaction-link. A brand cannot buy the who-layer anywhere else.
- **It is sold N times, not once.** Every brand in a category buys the same read. That is the
  definition of a measurement currency, and it is why we refuse exclusivity to everyone who asks.
- **It attaches to three different budgets in the same company** (see section 7).
- **The EU is the moat, not the obstacle.** The compliant version of this data can only be built
  the way we built it: no facial recognition, no biometric templates, no cross-visit tracking,
  aggregates of 25+ people only. Anyone arriving later has to solve that first.
- **Price anchor that is public and citable:** UZEI, a Czech state agency, pays ACNielsen about
  3.7M CZK a year for a monthly sell-out feed and about 230 to 240k CZK for a single narrow
  category report. That is the ladder our pricing sits on, and it is on the public procurement
  record.

**What we actually sell to a brand:**

| Layer | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Study (T0) | 150 to 500k CZK one-off | A single read. Take the money. **Not a seat** |
| **Founding seat** | **floor EUR 40k, ask EUR 40 to 60k** | Year 1 only, max 3 per category, 50% off list, density escrow |
| List (T1) | EUR 75 to 122k / yr | Undiscounted. 30 to 60 stores, 1 chain |
| Channel currency (T2) | EUR 122 to 327k / yr | 150 to 300 stores across 2+ chains |

**The number he needs burned in: EUR 40k is the floor.** Below that it is a study. We take the
money, we do not call it a seat, and we never use it as the year-two reference. He should never
quote a price at all (section 8), but he must know the size of the room he is opening doors to,
so he does not aim at wallets that cannot get there.

### And the value to whom, inside the brand

The same product has three buyers, three budgets and three reasons to care. He needs all three,
because the one who loves it is rarely the one who signs.

| Who | Their question | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade marketing, category, key account** | Where do I sell, on what terms, how do I argue with the retailer | Trade marketing. **The biggest wallet** |
| **Insights, research** (P&G calls it CMK, Nestle calls it Market Intelligence) | Is the method sound, does it beat what I buy today | Research |
| **Brand or marketing manager** | Which segment moves, did the campaign work | Brand and media |

**Trade marketing pays. Insights vetoes.** Full detail in section 7.

## 5. Retailers: they pay in rights, never in cash

**The doctrine (D1):** retailers are pure supply. We never ask them for money. They receive the
platform, their own store insights, an AI chatbot over their own traffic data, and a ready-to-sign
compliance pack. What they give is a rights rider.

**Why:** it makes yes cost nothing, it removes procurement, and it keeps our legal position clean
under the EU Data Act (the party who pays for the analytics can restrict monetisation of the
output, so being paid would make our rights position worse, not better).

**The ask ladder, and the revenue-share model.**

1. **Open: 0% share, free platform.** The value is their own store insights, deliverable day one,
   needing no other retailer's data. This is why the "you cannot benchmark me yet" objection never
   blocks the door.
2. **Revenue share is a real model, not a concession:** up to 40% on **retailer-attributed
   products**. The frame is "we help you monetize your own audience", which is a business we build
   for them rather than a discount we give them.
3. **It is a founding data-partner term.** Once a category read exists, later chains in that
   category no longer need the share to make the deal work, and they do not get it. Scarcity on the
   supply side, and it is true rather than invented.
4. **Never, at any rung:** a share of the cross-retailer aggregate. That is the currency, and
   sharing it is what turns us back into a vendor.

> **Instruction for him:** do not *open* with revenue share, because it starts a negotiation about
> percentages before they have understood what they get for free. If they ask "what is our cut", the
> answer is the asymmetry first and money second: *"the first thing you get is knowing more about
> your shoppers than your suppliers do, and then yes, there is a share on anything attributed to
> you."*

**The scoring filter (this is the counter-intuitive part, and it is the part he will get wrong
without being told):**

Score a chain on **slots unlocked x density tier reachable**, never on store count.

- Category slot-unlock counts (CZ): grocery **15 to 25** · drugstore/HPC **5 to 8** ·
  petrol/convenience **2 to 4** (tobacco is the prize) · electro/sport **1 to 3** ·
  pet specialty **1 to 2** · mall landlord **0** (different product entirely).
- Four scores, multiplied: **sales-cycle speed** (company-operated with central IT = 5, hybrid
  franchise = 3, fragmented co-op = 1) x **multibrand depth** (high third-party SKU share = 5,
  heavy private label = 2, all own-brand = 1) x **footprint sweet spot** (60 to 150 stores = 5,
  30 to 60 = 4, 200 to 500 = 3, 500+ = 1) x **access path** (warm intro = 5, second degree = 3,
  cold = 2).

**The sentence that makes it click for him:**

> "A 90-store multibrand drugstore chain outranks a 300-store own-brand chain, and no deployment
> dashboard will ever tell you that."

**Two more rules:**

- **Co-ops and franchises multiply the sales cycle by the number of independent owners.** A
  federation's headline store count is not a signature. Target company-operated chains with
  centralised IT.
- **The second chain in a category is worth more than the 200th store in the first.** Crossing from
  one chain to two roughly triples the price per brand seat (T1 to T2). It is also what makes
  chain-masking honest.

**The objection that kills rights deals, and the answer.** "We do not want brands to have our data,
they will use it against us." It is not irrational. Two of its five components are genuinely true
(cross-chain benchmarking, and disintermediation). The answer is to **invert the asymmetry**:

> "Today the brand knows more about your shopper than you do. They run research departments, you
> have one analyst per two or three categories, and that gap is already used against you in every
> trade negotiation. Your category manager will walk into the next supplier negotiation knowing
> which shopper segments actually convert on their SKUs in your stores, before the supplier sees
> any version of it. You have never had that."

Backed by contract controls he can name: trading-data firewall (we never ingest cost, margin,
sell-through or elasticity), chain masking by default, no competitor-ranking cuts ever, category
and brand embargo, publication lag, veto on every published output, k>=25 suppression, 90-day
insight priority.

**Ownership, in three sentences (he should be able to say these cold):**

1. "You own your footage and you own your till data. We never take title to either, and the footage
   never leaves your store."
2. "What we own is the anonymous aggregate that comes out of the far end, and only because you
   licence it to us. Nothing in it identifies a person, and by default nothing in it identifies
   you either."
3. "The licence is perpetual because a benchmark that can be switched off next quarter is not a
   benchmark. It is not exclusive: you can grant the same right to anyone else tomorrow."

Why the perpetual clause is non-negotiable: a rented data asset prices as a service (roughly 3 to
4x revenue), an owned cross-chain measurement right prices at roughly 8 to 12x. That single clause
is the difference between a vendor and a currency. If counsel pushes, we park and escalate, we
never drop it to close a week.

## 6. The brands ICP filter (the most valuable single page for him)

**The filter is not size, and it is not category. It is:**

> **Can this legal entity sign about EUR 40k without the signature leaving the country?**

We ran 181 companies through it. 53 shortlisted, 43 unique legal entities, **8 workable**. The
filter is what deleted the other 35, and the deletions are instructive:

**Bin A, genuine local pen (work these).** Owner-managed Czech or Slovak firms, or a country GM who
is personally a registered statutory officer. Examples: Consumer Brands International / DanCzek
(each jednatel signs alone, 65% owner is the chairman), BAT CZ (GM and jednatel fused in one person
since 2014, and Slovakia runs as a branch of the Czech entity so **one signature covers CZ and SK**),
Kofola (its own ultimate parent, Prague-listed, nothing above it), Mattoni, VAFO, Plaček, Teta,
GymBeam, Dermacol, Nutrend, Big Shock.

**Bin B, escalates to a cluster (do not pace these as founding-speed).** P&G (Bucharest CEE
intelligence hub, 20 years, a structural pattern rather than a fresh reorg), Colgate (Warsaw CEE
hub owns budgeting), Haleon (RHCS cluster stood up July 2025), L'Oreal / Coty / Henkel / Beiersdorf
/ Mars CZ (sole officers resident in Poland, Warsaw or Vienna), Farmina (Czech entity capitalised at
exactly CZK 20,000, the textbook rep-office signal), GGT (Bratislava holding).

Note for him: **the parent's nationality is not the filter, the budget's location is.** Sportisimo's
private-label cluster is 100% Czech-owned and still fails, because the decision sits in central
merchandising rather than in a discrete brand-marketing budget.

**Bin C, unknown, so ask.** This is his highest-leverage contribution. The question goes at the
**end** of a methodology call, after the method has been explained and **before any price is named**:

> "If you wanted to commission a Czech-only shopper-data study, roughly CZK 1m, from a new vendor,
> would that be your signature, or would it need to clear insights or procurement above Prague?"

A "yes, that's me" moves the account up a tier. A hesitation is worth more than another week of
desk research. Variants exist per company; he should ask it every time and log the answer verbatim.

**Three secondary filters, applied after autonomy:**

- **Wallet, measured against filed net profit, not revenue.** EUR 40k = CZK 980,000. If that is a
  material fraction of net profit, the account prices at the floor and not above it.
- **Estate presence.** Is this brand physically on shelf in an estate we have (or will have) rights
  to? A brand with no presence on our estate has nothing to buy.
- **Speed.** Weeks to three months = a possible 2026 seat. Three to six months = a 2027 order.
  Anything slower is pipeline, not plan.

**Capacity, which he must respect:** 8 live enterprise negotiations maximum, about 50 founder-hours
each, which is 36 hours a week for 11 weeks. **Slot 9 does not exist.** And no new enterprise logo
after 15 Nov, so the last useful first meeting is the week of 3 November, which means the opening
contact has to happen in September.

## 7. Who inside the brand (never single-thread)

Three named people per company, always. One is not enough and the wrong one is fatal.

| Role | What they do | Their vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade marketing / category / key account** | **Holds the money.** Biggest wallet in the building. Uses it to negotiate with retailers and decide where and how to sell | "trade spend", "listing", "channel" |
| **Insights / research** | **Vetoes on method.** Owns the incumbent data relationship. Can kill us without ever writing a cheque | P&G calls it **CMK** (Consumer & Market Knowledge), Nestle calls it Market Intelligence, others Consumer Insights |
| **Brand / marketing manager** | The campaign use-case: create and test campaigns, find the market-moving segment. Agency-influenced | "activation", "reach", "measurement" |

**Rule: trade marketing pays, insights vetoes. You need both. Never approach only one.**

**The three budgets, which is why the same product has three prices:**

1. **Sales / trade:** negotiate with retailers, choose where to sell and on what terms. The biggest
   wallet.
2. **Marketing / media:** build and test campaigns, find the segment that actually moves.
3. **Product development / NPD:** find the gap in the line, decide the next product or pack.

**Two quiet losers he must plan around, not hope past:**

- The **insights manager who owns the incumbent relationship**: a new source they did not bring in
  threatens their authority.
- The **agency of record**, if measurement insources.

Mitigation is structural, not hopeful: **sell through them, not around them.** Co-design credit, a
seat on a brand advisory micro-board, and they present the read internally as their own find. The
champion is the gatekeeper, flipped. If a brand mentions its agency, the right move is *"would it
help if we briefed them alongside you?"*

**One shortcut worth telling him:** hunt people who moved from NielsenIQ, GfK or Kantar into a
brand. They understand syndicated data instantly and they convert fastest. LinkedIn filter: past
company = NielsenIQ, current company = the brand.

## 9. Hard rules (print these, he cannot break them)

| Rule | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| **No exclusivity, ever**, to a brand or a retailer | Syndication is the entire multiplier. Exclusivity converts a currency into a one-off study. The compliant scarcity is stronger anyway: 3 founding seats per category, 1 to 2 founding data-partner slots per retail category, and real Q4 budget deadlines |
| **Trading-data firewall.** Retailer margins, sell-through and elasticity never reach a brand | It is a technical fact, not a promise. We never ingest it, so we cannot leak it |
| **One chain in a category is a channel read, never a market average or a benchmark** | Cross-chain claims require 2+ chains. This is the claim that would fail diligence |
| **Pooled brand-worn claims only, never per-brand** | Fill rate is 4 to 10%. Per-brand claims are indefensible until volume grows about 10x |
| **Internal benchmark numbers never leave the building** | Competitor contract values, incumbent supply prices, our unit costs |
| **Sell through the gatekeeper, not around them** | The insights manager can kill us without ever writing a cheque. Co-design credit converts them |

### The sequencing problem: not a rule, his judgment

- To sell a brand a specific retailer's data, we need that retailer's mandate.
- To get the mandate, it helps enormously to show brand demand.
- Classic chicken and egg. There is no rule that resolves it, and we are not giving him one.
- **Safe in any conversation:** the method, the privacy design, the shape of the product, the
  categories we are building.
- **Handle with care:** naming or implying a specific chain's data before its mandate is signed. If
  that trade looks worth making in a given room, he makes the call and tells us afterwards.

## 11. The five questions he will ask, with the answers

1. **"Is this legal? Is it not surveillance?"**
   "By design we cannot identify anyone. No facial recognition, no biometric templates, no
   re-identification, no cross-visit tracking. Sessions are opaque one-time observations. Everything
   leaves the system as aggregates of 25 or more people. We describe audiences, not persons. That
   constraint is also the moat: the compliant version of this can only be built the way we built it."

2. **"Footfall counters and heatmaps already exist."**
   "Counters track bodies, a number at the door. We describe shoppers across 450+ detectable
   attributes, about 88 per person, and match them to actual transactions, 92% at our pilot.
   Counting is solved and cheap. Understanding who buys is neither. Nobody else occupies depth
   x transaction-link."

3. **"Why will NIQ not just do this?"**
   "Their business is POS feeds and panels. They have no in-store visual shopper layer and no fast
   legal path to build one in the EU. Their own camera product looks at shelves, not shoppers. We are
   complementary, the who on top of their what, which makes them a distribution partner or an
   acquirer rather than a competitor. And our retailer rights are perpetual, so the portfolio
   compounds."

4. **"Retail sales cycles are two years. How is this a three-month plan?"**
   "Tier-one grocery is two years, which is exactly why we do not start there. Our pipeline is
   founder-led mid-size chains where one person can say yes. Our first eight chains took three and a
   half months to engage. The giants come later, approached with a proven multi-chain product and
   brand demand already in hand."

5. **"What have you actually sold?"**
   Answer straight: zero rights instruments signed, zero brand contracts signed today. Pilots
   running, one live retailer procurement process, one verbal greenlight, and a receipt-verified
   proof at GymBeam. Then: "which is precisely why the next eleven weeks are structured the way they
   are, and why you are in this room."
