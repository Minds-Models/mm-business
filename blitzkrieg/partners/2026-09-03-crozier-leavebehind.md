## 01 / The product: what we actually sell

Not a screen, not a device, not a store. We sell a category read.

> **Syndicated data tells you what sold. We show you who buys it, in the aisle, receipt-verified.**

- 450+ detectable visual attributes, about 88 captured per shopper.
- Matched to the receipt: **92%** of orders at our pilot store.
- No faces, no identities, no cross-visit tracking. Aggregated outputs only.
- No substitute exists. Counters count bodies. Shelf cameras watch shelves. Panels are recall.

**The unit is the category read: built once, sold to every brand in that category. Store count is
supply, never revenue.**

## 02 / The pivot: why we stopped selling screens

Same technology. Different customer.

- **The data was always the base.** To personalize anything, you first have to read who is standing
  there. That capture layer is what we actually built.
- **In-store media is a hard market.** Many stakeholders, long chains, slow decisions.
- **Retailers do not use in-store media to lift their own sales.** They use it to sell space to
  brands. In that model the retailer is a landlord, not a user.
- **So we were not solving any of their top three problems.** We were selling them a channel they
  resell.
- **Brands have a far bigger appetite for the data, and it scales better.** One retailer estate
  serves many brands.

*What did not get thrown away:* personalization and in-store media stay in the portfolio as an
application layer and a partner channel. They are no longer the pitch.

## 03 / The system: one loop, three currencies

```
RIGHTS  →  DATA  →  PROOF  →  REVENUE  →  PULL
retailers  their    receipt   brands buy   paid brands make
licence    cameras  match     the read     retailer no.2 say yes
```

| Market | Pays in | Gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Brands** | Cash | The category read, as an annual seat |
| **Retailers** | Rights | Free platform, own-store insight, share of attributed revenue |
| **Media operators, brokers** | Distribution | The audience layer under their own product |

**Collect at 1, sell at 2 and 3, partner at 4.**
1 collect (hard, the moat) · 2 insight · 3 actions · 4 execution (crowded, capital-heavy).

# CHAPTER ONE: BRANDS
**They pay in cash. This is the customer.**

## 04 / Inside a brand: three buyers, three budgets, three reasons

The one who loves it is rarely the one who signs. You need all three.

| Who | Their question | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade marketing** (category, key account) | Where do I sell, on what terms, how do I argue with the retailer | Trade marketing. **The biggest wallet** |
| **Insights** (P&G calls it CMK, Nestle calls it Market Intelligence) | Is the method sound, does it beat what I buy today | Research |
| **Brand or marketing manager** | Which segment moves, did the campaign work | Brand and media |

**Trade marketing pays. Insights vetoes. Never single-thread a brand.**

## 05 / The brand filter: which brands are worth an hour

Not size. Not category. **Five filters, all non-negotiable.**

1. **A local pen.** Can this legal entity sign about EUR 40k **without the signature leaving the
   country?** Owner-managed Czech or Slovak firms qualify, and so does a country GM who is
   personally a registered statutory officer.
   *Fails if the budget sits in Warsaw, Bucharest, Vienna, Bratislava or Basel.*
2. **A wallet that makes EUR 40k trivial.** Measured against **filed net profit, not revenue.** A
   big top line with thin margins prices at the floor and argues about every crown.
3. **Physically on shelf in an estate we have** (or will have). A brand with no presence in our
   stores has nothing to buy.
4. **Decides in weeks, not quarters.** Weeks to three months is a live seat. Three to six months is
   next year's order. Slower is pipeline, not plan.
5. **Sits in a category with depth.** Roughly five payable brands in a category for the read to
   syndicate. One buyer is a study. Three or more is a currency.

**When the pen is unknown, ask this** at the end of the meeting, before any price:

> *"If you wanted to commission a Czech-only shopper study, roughly CZK 1m, from a new vendor,
> would that be your signature, or would it clear above Prague?"*

**The parent's nationality is not the filter. The budget's location is.** Some Czech-owned companies
fail this test, and some multinationals pass it.

# CHAPTER TWO: RETAILERS
**They pay in rights, never in cash.**

## 06 / The offer: free platform, and a share of what we sell

- **They never pay cash.** They grant rights.
- They receive the platform free on their existing cameras, their own store insight from day one, an
  AI chatbot over their own traffic data, and a ready-to-sign compliance pack.
- **Revenue share is a real model, not a concession.** Up to 40% on retailer-attributed products.
  The frame is "we help you monetize your own audience", a business we build for them.
- **It is a founding data-partner term.** Once a category read exists, later chains in that category
  no longer need the share to make the deal work, and they do not get it.
- **Their footage and till data stay theirs.** Footage never leaves the store. What we hold is the
  anonymous aggregate, licensed to us, perpetual and non-exclusive.

*Why they say yes:* the asymmetry inverted. Today their suppliers know their shopper better than
they do, and that gap is used against them in every trade negotiation.

## 07 / The retailer score: which chains we want

> **Do not count stores. A 90-store multibrand drugstore chain beats a 300-store own-brand chain.**

**Five filters, all non-negotiable.**

1. **Multibrand, not private label.** High third-party SKU share is the whole point. We sell brands
   a read of their own shoppers, so if the shelf is mostly the retailer's own brands, there is
   nobody to sell to. *Fails if the assortment is majority own-brand.*
2. **Company-operated, with central IT.** One signature covers the estate. Franchise means
   negotiating twice. A co-operative multiplies the cycle by the number of independent owners.
   *Fails on fragmented co-ops, however big the store count looks.*
3. **Category breadth: how many reads it unlocks.** This is where the value actually sits.
   Grocery **15 to 25** · drugstore and HPC **5 to 8** · petrol and convenience **2 to 4** ·
   electro and sport **1 to 3** · pet specialty **1 to 2**. Two 200-store chains can differ by an
   order of magnitude on this one line alone.
4. **60 to 150 stores.** Fast enough to decide, big enough to be statistically real. Under 30 is
   thin. Over 500 is a two-year procurement cycle.
5. **A warm way in.** Founder-led, mid-size, one person can say yes. Warm intro beats second degree
   beats cold, and the gap is not small.

# CHAPTER THREE: PARTNERS
**They pay in distribution.**

## 08 / Media operators and brokers: we are the layer under retail media

Not a competitor to it.

- **RMN and DOOH operators are already asked by their clients for audience proof** and cannot
  deliver it. We can, running on their own players and under their own site contracts. This is the
  NANOVO conversation.
- **Two different deals, never merged:** operator access (their estate, our measurement) and data
  distribution (a partner reselling the read).
- Brokers and insight agencies are distribution and optionality, not the core.

## 10 / The lines: how we say it

| Audience | We are | The line |
|---|---|---|
| **Retailer** | In-store data intelligence platform | "Full visibility into your own stores, free. In exchange you licence anonymous aggregates, and you see everything before any brand sees a masked version." |

Attached to any depth claim, always: no facial recognition, no biometric templates, no identities,
aggregated outputs only.

## 11 / Principles: three things we do not bend

1. **No exclusivity, ever.** To a brand or a retailer. Syndication is the entire multiplier, and
   exclusivity turns a currency into a one-off study. The real scarcity is stronger anyway: three
   founding seats per category, one to two founding retailer slots.
2. **Privacy by design is the moat, not the constraint.** No facial recognition, no biometric
   templates, no identities, no cross-visit tracking, aggregated outputs only. The compliant version
   of this can only be built the way we built it, and anyone arriving later has to solve that first.
3. **Trading data stays theirs.** We never ingest cost, margin, sell-through or elasticity, so we
   cannot leak them. What we help a retailer sell is their **audience**, never their trading terms.
   That distinction is what makes the rights conversation possible at all.

## 12 / The open problem: chicken and egg

Yours to navigate. There is no rule that solves it.

- To sell a brand a specific retailer's data, we need that retailer's mandate.
- To get the mandate, it helps enormously to show brand demand.
- **Safe in any conversation:** the method, the privacy design, the shape of the product, the
  categories we are building.
- **Handle with care:** naming or implying a specific chain's data before its mandate is signed. If
  that trade looks worth making in a given room, make the call and tell us afterwards.

## 13 / Where you plug in

1. **Doors.** The named person, and the reason they take the call. One right name beats ten names.
2. **Budget cartography.** Which budget funds this, whose signature clears it, what band, and when
   their planning cycle runs. This is the answer we are missing most.
3. **Posture.** Two founders read as a startup. Add twenty years of CEE retail services and we read
   as a vendor a category director can buy from without career risk.
4. **Your 2022 experience.** You already tried to sell offline retail analytics into Czech retail.
   Who bought, who blocked, at what price, in which job title. That debrief is worth more to us than
   an introduction.

**Brand budgets for next year lock in October and November. First contact has to happen in
September.** A brand not already in conversation by then is buying out of a locked budget, which
usually means waiting a full cycle. The next few weeks matter more than the following six months.
