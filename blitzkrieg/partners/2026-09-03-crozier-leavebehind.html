01 / The product

What we actually sell

Not a screen, not a device, not a store. We sell a category read.

The licensed sentence Syndicated data tells you what sold. We show you who buys it, in the aisle, receipt-verified.
  • 450+ detectable visual attributes, about 88 captured per shopper.
  • Matched to the receipt: 92% of orders at our pilot store.
  • No faces, no identities, no cross-visit tracking. Aggregated outputs only.
  • No substitute exists. Counters count bodies. Shelf cameras watch shelves. Panels are recall.

The unit is the category read: built once, sold to every brand in that category. Store count is supply, never revenue.

02 / The pivot

Why we stopped selling screens

Same technology. Different customer.

  • The data was always the base. To personalize anything, you first have to read who is standing there. That capture layer is what we actually built.
  • In-store media is a hard market. Many stakeholders, long chains, slow decisions.
  • Retailers do not use in-store media to lift their own sales. They use it to sell space to brands. In that model the retailer is a landlord, not a user.
  • So we were not solving any of their top three problems. We were selling them a channel they resell.
  • Brands have a far bigger appetite for the data, and it scales better. One retailer estate serves many brands.

What did not get thrown away

Personalization and in-store media stay in the portfolio as an application layer and a partner channel. They are no longer the pitch.

05 / The brand filter

Which brands are worth an hour

Not size. Not category. Five filters, applied in this order.

All five are non-negotiable

  1. 01 A local pen

    Can this legal entity sign about EUR 40k without the signature leaving the country? Owner-managed Czech or Slovak firms qualify, and so does a country GM who is personally a registered statutory officer.

    Fails if the budget sits in Warsaw, Bucharest, Vienna, Bratislava or Basel
  2. 02 A wallet that makes EUR 40k trivial

    Measured against filed net profit, not revenue. A big top line with thin margins prices at the floor and argues about every crown.
  3. 03 Physically on shelf in an estate we have

    Or one we will have. A brand with no presence in our stores has nothing to buy.
  4. 04 Decides in weeks, not quarters

    Weeks to three months is a live seat. Three to six months is next year's order. Slower than that is pipeline, not plan.
  5. 05 Sits in a category with depth

    We need roughly five payable brands in a category for the read to syndicate. One buyer is a study. Three or more is a currency.

When the pen is unknown, ask this. End of the meeting, before any price

"If you wanted to commission a Czech-only shopper study, roughly CZK 1m, from a new vendor, would that be your signature, or would it clear above Prague?"

The parent's nationality is not the filter. The budget's location is. Some Czech-owned companies fail this test, and some multinationals pass it.

06 / The offer

Free platform, and a share of what we sell

  • They never pay cash. They grant rights.
  • They receive the platform free on their existing cameras, their own store insight from day one, an AI chatbot over their own traffic data, and a ready-to-sign compliance pack.
  • Revenue share is a real model, not a concession. Up to 40% on retailer-attributed products. The frame is "we help you monetize your own audience", a business we build for them.
  • It is a founding data-partner term. Once a category read exists, later chains in that category no longer need the share to make the deal work, and they do not get it.
  • Their footage and till data stay theirs. Footage never leaves the store. What we hold is the anonymous aggregate, licensed to us, perpetual and non-exclusive.

Why they say yes

The asymmetry inverted. Today their suppliers know their shopper better than they do, and that gap is used against them in every trade negotiation.

07 / The retailer score

Which chains we want

Start here Do not count stores. A 90-store multibrand drugstore chain beats a 300-store own-brand chain.

Five filters, all non-negotiable

  1. 01 Multibrand, not private label

    High third-party SKU share is the whole point. We sell brands a read of their own shoppers, so if the shelf is mostly the retailer's own brands, there is nobody to sell to.

    Fails if the assortment is majority own-brand
  2. 02 Company-operated, with central IT

    One signature covers the estate. Franchise means negotiating twice. A co-operative multiplies the cycle by the number of independent owners.

    Fails on fragmented co-ops, however big the store count looks
  3. 03 Category breadth: how many reads it unlocks

    This is where the value actually sits. Grocery 15 to 25 · drugstore and HPC 5 to 8 · petrol and convenience 2 to 4 · electro and sport 1 to 3 · pet specialty 1 to 2.

    Two 200-store chains can differ by an order of magnitude on this one line alone.
  4. 04 60 to 150 stores

    The sweet spot: fast enough to decide, big enough to be statistically real. Under 30 is thin. Over 500 is a two-year procurement cycle.
  5. 05 A warm way in

    Founder-led, mid-size, one person can say yes. Warm intro beats second degree beats cold, every time, and the gap is not small.

11 / Principles

Three things we do not bend

  1. 01 No exclusivity, ever

    To a brand or a retailer. Syndication is the entire multiplier, and exclusivity turns a currency into a one-off study. The real scarcity is stronger anyway: three founding seats per category, one to two founding retailer slots.

  2. 02 Privacy by design is the moat

    Not the constraint. No facial recognition, no biometric templates, no identities, no cross-visit tracking, aggregated outputs only. The compliant version of this can only be built the way we built it, and anyone arriving later has to solve that first.

  3. 03 Trading data stays theirs

    We never ingest cost, margin, sell-through or elasticity, so we cannot leak them. What we help a retailer sell is their audience, never their trading terms. That distinction is what makes the rights conversation possible at all.

12 / The open problem

Chicken and egg

Yours to navigate. There is no rule that solves it

  • To sell a brand a specific retailer's data, we need that retailer's mandate.
  • To get the mandate, it helps enormously to show brand demand.
  • Safe in any conversation: the method, the privacy design, the shape of the product, the categories we are building.
  • Handle with care: naming or implying a specific chain's data before its mandate is signed. If that trade looks worth making in a given room, make the call and tell us afterwards.
