The three things we are not re-litigating this window
  • Data value is the VC-backable case. Our exit multiple and our margin structure both come from the cross-retailer aggregate and syndicated category reads, not from any single retailer relationship.
  • Own-hardware personalization is not venture-backable. It is good, fundable-by-a-brand business at the margin, and we keep it exactly there. It stops being something we sell as the company.
  • Revenue-share clarity. The cross-retailer aggregate and syndicated reads stay 100% ours, always. A retailer only ever earns a share on the labelled, single-chain read tied to their own store, and even there the model runs that share at 30%, trending toward zero as a category matures into the aggregate.
01 · Why we're doing this

We changed the payer, not the product

The engine, the visual model, and the receipt-matching are the same asset we've been building for two years, and they keep compounding. What changed is who we ask to pay for the read. We assumed retailers would buy this as personalization. Eight months of pilots taught us they structurally can't, and that brands structurally can. That's not a change of belief, it's a change we bought with real pilot data.

The one-line version

"We changed the payer, not the product." Same engine, same data asset. Retailers give us supply for free; brands pay for the read.

Retailer as the customer (what we tried)

  • ~4% net margins, leaving little room for a discretionary insight line
  • Roughly one analyst covering two or three categories
  • No existing budget line for "shopper insight"; every euro needs a CFO and a procurement cycle
  • Result: strong pilot engagement, real willingness to test, but conversion from pilot to paid rollout kept stalling, because insight was never one of their top three problems

Brand as the customer (where we're going)

  • 14–16% margins and a dedicated data/insights team that evaluates products like ours for a living
  • An existing annual measurement budget, already spent on syndicated data from incumbents
  • Buys the same read once, then sells it to every brand in the category (syndication, not a bespoke project)
  • Budget windows lock every October and November, which is exactly the calendar we're racing right now

This also answers the objection almost every fund has given us: why won't the next eight months look like the last eight? The honest answer isn't a promise to move faster, it's that the thing which made us slow has been structurally removed.

What we're not saying

We're not saying personalization "didn't work." Where a brand funds it directly (the tobacco screens, at €120/device/month), it's good, real revenue and we keep it. What we're saying is narrower: it's a poor foundation for a venture-scale company when the buyer is a 4%-margin retailer with no line item for it, and we now have the pilot evidence to say that with confidence instead of a hunch.

02 · The pitch and the number

The new VC case: it's the multiple, not the revenue

The feedback we've heard from most rounds we've run has been consistent: this can be a nice business, but the maths didn't reach a fund-returning outcome. That feedback was right about the old model. It's the reason the new one is built backwards from the exit rather than forwards from a sales plan.

The reframe

You don't grow your way to a venture outcome here. You re-rate your way there. The same revenue is worth a different multiple depending on what kind of asset it is.

Syndicated measurement assets trade on revenue, not earnings, because the data set is the asset and margin follows scale later. The reference points are public: Advent acquired Nielsen Global Connect in 2021 for roughly $2.7bn; NielsenIQ merged with GfK in 2023 and listed on the NYSE in 2025 in an $11bn offering; Circana acquired Nielsen's marketing-mix modelling business; VusionGroup has been acquiring computer-vision and retail-media assets (Belive.ai, In-Store Media) rather than building them. Mature, slow-growing measurement trades near the bottom of an 8–12× band. A cross-chain rights portfolio still compounding above 60% a year sits in the middle of it, which is where our 10× working assumption comes from.

The multiple only holds if the underlying rights are durable. A licence a retailer can terminate prices as a rented service, at 3–4× revenue, not 10×. That's the entire reason the perpetual, irrevocable aggregate licence and the k≥25 anonymisation regime live in the data-processing agreement rather than in the deck: they are, literally, the multiple.

The tightest month isn't now, it's month 13 (Sep 2027), where modelled cash bottoms out around €114k, about a month of cover, before the seed round lands. 03 · The sprint

Blitzkrieg: 21 Aug → 21 Nov

We've committed the company to an all-in, thirteen-week sprint on three axioms: the data business is the venture-backable case, own-hardware personalization is not, and the brand is the ultimate customer. Three clocks are running at once, and only one of them is fully in our control.

Clock 1 · Retailer rights

  • First data-rights mandate, in motion now
  • Determines whether "free supply for rights" actually holds

Clock 2 · Brand budgets

  • Locks in Oct–Nov
  • Miss it, and 2027 brand revenue slips a full year

Clock 3 · Cash

  • Wall hits mid-December at current burn
  • The pre-seed has to land ahead of this, not react to it

How to read 21 Nov honestly (so a modest number doesn't read as failure)

The whole scoreboard collapses to one test: by 21 Nov, has every currency converted at least once? One retailer paid us in rights. One brand paid us in cash. One investor paid us in money. One proof point verified against a real receipt. One inbound lead we didn't chase. That's the floor, and it proves the loop turns. It is deliberately not the same thing as being on track for a big February revenue number, and we'd rather you judge us on that basis from the start than have us explain it after the fact if November's ARR line looks small.

04 · Spending discipline

Cutting cost without going quiet

We're reallocating hours, not gutting the team. Through 21 Nov, we're deliberately shifting engineering time away from personalization features and into business-facing work: rights conversations, brand meetings, the proof artifacts the raise needs. Net effect on burn is close to neutral. This is a deliberate, time-boxed choice, not a survival measure, and it reverts automatically if we don't have a term sheet by 16 October.

Why we can do this without stalling the roadmap

  • We already made the call to run on cameras retailers already own, not our own hardware, so there's no capex schedule to protect
  • The ingest pipeline was built generically (any camera, not one vendor), so the same engineering keeps compounding into the data product even while personalization work slows
  • Personalization isn't being killed. Where a brand already funds it directly, we keep shipping it exactly as committed

What doesn't change

  • Existing brand-funded screen commitments (tobacco, €120/device/month) continue unchanged, since a brand is paying for that work, so it's good business as-is
  • Core calibration and data-science work is untouched and fully protected, since it's the proof the whole raise depends on
  • No layoffs. This is an hours reallocation with a revert trigger, not a headcount cut
