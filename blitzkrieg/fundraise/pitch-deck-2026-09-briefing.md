## 1. The frame we are surfing, and why

The risk with this company is that an investor files it under "retail analytics vendor", a category
that was interesting around 2015 and has been a graveyard since. Everything in the deck is arranged
to stop that from happening. We are not selling a measurement device. We are producing insight that
nobody has been able to produce, in the last large channel of human behavior that was never recorded.
Note the vocabulary rule that follows from this: in front of an investor we talk about **insight and
answers**, not about a dataset. A dataset is inventory. An answer is a product.

Three things are true in the venture market right now, and all three point our way:

1. **Model access stopped being a moat.** The consensus question in diligence is no longer what the
   product does, it is what a well-funded competitor could not replicate quickly. The answers that
   survive are proprietary compounding data, workflow depth and, above all, what one widely-read
   piece calls *time that cannot be parallelized*. A retailer signature per chain is exactly that:
   no amount of capital makes a chain sign faster.
2. **The scarce input is real-world data, not compute.** The public corpus is exhausted and the data
   that counts is the data the public corpus lacks, the kind that cannot be bought. Ours was never
   recorded by anyone, so it cannot be scraped, licensed or reconstructed.
3. **Capital is pouring into the physical world.** Physical AI took $47.4bn across 521 deals in H1
   2026, close to four times H2 2025. We are not a robotics company and we must never imply that
   money is addressable revenue, but we are unambiguously in the physical-world-data wave, and that
   is the shelf the investor should file us on.

**Say this, roughly:** "E-commerce has had behavioral data for twenty years. Nine in ten FMCG euros
are still spent in a store that records nothing about the shopper. We turn that shelf into insight,
who is actually buying and what to do about it, and the rights to it are signed one retailer at a
time."

**Never say:** "we sell retail analytics", "our dashboard", "computer vision platform", "smart
store", "we use AI to". These land us in the 2015 category. Also avoid "incumbent" as a noun in the
room, it is jargon: say "the largest company in the market" or name NIQ.

### 02 · Problem
**The headline is now the whole argument:** consumer brands do not know **who buys them**. Under it,

**Then three cards, adjective first**, each one line: *expensive* consumer panels, *blind* checkout

**The number at the bottom:** $18bn a year is already spent on consumer measurement globally (NIQ's

**Support if asked:** ecommerce is 7.2% of global grocery spend (Kantar Worldpanel), so nine in ten

**Do not** use sell-out, sell-in, share of category, panel projection or any other category jargon.

**On the design:** red now marks exactly one idea on this slide, the missing "who". It appears in

### 03 · How, three steps
**Lands:** there is a route from a camera to an answer, and none of it involves recruiting anybody.
Three steps, left to right. *Make the data nobody has*: the signal is already in the room, every

receipt. *Shoppers sort themselves*: 88 visual attributes on every shopper, 18.8 million of them so
far, and at that size the segments draw themselves rather than being authored by a marketer.
*Ask it like an analyst*: one question in plain words against the whole model.
**The line to land on step one:** there is nobody to buy this from. That is the moat sentence.
**Note:** step three previews the chat, slide 05 delivers it. Do not demo here, just point.

### 04 · The data

**Why the worked row matters:** it is the single most convincing object in the deck for a technical

### 06 · Business model
**Lands:** the two-sided structure and the non-linearity. The bullets on both cards are deliberately
telegraphic now, four or five words each. They are prompts for you, not reading material for them.
Retailer: pays nothing. Gives the cameras it already owns plus its POS feed, and a perpetual
aggregate license, anonymized and chain-masked. Gets two things, a revenue share on data it never
had to collect, and the same read back at no cost, which is a view of its own shoppers no chain has
ever had. Say that second one out loud, it is the reason the conversation is easy.
Brands: pay 40k, 80k, 170k per category per year as density grows. Give a budget that already
exists, and no exclusivity, which is what makes one read sellable to every brand in the category.
Get the answer nobody can sell them today, and a read that deepens every year.
**The non-linearity, and this is the investment thesis in one line:** cost scales with stores,
revenue scales with seats. Across the plan the deployed base grows about 25x while ARR grows about
125x, so ARR per instrumented store rises from about 2.9k to about 14.2k EUR.
**If pushed on why:** each new chain adds categories to the same footprint, each category fills with
more brand seats over 24 months, and the per-seat price steps up when a category runs in two or more
chains of a market. Three multipliers on one deployment.

## 4. Investor FAQ

### The data and the moat

**What stops a big player doing this next quarter?**
Not the technology. The rights. Every chain signs its own aggregate mandate, once, with its own
data-processing agreement, and a signature cannot be parallelized with capital. The company that
already sells the what-sold half has no path to the who half that does not go through the same 26
conversations we are having.

**Is the data really proprietary, or is it the retailer's?**
The retailer owns its stores and its POS. What we hold is a perpetual license to the anonymized,
aggregated, chain-masked derivative, granted per chain. That distinction is written into the
data-processing terms, which sit in the assumptions of the financial model rather than in a legal
annex, because the whole exit spread turns on it.

**Why can't a brand just buy this from the retailer directly?**
The retailer has cameras and receipts. It does not have the join, the model, or the cross-chain
view, and it has no interest in building a measurement business. It also cannot sell a market read,
because a market read by definition spans chains that compete with it.

**Does the data compound, or is it a static pile?**
It compounds on three axes at once: more chains, more categories per chain, and more history per
category. History is the one nobody can buy back, and it is what turns a read into a trend.

**Is this facial recognition?**
No. No identities, no biometric templates, nothing identifiable leaves the store, aggregates only
at k>=25, and age is estimated with error bands rather than detected. This is the disarm line and it
should be said unprompted the first time cameras come up.

### The model and the numbers

**Why does ARR grow 125x while stores grow 25x?**
Three multipliers on the same deployment: categories per chain rise to four, seats per category rise
from two to about three over 24 months, and the per-seat price steps 40k to 80k to 170k as the
category reaches two or more chains in a market. Cost only tracks the first of those.

**What are the unit economics?**
LTV/CAC 6.1x, CAC payback 9.9 months, ARR per instrumented category 473k, ARR per chain deployed
819k, ARR per head 266k at 80 people. Gross margin 67.7% in 2031, 94.0% measured on net revenue
after the retailer share.

**When does it make money?**
EBITDA positive from December 2030 and stays positive. Full year 2031 is +2.75M EUR, 14.9%, with
19.9% in Q4. The full-year figure averages loss-making months with profitable ones, which is why
the quarterly run-rate is the fairer number.

**How much capital in total?**
5.72M EUR across all rounds: 500k pre-seed, 2.6M seed, 2.5M Series A. Founders hold 53.4% at exit
with a 12.0% option pool.

**What does the 500k actually buy, line by line?**
The model does not carry a percentage split for the pre-seed window, and inventing one would be
worse than the drivers it does carry: 20k EUR per chain integrated, one category analyst per five
live categories, one data or ML engineer per seven, one deployment engineer per 4.5 chains, sales
sized at 800k of new ARR per account executive, one legal/DPO per 1.5 markets. Hires land two months
before the driver that justifies them. That is what makes the ask checkable.

**What if the Series A never happens?**
The United States never opens and 2031 ARR lands near half the base case. The company does not run
out of cash. The Series A buys the American half of the exit, not survival.

**What if churn is three times what you assume?**
Tested at 15% and 25% against a modeled 8%. The larger case is what sized the Series A. Every tested
case stays funded.

**Your 2026 revenue is tiny. Why should I believe 2031?**
You should not believe 2031. You should believe the next four observations, which is exactly what
this round is scoped to produce, and they are on the ask slide. The model is a consequence of those
four, not a substitute for them.

**Where did the exit multiple come from?**
An observable band, not an assertion. Listed data businesses 8.9x to 14.1x revenue, median software
acquisition 4.5x. We apply 8x to 10x to 21.3M of 2031 ARR. The exit ladder in the model runs from
2.3x (49.0M) through 4x (85.2M), 8x (170.4M), 10x (212.9M) to 12x (255.5M). The spread turns almost
entirely on whether the license is perpetual and cross-chain.

### Competition

**What about NIQ, Circana, Kantar?**
They own the what-sold half and sell it extremely well. They do not have the who half, and their
route to it is the same retailer-by-retailer rights conversation. Historically they have bought
rather than built: the in-store vision and shelf-sensing capability in that market has come through
acquisition. We are structured to be that acquisition, from the other side of the join.

**Footfall counters and people-counting vendors?**
They count bodies and they sell to the retailer's operations budget. There is no purchase attached,
no brand on the other side, and no rights portfolio. One was acquired at roughly 2.3x revenue, which
is the market's verdict on analytics sold without owned rights, and it is the floor row on our
ladder.

**Retail media networks?**
Adjacent, and eventually a customer. They optimize the placement of a message. We measure who bought
after it. A retail media network with no who-layer is buying its own measurement from us, not
competing with us.

### Team and execution

**Why you two?**
One founder has four-plus years taking AI products to market, the other does research in algorithmic
personalization. The collective bench covers consumer intelligence at NielsenIQ, recommender systems
at Alza, product and GTM at SpaceKnow, computer vision and AI infrastructure. The two halves of this
problem, measurement and vision, are both in the room.

**Czechia first, seriously?**
Yes, and deliberately. Retail here is consolidated, the chains are reachable by two founders inside
a quarter, and a category read only needs about 60 stores in a chain to be statistically usable.
Poland, DACH and the rest follow the seed. The US is scenario 3 on the markets tab and is the
upside the Series A buys, not part of the base case.

### The round

**Instrument and terms?**
Answer only if asked: a convertible loan agreement, rolling close, targeted 21 Nov 2026. Terms in
the data room. Do not put this on a slide.

**Is anything committed?**
Answer with whatever is true on the day. Do not use artificial urgency; the execution guide's
standing rule is that momentum is announced only when it is real.
