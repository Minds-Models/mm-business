# Three minute pitch: script and speaker notes

**Event:** Czech Tech Pitches #2, Wednesday 30 September 2026, 17:00, online on Airmeet.
**Format:** one camera, one speaker, one account. Three minutes, in English. 1:1 investor
matchmaking afterwards. Investors named in the invite include Heartcore Capital, Rockaway and
KAYA VC.
**Deck:** `pitch-deck-2026-09-3min.pdf`, six slides. The long ten slide version stays as
`pitch-deck-2026-09-final-v2.pdf` and is what you send after a good 1:1.

Two format notes that shaped this. You are screen sharing into a platform where people watch in a
small tile, so every slide is headline plus numbers and nothing that has to be read. And you cannot
safely alt-tab in a three minute slot, so the chat is a screenshot on slide 03 and you say the URL
out loud rather than opening it.

---

## The script, timed

About 420 words at roughly 140 words per minute. Read it out loud with a timer twice before you go
live. If you land under 2:50 on the rehearsal, slow down rather than adding anything.

### Slide 01 · Cover · 0:00 to 0:20

> Hi, I am Ondrej, co-founder of Minds and Models.
>
> Online retail has known who its customers are for twenty years. Physical retail still does not.
>
> We built the first behavioral data layer for physical stores, out of the data every store already
> generates every second, and throws away.

### Slide 02 · Problem · 0:20 to 0:50

> Nine in ten customers walk into a shop, buy something, and leave no record of who they were.
>
> So consumer brands set next year's budget on three broken sources. Panels that are expensive and
> months late. Checkout data that is blind to the person. Loyalty cards that cover one chain and
> nobody else.
>
> Eighteen billion dollars a year buys what was sold. Nobody sells who bought it.

### Slide 03 · Solution · 0:50 to 1:30

> Three steps, and no panel, no survey, no recruitment.
>
> One. We read the shopper from the cameras the store already has, on the edge, and we join that to
> the receipt from the same visit. That gives us a row nobody else can produce.
>
> Two. At eighty-eight attributes per shopper, and almost nineteen million of them, the segments
> draw themselves. Nobody has to invent them.
>
> Three. The brand just asks, in plain language, and the answer comes back as a slide, a
> spreadsheet or a one-pager. This is live today at chat dot mindsmodels dot ai.

### Slide 04 · Business model · 1:30 to 2:00

> The model is what makes this move fast.
>
> The retailer never pays. They give us the cameras they already own and the receipt feed, and they
> take a revenue share.
>
> The brands pay. Forty thousand euros a year per category, rising to a hundred and seventy as the
> read covers more chains.
>
> So our cost scales with stores and our revenue scales with seats. Across the plan the footprint
> grows twenty-five times and the revenue a hundred and twenty-five.

### Slide 05 · Traction · 2:00 to 2:30

> This is not a concept. We went live in the second quarter of this year.
>
> Seven enterprise pilots. Almost nineteen million visual data points. And over half a million real
> shoppers already matched to the basket they paid for.
>
> Those are real chains, in their own stores. O2, GymBeam, CEPRO, CPI and three more.

### Slide 06 · The ask · 2:30 to 3:00

> We are raising a five hundred thousand euro pre-seed, and it is already partially committed.
>
> It buys one thing: density. A category read starts selling once about sixty stores in one chain
> are live, so what stands between us and revenue is rollout, not invention.
>
> The plan reaches twenty-one million of ARR by 2031 on under six million of total capital, in a
> category where owned data trades at eight to ten times revenue.
>
> I would love to go deeper in the one-on-ones. Thank you.

## Speaker notes, if you would rather improvise

**01 Cover.** Name, company, one sentence of category. Do not explain the company yet. The only job
is to stop them filing you under retail software.

- Online has known for twenty years. Physical has not.
- First behavioral data layer for physical stores.
- Built from data that already exists and gets thrown away.

**02 Problem.** Name the customer out loud. It is the consumer brand, not the retailer.

- Nine in ten customers leave no record.
- Three sources, one flaw each: expensive, blind, scarce.
- $18bn a year buys the what. Nobody sells the who.

**03 Solution.** Longest beat. Do not rush step one, it is the part they will not take on trust.

- Cameras the store already owns, read on the edge, joined to the receipt.
- 88 attributes, 18.8 million shoppers, segments emerge rather than being authored.
- Ask in plain words. No dashboard to learn. Live today.

**04 Business model.** The line that makes it a venture case is the last one.

- Retailer pays nothing and takes a revenue share.
- Brands pay 40k rising to 170k per category per year.
- Cost scales with stores, revenue scales with seats. 25x footprint, 125x revenue.

**05 Traction.** Say the launch quarter first, it reframes everything before it as real.

- Live since Q2 2026.
- 7 pilots, 18.8M visual data points, over half a million shoppers matched.
- Name three chains, let the logo row do the rest.

**06 Ask.** End on the ask, not on thanks. Leave the slide up.

- 500k pre-seed, partially committed.
- Buys density, which is rollout, not invention.
- 21.3M ARR by 2031 on under 6M of capital, 8 to 10 times revenue in this category.

## If you are running long

Cut in this order. Each of these is a clean cut that does not break the argument.

1. The three source names on slide 02. Say "three broken sources" and move on. Saves about 8s.
2. The 25x and 125x line on slide 04. The pricing ladder already implies it. Saves about 8s.
3. The three chain names on slide 05. The logos are on screen. Saves about 6s.
4. The multiple on slide 06. Keep the ARR and the capital. Saves about 7s.

Do not cut: the join in step one, the fact that the retailer pays nothing, the launch quarter, or
the amount you are raising.

## Likely questions, for the 1:1s

**Is this facial recognition?** No. No identities, no biometric templates, nothing identifiable
leaves the store, aggregates only, and age is estimated with error bands rather than detected. Say
this unprompted the first time cameras come up.

**What stops a big player doing this?** Not the technology, the rights. Every chain signs its own
perpetual aggregate mandate once, with its own data processing agreement, and a signature does not
go faster because you have more capital.

**Why does the retailer agree?** They pay nothing, they take a revenue share on data they never had
to collect, and they get the read back on their own shoppers, which no chain has ever had.

**Who is the competition?** NIQ, Circana and Kantar own the what-was-sold half and sell it very
well. None of them has the who half, and their route to it is the same retailer by retailer rights
conversation we are already having.

**Why Czechia first?** Retail here is consolidated, two founders can reach the chains inside a
quarter, and a category read only needs about sixty stores in a chain to be statistically usable.
Poland and DACH follow the seed.

**What is unproven?** Rights breadth. The plan assumes we sign perpetual aggregate mandates chain by
chain, and that is exactly the constraint this round exists to remove. Say it plainly, it lands
better than hedging.

## Two numbers to be careful with

Both are flagged `needs-source` in `messaging/stats.yaml` and are the most exposed claims in the
deck. If anyone asks for the as-of date in a 1:1, say you will send it rather than guessing.

- 18.8M visual data points and 524,692 matched shoppers still need one cumulative re-pull with a
  single as-of date.
- "Launched Q2 2026" is not yet pinned to a first-capture date in the database.
